What is actually stopping it?
=============================

Script: experiments/g10_03_what_is_the_ceiling.py
Status: RUN, at chunk 64 and chunk 256. Pure counting -- no model, no training,
        seconds, local.

ANSWER: most of the "underfitting" is the PER-CHUNK RESET, not a failure to
        learn. At chunk 64 the model BEATS counting inside the same window. At
        chunk 256 counting overtakes it, and the thing that overtakes it is
        superposition.


IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
The device was being compared against a counter that had read two hundred
thousand characters, while the device itself only ever remembers the last sixty
four. Losing that comparison says almost nothing about the device.

So this builds counters with the same handicap -- allowed to see only the same
short window -- and asks where the device lands against those instead.

Against a counter with its own memory limits, the device is **better**. The
enormous gap to the full counter is the price of forgetting between windows.

But when the window is made longer, the counter improves quickly and the device
barely moves. That is the real limit, and it is a specific one.


PREDICTIONS -- A DECISION RULE, WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN
------------------------------------------------------------------
No numbers were predicted -- this is counting, and guessing counts is not
informative. What was registered in advance, in the script's docstring before it
was run, is the rule for reading the answer:

    matching `bigram (chunk)`      -> the per-chunk reset is the whole story
    matching `last-occurrence`     -> superposition is costing the distribution
    below both                     -> the limit is the readout

That rule is what makes this a measurement rather than a description, and it is
recorded here as written rather than reconstructed.


THE RESULT
-----------
                       chunk 64     chunk 256    what it may know
    uniform               6.426         6.426    nothing at all
    unigram               4.756         4.756    base rates, all training text
    bigram (full)         3.711         3.711    transitions, all training text
    bigram (chunk)        5.988         5.349    transitions, THIS CHUNK only
    last-occurrence       6.221         6.113    newest successor, this chunk
    ------------------------------------------------------------------------
    THE MODEL             5.830         5.734    measured at each chunk

**At chunk 64 the model beats within-chunk counting by 0.158 bits** and beats
keeping-only-the-newest by 0.391. Given what it is allowed to remember, it is
doing better than counting, not worse.

**At chunk 256 counting overtakes it by 0.385 bits.** Between the two chunk
lengths, counting improves by **0.639** bits and the model by **0.096**.

So the two handicaps swap places as context grows:

    the per-chunk reset costs   2.277 bits at chunk 64, 1.637 at chunk 256
    superposition costs         0.232 bits at chunk 64, 0.765 at chunk 256

**Superposition is the binding limit as soon as there is enough context to
superpose.** Several past occurrences of `e` collapse into one vector, and the
model gets an average successor where counting keeps a distribution. At a short
window there is little to collapse and the model's trained readout wins; at a
longer one the collapse costs more than the readout gains.


WHAT THIS CORRECTS
-------------------
[g10-02](g10-02-underfit-or-undertrained.txt) concluded *"it is not learning the
text"*. That was measured against `bigram (full)`, which sees 210,000 characters
of context where the model sees 64. **It is misleading and it is corrected
there**: the model is learning, up to the limit of what a per-chunk store can
know, and at chunk 64 it exceeds counting within that limit.

What survives from g10-02 unchanged: epochs are not the constraint, and width is
not the constraint. Both were measured and both still hold.


WHAT TO BUILD, IN ORDER -- **THIS ORDERING WAS WRONG, SEE BELOW**
-------------------------------------------------------------------
1. **A store that persists across chunks.** Worth up to 1.6-2.3 bits, which is
   the single largest number on this page. It is also what BACKLOG's replay item
   is about, arriving from a completely different direction.
2. **Something that keeps a distribution rather than an average.** Worth 0.77
   bits at chunk 256 and growing with context. This is what a bounded set of
   slots does -- and the g9 line has already built one.

Neither of these is a tuning change and neither is about node size.

**CORRECTED BY [g10-04](g10-04-does-context-help-this-model.txt): THE ORDER IS
THE OTHER WAY ROUND.**

The 1.6-2.3 bits above is what a COUNTER gains from more context. It is not what
this mechanism gains. Handed chunks of 64, 128, 256 and 512, the model's own
curve is nearly flat -- 5.927, 5.804, 5.856, 5.687 -- while counting improves
from 5.988 to 4.976. **It captures 24% of what the extra context is worth, and
the gap widens at every step.**

Chunk length IS the persistence horizon, so a store that survived between chunks
would hand this mechanism more of exactly what it already fails to use.

**The error is one this project keeps catching in other forms: a number measured
on one thing, applied to another.** The gap was real and the recommendation drawn
from it was not, because nothing checked whether the mechanism could close it.
The check cost minutes and should have come before the recommendation rather
than one cycle after it.

Superposition goes first.


COST
-----
Seconds, local, no runner. Counting only.


A NUMBER THAT WAS NEARLY WRONG
--------------------------------
The first version carried ONE model number, 5.83, measured at chunk 64, and
applied it at chunk 256 as well -- where the true value is 5.734 and the verdict
flips from "beats counting" to "loses to counting". The comment above it said
where it came from, and that did not help: a caveat next to a number does not
attach to the number when the number is used elsewhere.

It is now a table keyed by chunk, and the script REFUSES a chunk it has no
matching measurement for rather than reaching for the nearest one.
