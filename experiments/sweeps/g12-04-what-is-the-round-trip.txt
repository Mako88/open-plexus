What is the round trip, and what does that make d_max?
======================================================

Script: testbed/run.py, real containers, `tc netem` on the link
Status: RUN, 6 of 6 cells, run 30399620805. Every cell agreed with the
        single-process model, so the timings are interpretable.

ANSWER: **d_max is about 640 ms on the worst link tested**, and this project
        finally has a waiting parameter in a unit that can be checked against a
        network. THREE OF SIX PREDICTIONS REFUTED, including one that corrects a
        figure published in decision 127.


RESULTS
-------
160 votes per cell, 4 nodes, width 16, 40 steps, window 4. Milliseconds.

  cell                              mean      p50      p99      max   3 x p99
  clean                             0.75     0.61     2.54     2.64       7.6
  loss 2%                           0.82     0.76     2.32     2.65       7.0
  delay 20ms                       20.55    20.45    21.10    21.15      63.3
  delay 80ms                       80.55    80.51    81.53    81.58     244.6
  delay 80ms jitter 20ms           82.74    84.40   100.38   100.42     301.1
  delay 80ms jitter 20ms loss 2%   95.25    87.22   211.88   265.20     635.6


SCORING
-------
  P1  REFUTED, and the statistic was the wrong one. p99 exceeds the mean by more
      than 2x on the clean link (3.4x) and under loss alone (2.8x) -- and by only
      1.01x at delay 80ms. **Once a fixed delay dominates, the mean and the p99
      converge**, because a constant added to every sample moves both equally.

      The ratio measures how much VARIANCE there is relative to the total, and a
      timeout does not care about that. What it has to cover is the absolute gap
      p99 - p50, which runs 1.0 ms at delay 80, 16.0 ms with jitter added, and
      124.7 ms with loss on top. **Quote the gap, not the ratio.**

  P2  REFUTED, and it corrects decision 127. The clean-link p99 is **2.54 ms**,
      not "within 2x of the in-process 24.19 ms" -- it is TEN TIMES SMALLER.

      **The in-process figure was measuring this development machine's process
      scheduler, not the model.** Four Python processes on Windows, 120 votes;
      the containers give 160 votes at a fortieth of the tail. The claim that
      "the tail runs 64x the median on the easiest link available" is withdrawn:
      the easiest link is 4x, and the 64x was Windows.

      Decision 127 and the g12-04 predictions both quoted it. Recorded rather
      than quietly dropped, because it was the number used to argue that
      percentiles were worth building -- and they were, for a different reason.

  P3  CONFIRMED, almost exactly. 80 ms of delay moves the median from 0.61 ms to
      80.51 ms, **+79.9 ms**. The link's contribution is additive and lands in
      the middle of the distribution.

  P4  CONFIRMED, decisively. The p50-to-p99 gap goes from **1.0 ms** at delay 80
      to **16.0 ms** once 20 ms of jitter is added -- sixteen times wider, from
      an impairment a fourth the size of the delay. Variance lands in the tail.

  P5  REFUTED. 2% loss ALONE is invisible: p50 +0.15 ms and p99 **-0.22 ms**
      against the clean link, which is noise.

      **But loss is not additive, it is multiplicative with delay.** Adding the
      same 2% to the delay-80-jitter-20 link takes p99 from 100.4 ms to
      **211.9 ms** and the max to 265 ms. A retransmit costs a round trip, so
      the cost of losing a packet is proportional to how long a round trip
      takes -- on a fast link that is nothing, and on a slow one it doubles the
      tail. The prediction was right about the mechanism and wrong to test it in
      isolation.

  P6  CONFIRMED, and it is the number the sweep exists for. `T' >= 3 x p99`
      reaches **635.6 ms** on the worst link.


WHAT THIS GIVES THE PROJECT
----------------------------
**d_max = ~640 ms**, measured, on an 80 ms link with 20 ms jitter and 2% loss.
That is simultaneously the C2 asynchrony bound and the C3 churn timeout, and it
is the first time either has been a number rather than a count of steps.

`RETRY_AFTER_STEPS = 8` should become a duration derived from this. **A step has
no fixed duration** -- at 40 steps in 0.014 s on the clean link, eight steps is
under 3 ms; on the worst link the same eight steps span several seconds. The same
constant means two things three orders of magnitude apart.

**It is a FLOOR from these links, not a universal constant.** A worse link raises
it and nothing here tested one; intercontinental paths, mobile networks and
congested uplinks are all outside this grid.


TWO DEFECTS THIS SWEEP FOUND IN ITS OWN PLUMBING
-------------------------------------------------
Both cost the aggregation and neither touched the data.

1. **`testbed/run.py` printed progress to STDOUT**, and the workflow piped
   stdout into a `.json` file. All six artifacts had two lines of prose above
   the JSON object and could not be parsed. A program whose stdout is a data
   format has no business printing anything else there -- progress now goes to
   stderr.

2. **`tools/summarise_g12_04.py` shipped with a SYNTAX ERROR** and every local
   check passed, because **nothing imports a summariser at check time**: the
   suite does not touch them, the mutation harness does not target them, and the
   rails read them only as text. It surfaced in CI, after the matrix had run, in
   the aggregation step.

   A summariser is the one piece of code that runs exactly once, at the end,
   when the expensive thing has already happened -- the worst possible place for
   an error a parser catches in milliseconds. **R5 in `check_rails.py` now
   compiles every file under `tools/` and `experiments/`**, and is verified to
   bite by breaking one deliberately.


IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
Everywhere this system waits for another machine, it waits a number of STEPS. A
step is however long a step happens to take, so "wait 8 steps" is not a length of
time and cannot be checked against a network.

This measures how long an answer actually takes to come back, over links with
stated amounts of delay, jitter and packet loss. The point is to replace a
guessed number of steps with a measured number of milliseconds.


WHY THIS EXISTS
----------------
Note 003 unified `d_max` as the C2 asynchrony bound and the C3 churn timeout --
"two constraints, one parameter" -- and **it has never been measured.** C2's own
wording is that a design "must state a bound it tolerates", and a bound in steps
is not a bound.

Decision 127 read the SWIM paper and found the same quantity doing the same job
there, except measured: the indirect-probe timeout comes from an estimate of the
round-trip distribution -- the mean or the 99th percentile -- and the protocol
period must be **at least three times** the round-trip estimate. Our equivalent,
`RETRY_AFTER_STEPS = 8`, is a guess in the wrong unit.

**A mean cannot size a timeout.** Measured in-process on loopback with no
impairment at all, before this sweep:

    p50 0.38 ms      p99 24.19 ms      max 24.21 ms

The tail runs **64x the median** on the easiest link available. A timeout set
from the mean would expire on a large share of perfectly healthy votes, which is
exactly the false-positive problem SWIM's suspicion mechanism exists to contain
-- and which this project reproduced independently by ejecting a node on its
first missed send (decision 126).


WHAT IS MEASURED
-----------------
`Network.vote_latencies` records, for every vote, the seconds from the step being
DISPATCHED to that vote arriving. Recorded before the vote is counted, so a step
that settles short does not lose its late arrivals -- those are precisely the
samples that say how long a deadline should have been.

**It is not a pure network round trip.** It includes the node's compute for that
token, so it is an upper bound on link latency and the right quantity for a
timeout that has to cover a real reply. SWIM's ping/ack measures the link alone;
this measures the wait, and the wait is what expires.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
  P1  p99 exceeds the mean by more than 2x at every impairment level, including
      the clean one. If it does not, a mean would do and the percentile
      machinery is unnecessary.

  P2  The clean-link p99 is dominated by COMPUTE, not the link: it lands within
      2x of the in-process 24 ms rather than near zero.

  P3  Adding 80 ms of delay moves the MEDIAN by roughly 80 ms -- the link's
      contribution is additive and shows up in the middle of the distribution.

  P4  Adding jitter widens the gap between p50 and p99 by more than the delay
      alone does. Jitter is variance and the tail is where variance lands.

  P5  2% loss moves p99 far more than it moves p50, because a lost packet costs
      a whole retransmit rather than a small delay.

  P6  `T' >= 3 x p99` lands in the hundreds of milliseconds on the impaired
      links -- so a workable `d_max` is a fraction of a second, not the tens of
      milliseconds a naive reading of "80 ms link" would suggest.

P1 is the rail: it says whether reporting percentiles was worth building. P6 is
the one that produces a number the project can use.


COST
----
6 cells, one per impairment configuration, on the testbed workflow that already
exists. Each is 4 nodes at width 16 for 40 steps -- the same shape g12-01 through
g12-03 ran, whose wall-clock was minutes.

Estimated from the MOST EXPENSIVE cell, which is the one with both the largest
delay and loss: 40 steps x (80 ms + retransmits) is dominated by the link rather
than by compute, so ~10 s of network time per cell plus container start-up.


MEASURED ON
-----------
testbed/run.py, 4 nodes, width 16, 40 steps, window 4. Window above 1 on purpose:
at window 1 the driver waits for every vote before sending the next token, so
every round trip is serialised and the distribution measures lock-step rather
than the link.

Impairment is a SWEPT PARAMETER, not ambient noise -- a test running under random
loss is a flaky test, and one running at a stated loss rate is a measurement.
