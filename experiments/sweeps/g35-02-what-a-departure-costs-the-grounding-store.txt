What does a departure cost, when nothing is replicated?
=======================================================

Script: experiments/g35_02_what_a_departure_costs_the_grounding_store.py
Status: RUN, local, 2026-07-31, 310.5s. Predictions committed at `f2e1bd3`
        before the script was executed.

ANSWER: **all five predictions held, and the answer reverses the alarming
        reading of the fourth.** More modalities means more concepts are TOUCHED
        by a departure — 0.198, 0.260, 0.479 at one node of eight — and almost
        none are LOST: `connected` goes 0.8901, 0.9923, **1.0000** over the same
        range. At HALF the network gone it is 0.5522, 0.8160, **0.9596**.

        **So multimodality buys fault tolerance rather than costing it**, and
        `GOALS.md`'s multimodal requirement and C3 reinforce each other instead
        of pulling apart. A surviving surface needs only ONE partner left, and
        five surfaces give it four chances where two give it one.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
This project's premise is that machines leaving is **normal**, not an accident.
So the question is not whether the system survives a departure but what one
costs.

The older store kept three copies of everything, so losing a machine mostly cost
redundancy. The grounding store keeps **one**. When a machine goes, everything it
knew about its share of things is gone for good, and nothing anywhere has a copy.

There is a second cost that is easy to miss. To judge whether two things belong
together, a machine has to ask how common the *other* thing is — and if the
machine holding that answer has left, nobody can score it. So a departure damages
things it never held, by making them unjudgeable.

That means a concept is in trouble if **any** of its appearances lived on the
departed machine. A dog known by a picture, a sound and a word has three chances
to be hit rather than one.

**Which raises the uncomfortable question this run is really about.** The whole
point of multiple modalities is that a concept can be reached several ways. Does
that make it more robust to a machine leaving, or less? More ways to reach it, or
more ways to lose part of it? The answer is not obvious and it matters, because
multimodality is a requirement here rather than a feature.


WHAT IS BEING MEASURED
----------------------
    ring share      the departed nodes' share of the network, `lost / nodes`
    surfaces gone   share of surfaces whose owner has left
    concepts hit    share of concepts with AT LEAST ONE surface gone
    f1              recovery, scoring a lost surface as **zero** rather than
                    skipping it. Skipping would average over the survivors,
                    which is a different question and a flattering one
    largest         biggest recovered class, to catch a collapse

An unreachable candidate is **dropped** from a ranking rather than scored zero. A
zero marginal is an ordinary count and would make a departed peer look like a
surface nobody ever saw; dropping is local, graceful, and the only option that
gives a smaller answer rather than a wrong one.


MEASURED ON
-----------
Local, single process, Python 3.14, no numpy. `Federation`, so ownership and the
crossings are real; no sockets, because this is a question about what
INFORMATION is lost and transport cannot change that.

    concepts       32
    nodes           8      carried from g33-03
    occasions   4,000      125 per concept, above g32-02's ~16 threshold

Swept: `surfaces` per concept in {2, 3, 5}, nodes lost in {0, 1, 2, 4}.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
P1  CONTROL, near-arithmetic. `surfaces gone` tracks `ring share` to within
    about 0.06 — consistent hashing spreads keys evenly but a ring is lumpy, and
    `Ring.balance` already exists because of it.
    REFUTED IF the two diverge widely, which would mean ownership is not
    spreading and every other row is about a different arrangement.

P2  A DEPARTURE COSTS MORE THAN ITS SHARE. `concepts hit` is far above
    `surfaces gone`, because a concept is damaged if ANY of its surfaces is
    lost: about `1 - (1 - share)^surfaces`. At 3 surfaces and one node of eight
    that is roughly **0.33 against 0.125**.
    REFUTED IF the two are close, which would mean surfaces of one concept land
    on the same node — and that would be worth knowing, because it would make
    the ring's placement correlated in a way nothing has checked.

P3  IT DEGRADES RATHER THAN COLLAPSING. `f1` falls roughly in step with
    `concepts hit`, and `largest` stays small at every loss level.
    REFUTED IF `largest` rises or `f1` falls off a cliff — either would mean
    dropping unreachable candidates destabilises the walk rather than shrinking
    its answer, and the graceful-degradation claim would be wrong.

P5  REGISTERED AFTER THE FIRST RUN AND BEFORE THE SECOND, which is stated so it
    is not counted as a prediction made in advance of everything. The first run
    could not resolve P4's consequence because `f1`'s floor moves with the class
    size. `connected` — the share of SURVIVING surfaces whose recovered class
    still holds at least one true partner — has no size-dependent floor.

    **`connected` RISES with the surface count at a fixed loss.** A surviving
    surface needs only ONE partner to remain reachable, and at five surfaces it
    has four chances where at two it has one: roughly `1 - gone^(surfaces-1)`,
    so about 0.90 at two surfaces and near 1.00 at five with one node of eight
    gone.
    REFUTED IF `connected` is flat or falls with surfaces. **That would settle
    P4's alarming reading**: more modalities would be pure exposure with no
    compensating redundancy, and replication would be a prerequisite for the
    multimodal goal rather than an improvement to it.

    If it holds, the two metrics say different true things — more concepts are
    TOUCHED and fewer concepts are LOST — and the alarming reading of P4 is
    simply the wrong quantity.

P4  MORE MODALITIES MAKES CHURN WORSE, NOT BETTER. At a fixed loss, `concepts
    hit` RISES with `surfaces` — 2, 3, then 5 — because more surfaces is more
    chances one of them was on the departed node. At one node of eight:
    roughly 0.23, 0.33, 0.49.
    REFUTED IF more surfaces helps. **That refutation would be the good news
    and it is worth wanting**: it would mean the redundancy intuition holds and
    multimodality buys fault tolerance for free. If it does not, then
    `GOALS.md`'s multimodal requirement and C3 pull against each other, and
    replication stops being an optimisation and becomes a prerequisite.

THE READING P4 INVITED WAS WRONG, AND A FLOOR-FREE METRIC SETTLED IT
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The obvious next sentence after P4 is *"so multimodality and C3 pull against each
other"*. **It is wrong**, and the first run could not say so because of a defect
in its own design.

Look at `f1` across surface counts at a fixed loss:

    one node lost      2 surfaces 0.8639     3 surfaces 0.8708     5 surfaces 0.8534
    four nodes lost    2 surfaces 0.3958     3 surfaces 0.4135     5 surfaces 0.3701

Flat. Which would say the extra exposure costs nothing — more concepts are
touched, but each keeps more surviving surfaces, and the two effects cancel.

**That comparison is not admissible, because the metric's floor moves with the
surface count.** A concept recovered entirely alone scores:

    2 surfaces   0.6667
    3 surfaces   0.5000
    5 surfaces   0.3333

and a surface whose owner departed is scored **0.0**, below all of them. So the
same `f1` means *less* degradation at 5 surfaces than at 2, and the flat row is
not evidence of equality — it is three different scales printed in one column.

**This is CLAUDE.md's own standard, applied to me a second time today.** The rule
is to compute what a metric returns for the degenerate case before reading a
number off it; `g32-01`'s 0.5 floor was that lesson and the floor was fixed
there, so nothing prompted a re-check when `surfaces` became an axis.

**So a floor-free quantity was added and the grid re-run.** `connected` asks a
yes-or-no question of each surviving surface — *did you end up with any of your
own?* — which means the same thing at every class size.

**It rises with the surface count, steeply.** At half the network gone, two
surfaces leaves 0.5522 of survivors still connected and five leaves **0.9596**.
The reason is simple once stated: a surface needs only ONE partner to remain
reachable, and five surfaces give it four chances where two give it one.

**So the two metrics say different true things.** More concepts are TOUCHED by a
departure and far fewer are LOST — and `concepts hit`, which counts a concept as
damaged if a single surface went, was the wrong quantity to worry about. The
redundancy intuition holds after all, for a reason the arithmetic predicts.


WHAT THIS DOES SETTLE
----------------------
**A departure is amplified by the modality count and the exponent is measured**:
at 5 surfaces, losing one node of eight TOUCHES **48%** of concepts.

**And almost none of them are lost.** `connected` at that cell is **1.0000**, so
every touched concept still reaches at least one of its own surfaces.

**MULTIMODALITY IS THE REDUNDANCY.** With nothing replicated, the surfaces
themselves are the copies — which answers, for this store, the question
`partitioned.ConceptStore.lose` leaves open in its own docstring: *"which is
preferable is a measurement, not a preference."*

**Nothing collapses at any loss level**, including half the network gone.

**Replication is therefore an improvement rather than a prerequisite**, which is
a weaker claim than this file made before the second run and is the right one.
The two-surface row is where it would pay: **0.5522** connected at half the
network gone, against **0.9596** at five surfaces. The repair is the anti-entropy
`ConceptStore.lose` already describes, and it is a ranked option rather than an
urgent gap.

**What is still not measured**: a concept's surfaces are placed independently by
the ring here. Replication would place copies deliberately, and whether that
beats simply having more modalities is a comparison nobody has run.
