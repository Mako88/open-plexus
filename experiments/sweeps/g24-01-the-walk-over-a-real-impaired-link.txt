The walk over a real impaired link, at last
============================================

Script: testbed/run.py --mode peer, driving tools/peer_walk_timing.py
Status: COMPLETE. Run 2026-07-30, in containers, locally, Docker 29.6.1.

ANSWER: THE WALK MISSES `d_max` AT DEPTH 2, NOT DEPTH 8. Note 101's arithmetic
priced a round at an assumed 50 ms; measured on an 80 ms link it is **161 ms**,
and with jitter and loss it grows with depth rather than staying constant.


IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
To answer a question the system walks a chain across machines. Each step needs
two messages that have to happen one after the other, so a longer chain costs
more time, and there is a budget: 640 ms, after which a machine is treated as
gone rather than slow.

Every estimate of how deep a walk can go has assumed each step costs 50 ms,
because nobody had run it over a real slow link -- only on the local machine,
where a message takes a twentieth of a millisecond.

This is that run. On a link with the delay of an intercontinental connection, a
step costs **161 ms**, not 50. So the walk runs out of budget at **two steps**
instead of eight.


WHY THIS EXISTS
----------------
`note 094`: *"`tools/cluster_compose.py` can put peers in containers with
`tc netem`, and has not been pointed at this."*

`note 101`: *"the `tc netem` container harness has still never been pointed at
the peer path."*

Two notes, independently, naming the same gap. `DECISIONS.md` carries the peer
transport as *"measured in: loopback only, priced at an assumed 50 ms RTT."*


THE PREDICTION WAS ALREADY COMMITTED, AND IT IS NOTE 101'S TABLE
------------------------------------------------------------------
This run did not register a fresh prediction, and it did not need to: note 101
committed one, dated, months of commits ago:

    depth   reads   rounds   at 50 ms RTT   against d_max 640 ms
        3      21        6          300 ms   fits
        5      37       10          500 ms   fits, 78%
        8      61       16          800 ms   OVER
       10      77       20        1,000 ms   OVER by 1.6x

**That is the prediction under test.** It is refuted on the number and confirmed
on the structure -- rounds really are `2 * depth`, at every depth, in every run.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
**Not written for this run, and deliberately so: they are note 101's, committed
long before it.** The rail wants predictions registered ahead of the measurement
and these were, by months. Scored here rather than restated.

  P1  A walk costs `2 * depth` sequential rounds.
      CONFIRMED, in every row of every run, impaired and not. The structure note
      101 derived is exactly right.

  P2  At 50 ms per round, depth 3 fits (300 ms) and depth 5 fits at 78% of
      budget (500 ms).
      REFUTED. A round costs 161 ms. Depth 3 costs 968 ms and depth 5 costs
      1,614 ms -- two and a half times the budget note 101 says it fits inside.

  P3  The breaking point is depth 8.
      REFUTED. It is depth 2.

  P4  Cost is `rounds x RTT`, i.e. LINEAR in depth.
      REFUTED under loss, CONFIRMED without it. Clean, `ms/round` is flat to two
      decimals across a fivefold change in depth. With 2% loss it grows by 84%
      from depth 1 to depth 3. **This is the prediction nobody knew they were
      making** -- linearity is assumed by `tools/walk_rounds.py` and by every
      latency argument in the project, and it holds only on a lossless link.


THE SETUP, AND ONE CHOICE THAT MATTERS
----------------------------------------
Four peers plus an asker, each its own container on a Docker bridge, running
`openplexus.node_main` in peer mode. The asker runs `tools/peer_walk_timing.py`
inside the network.

**`tc netem` is applied to the ASKER as well as the peers.** `netem delay` shapes
egress, so impairing only the peers would leave the request leg free and the
reply leg slow -- half a link. Both sides get it, so `--delay 80ms` is an 80 ms
one-way link and about **160 ms of round trip**, which is the intercontinental
figure `GOALS.md` names.

Coverage was asserted from the peers' own logs, not assumed: 10 + 14 + 10 + 14 =
48 = 24 facts x 2 replicas.


THE RESULT
----------
Clean 80 ms delay, no jitter, no loss:

     depth   rounds  expected     wall ms   ms/round    vs d_max 640
         1        2         2       322.3     161.15    fits
         2        4         4       645.3     161.33    OVER
         3        6         6       967.8     161.30    OVER
         5       10        10      1614.4     161.44    OVER

80 ms delay + 20 ms jitter + 2% loss -- the impairment `128` measured the
transport on:

     depth   rounds  expected     wall ms   ms/round    vs d_max 640
         1        2         2       328.5     164.25    fits
         2        4         4      1083.3     270.83    OVER
         3        6         6      1814.7     302.45    OVER

Unimpaired, on the same bridge, for the identity check: 0.40 to 1.21 ms/round.
Rounds equal `2 * depth` in **every** row of every run, which is the guard that
the walk actually traversed rather than short-circuiting.


TWO FINDINGS, AND THE SECOND IS NOT IN ANY MODEL HERE
--------------------------------------------------------
**1. The assumed RTT was 3.2x optimistic.** 50 ms assumed against 161 ms
measured. The breaking point moves from depth 8 to **depth 2**, and depth 5 --
which note 101 records as fitting at 78% of budget -- costs **1,614 ms**, two and
a half times the budget.

This is the same shape as `CLAUDE.md`'s runner-time calibration, where a 3x
allowance for a slower machine turned out to be 7.6x. An assumption that makes
the thing look affordable.

**2. WITH LOSS, COST IS SUPERLINEAR IN DEPTH.** Clean, `ms/round` is flat to two
decimals -- 161.15, 161.33, 161.30, 161.44. With 2% loss it *grows*: 164, 271,
302.

Every model in this project treats a walk as `rounds x RTT`, which is linear.
It is not, once packets are dropped: each additional round is another chance to
hit a retransmit, and a TCP retransmit costs a timeout rather than a round trip.
**So depth is more expensive than the arithmetic says exactly when the network is
worst**, which is the opposite of the direction anyone would want to be wrong in.

Nothing in `tools/walk_rounds.py` or note 101 models this. It is a new term.


WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE
---------------------------
**`d_max` itself is a floor.** Decision 128 derived 640 ms as 3x a measured p99 on
six SIMULATED links. A real WAN raises it, which would move the bar in the
walk's favour -- and nobody has measured that either. The ratio is what is solid
here: a round costs about one RTT, and the walk needs `2 * depth` of them.

**One machine, one bridge, four peers.** `tc netem` on a local Docker bridge is a
far better model than loopback and is not a real internet path: no route changes,
no competing traffic, no asymmetry, no NAT.

**The migrating walk is the untested answer.** `note 102` prices a rendezvous at
0.089 with its period unmeasurable, and it is the mechanism that would take a
walk from `2 * depth` rounds to something less. It remains unbuilt, and this run
raises what it has to deliver from a factor of two to a factor of about eight.


COST
----
About four minutes per configuration including the image build, locally. No
dispatch, no runner time.

-----------
Docker 29.6.1, `testbed/Dockerfile`, four peer containers plus an asker on a
