Does the forward score keep the link and refuse the distractor?
================================================================

Script: experiments/g39_04_does_the_forward_score_refuse_the_distractor.py
Status: RUN, local, 2026-07-31, 12.6s. Predictions committed at `9d997c4`
        before the script was executed.

ANSWER: **YES, AND THE ANSWER IS A SIMPLIFICATION RATHER THAN A MECHANISM.**
        8 of 24 settings meet the bar against `g39-03`'s **0 of 24**, and the
        eight are exactly the eight where `alpha = 1.0` — both arms, both stream
        lengths, both depths. At 24,000 occasions, `together`: link **0.9867**,
        coverage **1.0000**, distractor **0.0000**.

        `forward` at `alpha = 1.0` is plain `conditional` read from the query's
        own side. **So the working rule is: keep every edge, drop the cut, drop
        mutuality, drop symmetrisation — rank and read.** Against
        `equivalence_classes`'s 0.9216 at the same length.

        Four of five predictions refuted, including P2 in the better direction:
        depth 2 was predicted to readmit the distractor and it does not, in any
        cell.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
One failure has survived everything tried against it this week. Something present
in every single moment -- a hum in the room, the word "the" -- gets treated as
meaningful, because it turns up alongside everything.

Five things were tried. None worked, and the last run explained why: **we were
looking on the wrong axis entirely.** The thing that used to keep the hum out was
never the scoring rule. It was a hard cut that threw away weak connections -- and
throwing away connections is precisely what we removed, on purpose, because it
was also throwing away the word.

But the arithmetic pointed somewhere the grid never went.

Every connection has two ends, and they disagree. From the word's side, its own
pictures are its strongest partners and the hum is much weaker. From the hum's
side, everything looks maximally connected -- because the hum genuinely is
present with everything, always. That is not an error; it is true.

Everything tried so far **averaged the two ends together** in one way or another.
And averaging is what lets the hum's true-but-useless "I am always here" leak in
and outrank a real partner.

So the fix might be to stop averaging. Ask only from the asking side.

A quick check suggested that works: the link stays high and the hum is refused
completely, for the first time. **But that was one setting.** This asks whether
it survives the axes that killed everything else -- both arrangements of the
senses, three settings of the correction dial, one hop and two.

The place it is most likely to break is named in advance. Discarding half of
every connection is exactly what this project's own notes warn against, because a
one-sided rule normally lets an always-present thing attach itself to everything.
The reason it may be safe here is that the question always starts from the word,
so the hum never gets to nominate itself -- and that reason stops applying the
moment the search takes a second step.


WHAT IS BEING MEASURED
----------------------
    link         precision over the top-`k` image codes a word reaches
    covered      how many of the codes it SHOULD find it had a chance to score
    distractor   share of words admitting the ever-present distractor
    BOTH?        link >= 0.80 AND covered >= 0.80 AND distractor <= 0.05

Identical columns and identical thresholds to `g39-03`, so the two tables can be
read side by side. That run's answer was **0 of 24**.

---------------------------------
A scratch probe measured `forward` at link 0.9800, coverage 1.0000, distractor
0.0000 before these predictions were written -- **at one arm, one alpha, one
depth, one stream length.** So P1 is a summary for that cell and a commitment
nowhere else. P2 through P5 are genuine: nothing has measured `forward` at any
other setting.

`CLAUDE.md` requires this said out loud, because a prediction written after a
control has run is not evidence that the mechanism was understood in advance.

P4  COVERAGE IS THE COST, IF THERE IS ONE. Where a cell fails, it fails on

THE ARITHMETIC EXPLAINS EVERY ROW, WHICH IS WHY IT IS BELIEVABLE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
`damped(alpha)` scores `c_xy / c_y**alpha`, read forward from the word `w`. For
its own image code `c` (about 60 occasions) against the distractor `d` (3,000):

    alpha = 1.0    own code  60/60    = 1.00     distractor  845/3000  = 0.28
    alpha = 0.5    own code  60/7.75  = 7.75     distractor  845/54.8  = 15.4

**At full damping the word's own codes win; at half damping the distractor wins**,
because a square root under-corrects for a surface fifty times more common. That
is the whole of the alpha column, and it was predictable from the definition.


SCORECARD
---------
  P1  **REFUTED.** Not every depth-1 cell works — only the `alpha = 1.0` ones.
            At alpha 0.5 the distractor is admitted at **1.0000** in both arms,
            and at 0.75 at **0.1667** to **0.5333**.

            The probe that made this prediction weak had run at alpha 1.0, so it
            had measured the one value that works and generalised from it. **The
            marking was right and the prediction was still wrong**, which is the
            useful reminder: labelling a prediction weak does not make it safer,
            it makes the failure interpretable.

  P2  **REFUTED, IN THE BETTER DIRECTION — the outcome this file called the
            stronger result.** Depth 2 was predicted to readmit the distractor,
            because the second hop starts from an image code rather than the
            word, and from a code's side an ever-present surface is a strong
            partner.

            `distractor` is **0.0000 in every depth-2 cell**, at every alpha and
            both arms. The one-sided rule is safe at two hops, not merely safe
            where the query happens to start.

            **But depth 2 destroys the LINK below alpha 1.0** — 0.0200, 0.1867,
            0.0267, 0.0800 — because an under-corrected second hop drags in
            everything. At alpha 1.0, depth 2 reproduces depth 1 to four
            decimals in all four cells, so the second hop adds exactly nothing,
            which is `g38-03`'s finding arriving again by another route.

  P3  **REFUTED, and it is the deciding variable rather than a minor one.**
            `link` ranges **0.0200 to 1.0000** across alpha at fixed arm and
            depth. `g39-03` found alpha moving coverage rather than precision
            under the symmetric combiners; under `forward` it decides
            everything.

  P4  **REFUTED.** Failures are not confined to coverage. The alpha-0.5 depth-1
            cells fail on `distractor` at 1.0000 while `link` is 0.9912, and
            every depth-2 cell below alpha 1.0 fails on `link` at 0.02 to 0.19.
            All three columns are load-bearing and no single one predicts the
            verdict.

  P5  **HELD.** `alternating` meets the bar at alpha 1.0 at both lengths —
            0.9395 and 0.9800 — so this is not a fix for the simultaneous case

            one, and a fix that missed it would have been close to useless.


WHAT THIS CHANGES
-----------------
**The week's open problem is closed, and by removing things rather than adding
them.** The working rule is: score `conditional` from the query's own side, keep
every edge, take the strongest, walk. No cut, no mutuality, no symmetrisation.

Against the incumbent at the same length and arm: **0.9867 with the distractor
refused, against 0.9216**. The incumbent's refusal came from the cut, and the cut
cost it the word; this refuses without the cut.

**And `strength`'s original justification is fully retired.** It was introduced
as soft mutuality, on the argument that an edge should be as strong as its weaker
direction. That argument was recorded with a doubt attached, the doubt was right
about `min`, and the answer turned out to lie off the min-to-max axis entirely.
`SYMMETRIC` now names the four that combine and `forward` is documented as
deliberately not one of them.

**The one-sided-rule objection is answered by measurement rather than argument.**
`grounding.py`'s header warns that a one-sided rule lets a hub attach itself to
everything. P2 tested exactly that at two hops and it did not happen — because
the objection is about the DISTRACTOR'S list, and a query that starts at the word
never reads it.


WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE
-------------------------
**Depth 3 and beyond.** P2 is clean at two hops and the objection it answers gets
stronger with distance. `g38-03` found depth 3 worse and dearer under `mean`, and
`forward` has not been run there.

**One code count, one beam.** 50 and 16.

**`equivalence_classes` is not retired.** It stays as the measured alternative per
rule 14c, and every earlier result scored through it remains reproducible.

**Nothing is distributed.** `forward` costs ONE remote read per candidate — the
same as `conditional` and half of what a symmetric combiner would need if the
pair count were not already symmetric. That is arithmetic from where each
quantity lives, not an observation on a socket.
