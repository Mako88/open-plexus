WHAT THE CEILING IS AND IS NOT
-------------------------------
It is the RETRIEVAL CHAIN under the assumption search does its job -- steps 1
and 3 held at their out-degree-1 value, because putting them into that regime is
precisely what search is for. **If search does not reach that regime, this
ceiling is not reached either.** It is an upper bound conditional on the
mechanism working, not a prediction of what the mechanism will score.

Composition on top of clean retrievals is a separate factor and is NOT measured
here. Decision 102 measured it at 1.000 over the whole rule table -- "sixteen
rules in a wide space are linearly separable whatever the labels do" -- so it is
not expected to bind, but that was measured with `concat` on a different
configuration and it is an assumption until re-run.

**The asymmetry is the real finding.** Step 2's ambiguity is 5.1% of sequences;
step 1's out-degree >= 2 was 50%. The middle step is reliable because a
(subject, relation) pair names one person almost always, where (FACT, subject)
names one of several relations half the time. That is why the traversal's weak
steps are its ends and not its middle, and it is what makes a verifier built from
step 2 trustworthy.


IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
To answer "how is Alice related to Carol", the model has to do three lookups:
find Alice's relation, follow it to the person in the middle, then find that
person's relation. The first and third are unreliable when someone appears in
several facts -- there are two answers and nothing says which.

The middle step is different. It asks "Alice, parent-of, who?" and that pair
names exactly one person almost every time.

This measures the middle step. It matters because the fix for the other two --
try a possibility and check where it lands -- is BUILT OUT OF the middle step.
If the middle step is reliable, checking is reliable, and the fix works. If it
is not, the checker is as unreliable as the thing it checks and there is no
point building it.


WHY THIS EXISTS
----------------
Decision 107 declined to build the traversal and derived the whole case from
three measured steps:

    step                      chained   given a perfect input
    1  key(FACT,S) -> R1        0.710                   0.710
    2  key(S,R1)   -> M         0.703                   0.960
    3  key(FACT,M) -> R2        0.497                   0.677

    "take the two entity-lookup steps to the 0.95 that step 2 already reaches
     and the product is 0.87"

**That 0.87 is why traversal-plus-search is worth building at all, and the 0.960
it rests on came from an inline probe that left no script behind** -- the same
gap that left decisions 99 through 119 with no committed instrument until
g13-01.

Decision 111 refused search on one ground: "you cannot search your way out of
noisy primitives, because the verifier is built from the primitives." g13-01
measured the primitive at 1.000 +/-0.000 at out-degree 1, so the refusal has
expired. **Every follow-and-check operation search performs IS step 2**, which
makes step 2's accuracy simultaneously the traversal's cost and the verifier's
reliability.


WHAT IS MEASURED
-----------------
`generate_object_question` asks `S R -> ?`. A fact is laid out `FACT S R O` and
the store binds the previous position's key to the current position's value, so
the write at `O` binds `key(S, R)`; the question ends `... S R` to read exactly
that binding.

No retrieval probe, same as g13-01 and for the same reason: reimplementing one
is the mistake `run()`'s own docstring records, where the 150/300 cap values came
from a reimplementation whose store never bound.

Split by how many stated facts share the asked `(S, R)`. `generate` rejects a
repeated `(subject, object)` but nothing stops a subject holding one relation to
two people, and that ambiguity is left in and counted rather than engineered
away.

Two mutations were added with the generator and both are caught:
`the-object-question-ends-on-the-wrong-pair` and
`the-object-question-follows-a-distractor`. `kinship.py` carried NO mutation
before this.
