Does the contrastive OBJECTIVE beat plain counting, on graphs?
===============================================================

Script: tools/relation_contrastive.py --graph <path> --counted-arm  (to be added)
Status: NOT RUN. Prediction registered and COMMITTED before the arm exists.

ANSWER: pending.


IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
Yesterday's result: a learned representation guesses missing relationship rules
much better than chance, on two big real-world knowledge graphs.

The obvious question, and the one that would deflate it: **would simply counting
do just as well?** No learning, no objective, no gradient -- just "when this
relationship appears, what usually follows it?"

If counting matches the learned version, the learning is decoration and the
result is about the data having easy statistics, not about a representation being
learned.

This project has been here before and the counting version won that time.


WHY THIS EXISTS
----------------
`DECISIONS.md` kill-list item 3 is *"does a graph database or symbolic system
already do this"*, and John's instruction is to be **delighted** when a
conventional system wins cheaply, because that says which parts do not need
learning.

The contrastive result on graphs (`docs/options/structured-relations.md`,
2026-07-30) is:

    EN_DE_15K_V2/rel_triples_1   dim 0   contrastive 0.3602   majority 0.0942
    D_W_15K_V1/rel_triples_1     dim 2   contrastive 0.3559   majority 0.0559

**The majority arm is a weak opponent** -- it ignores the question entirely and
answers the same relation every time. Counting is the strong cheap opponent and
it has not been run.

**And on CLUTRR, counting was the incumbent.** `tools/relation_profiles.py` builds
relation vectors by counting attachments and reaches 0.223 on note 070's split,
against the contrastive rule's 0.244 -- close enough that on THAT task the
objective bought little. Whether the gap is bigger on a real graph is the
question.


THE COUNTED ARM, DEFINED BEFORE IT IS RUN
-------------------------------------------
For a held-out pair `(r1, r2)`, predict, in order:

    1. the commonest r3 among TRAINING rules whose first element is r1
    2. failing that, the commonest r3 among training rules whose second is r2
    3. failing that, the global commonest r3

No learning, no vectors, no gradient. It sees exactly the same training rules the
contrastive arm sees, so a difference is the objective and not the data.

**Specified here rather than after the numbers**, because a counting baseline has
many reasonable definitions and picking the one that loses is the easiest
unconscious cheat available.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
  P1  THE TEST. `counted` scores BELOW `contrastive` on both graphs.

      Refuted if it matches or beats it. **A refutation means the objective is
      decoration** and the graph result is about easy statistics rather than a
      learned representation -- which would take the row back to where note 088
      left it.

  P2  `counted` beats the `majority` arm on both graphs.

      If it does not, the counted arm is broken rather than weak, and P1 is
      unreadable. This is the gate.

  P3  The gap between `counted` and `contrastive` is LARGER on the graphs than
      the 0.223-against-0.244 seen on CLUTRR.

      The reason to expect it: CLUTRR has 20 relations and a near-deterministic
      rule table, so counting has little room to be wrong. These graphs have 169
      and 248 relations at determinism 0.778, where a first-order count should
      degrade faster than a representation that composes.

  P4  THE ONE I AM LEAST SURE OF. `counted` lands above 0.20 -- i.e. it is a
      genuinely strong baseline rather than a formality.

      Registered because if counting reaches 0.30 against contrastive's 0.36,
      the honest headline is "a bit better than counting", not "learned
      representation works". I would rather have committed to that in advance
      than choose the framing afterwards.


WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS NOT COUNT
--------------------------------
**The two arms must see identical training rules.** Same seed, same permutation,
same 75/25 split, same items. Anything else compares a split rather than a
mechanism.

**No tuning of either.** The contrastive knobs are already carried from the
rule-prediction task and stay carried; the counted arm has no knobs by
construction, which is part of why it is the fair opponent.


COST
----
Seconds. The counted arm is two dictionaries and the contrastive numbers already
exist. Local, no dispatch.


MEASURED ON
-----------
`EN_DE_15K_V2/rel_triples_1` (invariant dimension 0) and
`D_W_15K_V1/rel_triples_1` (dimension 2), 5 seeds, 75/25 rule split, scored by
exact match on the held-out pair's modal relation.
