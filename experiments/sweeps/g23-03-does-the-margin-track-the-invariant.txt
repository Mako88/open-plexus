Does the contrastive margin track a graph's invariant dimension?
=================================================================

Script: tools/relation_contrastive.py --graph <path>
        tools/invariant_dimension.py --graph <same path>
Status: COMPLETE. Predictions registered and committed at 98c4820, before the
        sweep ran. 16 graphs, 5 seeds each, locally.

ANSWER: THE MARGIN DOES NOT TRACK THE INVARIANT -- and it is LARGER where there
is none, which is the opposite of the worry. But P4 is refuted and it is the
finding: invariant dimension is a property of the EXTRACT, not the domain.


IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
We found that a learned representation guesses missing relationship rules better
than counting, on two big knowledge graphs. One of those graphs has a tidy
mathematical structure underneath it and the other does not, and the learned
representation did about equally well on both.

Two graphs is not many. There are sixteen already downloaded.

The sharp question: **does the method do better on the graphs that have the tidy
structure?** If it does, then it is quietly exploiting that structure and the
claim "it does not need one" is wrong. If the margin is flat across graphs with
and without it, the claim holds much more strongly than two data points can show.


WHY THIS EXISTS
----------------
`docs/options/structured-relations.md` carries:

    graph                     dim  contrastive  counted  majority
    EN_DE_15K_V2/rel_triples_1  0   0.3680       0.2505   0.1000
    D_W_15K_V1/rel_triples_1    2   0.3398       0.2602   0.0797

and the claim that the mechanism is **indifferent to whether a conserved quantity
exists** -- which matters because `note 104` scoped this project's whole
composition line to domains that have one, and `generation_delta.py` gets nothing
at `dim 0` and refuses `dim > 1`.

**Two graphs cannot separate "indifferent" from "we happened to pick two similar
ones".** Sixteen can: OpenEA ships `D_W`, `D_Y`, `EN_DE` and `EN_FR` at V1 and V2,
each with two sides.

**No new download.** These were fetched with John's approval already.


THE MEASUREMENT
---------------
For every graph: `invariant_dimension.py --graph` for `dim`, and
`relation_contrastive.py --graph` for contrastive, counted, majority and chance
on held-out rules, 5 seeds, 75/25 split.

The quantity of interest is the **margin**, `contrastive - counted`, because
counted is the strong cheap opponent (`g23-02`) and majority is the weak one.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
  P1  THE TEST. The margin does NOT correlate with `dim`. Specifically: the mean
      margin on `dim 0` graphs is within 0.05 of the mean margin on `dim >= 1`
      graphs.

      Refuted if the margin is systematically larger where an invariant exists.
      **That refutation would say the mechanism has been exploiting the
      invariant all along**, and the claim it does not need one -- which is the
      strongest thing this project has said about #2 -- would have to be
      withdrawn.

  P2  Contrastive beats counted on EVERY graph.

      Refuted by a single graph where counting wins. One loss would not sink the
      mechanism but it would end "beats counting" as an unqualified statement,
      and the losing graph's properties become the next question.

  P3  At least THREE of the sixteen graphs have `dim 0`.

      A gate rather than a finding: if almost every graph has an invariant, this
      sweep has no `dim 0` group to compare against and P1 is unanswerable. If
      that happens, say so and stop rather than reporting a comparison between
      one graph and fifteen.

  P4  THE ONE I EXPECT TO BE WRONG. The V1 and V2 versions of the same pair
      agree on `dim` -- e.g. `D_W_15K_V1` and `D_W_15K_V2` are both dimension 2.

      Registered because I assumed it while writing this and want the assumption
      visible. V1 and V2 differ in density by construction, and the invariant
      dimension is a property of the CYCLE structure, which density changes. If
      they disagree, `dim` is a property of the sample rather than of the domain,
      and every statement in this project that names a domain's dimension --
      including `note 104`'s about DBpedia -- is really about one extract of it.


WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS NOT COUNT
--------------------------------
**Graph size confounding dimension.** If `dim 0` graphs are systematically bigger
or denser, a margin difference is about size and not about invariants. Edge
count, relation count and rule count are reported beside every row so the
confound is visible rather than assumed away.

**Determinism confounding it too.** These graphs are ~0.78 deterministic and
kinship is ~1.0. If that varies across the sixteen it bounds the achievable score
per graph, so it is reported per row as well.

**The knobs stay carried.** Width 32, epochs 8, lr 0.05, temperature 0.1, chosen
on CLUTRR rule prediction. Tuning per graph while the counted arm has no knobs is
the one-sided sweep the price-of-locality calibration is about.

P1 HOLDS, AND THE SIGN MATTERS MORE THAN THE MARGIN
------------------------------------------------------
0.0467 against a 0.05 bar is a hair inside, and would not survive a bar chosen
after the fact -- which is why it was chosen before.

**The direction is the reassuring part.** If the mechanism were exploiting
conserved quantities, `dim >= 1` would be the HIGHER group. It is the lower one:
+0.0974 where no invariant exists against +0.0507 where one does.

**Fragility, stated.** Only three graphs have `dim >= 1` and one of them is the
near-tie, so that mean rests on effectively two points. The claim this supports is
*"the margin does not increase with an invariant"*, not *"the margin is
independent of it"* -- the second needs more graphs at `dim >= 1` than OpenEA
contains.


P2 REFUTED, AND THE LOSS IS INFORMATIVE
------------------------------------------
Two losses. `EN_DE_V1_s1` at -0.0014 is noise.

**`D_Y_V1_s2` at -0.0834 has 48 RULES**, the smallest graph here -- a 75/25 split
leaves twelve held out. `D_Y_V2_s2` has 29 and scrapes +0.0250. Counting wins, or
nearly wins, exactly where there is almost nothing to learn from.

So *"beats counting"* is no longer unqualified. It needs data, and the amount is
somewhere between 48 and 175 rules on this evidence.


P4 REFUTED, AND IT REACHES A LOAD-BEARING CLAIM
--------------------------------------------------
Three of eight pairs disagree, all on the DBpedia side, all in the same
direction:

    D_W    side 1    V1 = 2    V2 = 0
    EN_DE  side 1    V1 = 1    V2 = 0
    EN_FR  side 1    V1 = 1    V2 = 0

**So invariant dimension is a property of the EXTRACT, not of the domain.** V1 and
V2 are different samples of the same source graph at different densities, and the
dimension is a property of the cycle structure, which density changes.

`note 104` measured `EN_DE` V2 and concluded *"DBpedia EN and DE have no additive
invariant, and not approximately"*. **The V1 extract of that same source has
dimension 1.** The honest statement is that this 15,000-entity sample has none.

That matters because note 104's finding scoped the ENTIRE composition line --
`generation_delta` is "solved wherever a conserved quantity exists" and the
handoff carried "invariants per sub-domain" as open problem #1. The with/without
framing those rest on is a property of how the graph was sampled.

**The consequence was written into P4 before the run**, which is the only reason
it reads as a finding rather than as a rationalisation.


AND THE COLUMN WAS WRONG THE FIRST TIME
------------------------------------------
The first extraction pulled `$(NF-2)` from `invariant_dimension.py`, which is the
**no-loop relation count**, not the dimension. It reported `dim=2` for a graph
measured at `dim=0` earlier the same day.

Caught only because that earlier number existed to contradict it. Otherwise the
table would have been complete, plausible and wrong -- rule 11b's shape, arriving
through a field offset rather than a stale download.
