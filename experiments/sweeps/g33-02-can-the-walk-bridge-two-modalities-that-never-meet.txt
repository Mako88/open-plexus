Can the walk bridge two modalities that never meet?
====================================================

Script: experiments/g33_02_bridging_modalities.py
Status: RUN, local, 2026-07-31, 28.4s. Predictions committed at `abbc87e`
        before the script was executed.

ANSWER: **the walk bridges a CHAIN of any length, and cannot express a STAR at
        all.** A single global `k` has to be at least the hub's degree, and
        raising it that far collapses every other class in the world.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
Everything measured so far has been easy in one specific way that nobody has
said out loud: **every occasion showed all three surfaces of a concept at once.**
The picture, the sound and the word all turned up together, so learning that they
belong together only ever required noticing one direct co-occurrence.

The real world is not like that. You see a dog and hear someone say "dog", often.
You hear a bark and hear someone say "dog", often. But you may **never** see the
picture and hear the bark in the same moment with nobody speaking. The link
between two of the three has to be inferred through the third.

That is exactly what `GOALS.md` gate **G7** asks — a concept introduced through
one modality and queried through another — and it is what
`identity-without-a-global-id.md` claims when it says a concept is *"the
equivalence class reached by starting at any member and WALKING"*. Walking is
only doing work if some members are not directly connected.

**If the walk cannot bridge them, the design has been passing an easier test than
the one it was written for**, and every number in g32 and g33 is about direct
association rather than about concepts.


WHY IT MIGHT FAIL, WHICH IS THE POINT
--------------------------------------
The recovery keeps an edge only where the ranking is MUTUAL and each surface
keeps at most `k`. With three surfaces and `k = 2` the arithmetic is comfortable:
the word's two best partners are the picture and the sound, and each of those has
the word among its own two, so all three land in one component.

**That comfort is an artefact of the sizes.** Make the chain longer — four or
five modalities where each only ever meets its neighbour — and the middle links
have to spend their `k` on the right partners while every noise surface competes
for the same slots. Nothing measured says how far a chain can stretch.

And there is a second failure available: if the bridge surface is the one that is
COMMON, it behaves like g32-02's frequency hub and may be pruned by the very
correction that makes the rest work.


WHAT WOULD BE MEASURED
----------------------
`occasions.py` already tags every surface with a modality and deliberately never
hands that tag to the mechanism. What it does NOT yet have is a way to stop two
modalities co-occurring, so it needs one knob — a pairing structure saying which
modalities may appear together on one occasion.

    chain      m0-m1, m1-m2, m2-m3 ...   neighbours only; the ends never meet
    star       m0-m1, m0-m2, m0-m3       everything meets the hub and nothing else
    complete   every pair                what every run so far has used

Scored the same way, plus one new quantity that is the gate itself:

    reachable   share of surface pairs from the SAME concept that end in one
                recovered class, counted ONLY over pairs whose modalities never
                co-occurred. Direct pairs are excluded because they are what
                the previous runs already measured

    distinguishes them by frequency alone.


HOW IT IS SCORED, AND WHY NOT WITH f1
--------------------------------------
`reached_together` over `config.apart()`, which is the set of modality pairs the
stream never shows together. `f1` averages over every surface, so a class that is
mostly right scores well even when the ONE link that had to be inferred was
missed — and that link is the entire question. Both are reported; `bridged` is
the one that answers it.

`apart()` is empty for `complete`, and `reached_together` RAISES on an empty pair
list rather than returning 1.0. Reporting a perfect score for the absence of the
question is the specific way this measurement could look like a pass while
testing nothing.

**Nothing here has been run and no number below this line exists.**

SCORECARD
---------
  P1  HELD. `complete` at 3 surfaces and k 2 gives f1 1.0000 with largest
            0.0155, reproducing g33-01 exactly. The knob changed nothing it
            should not have, which the byte-identity test also asserts.

  P2  HELD, AND IT IS THE RESULT THE DESIGN NEEDED. A three-modality chain --
            the ends never once seen together -- is bridged at **1.0000**, with
            largest 0.0777, so it is a real bridge and not a collapse.
            `identity-without-a-global-id.md`'s central claim survives its first
            actual test: the class IS reached by walking, not merely by having
            observed every pair.

  P3  REFUTED, AND FOR A REASON AVAILABLE BEFORE THE RUN. Bridging was predicted
            to degrade as the chain lengthens. It is **flat at 1.0000** for 3, 4
            and 5 modalities, and f1 actually RISES (0.6864, 0.7233, 0.7835).

            The reason is structural and I should have seen it: **a node in a
            chain has at most two neighbours**, so `k` 2 is sufficient at any
            length by construction. That is CLAUDE.md rule 10's shape again --
            an outcome guaranteed by how the condition is built -- and it is the
            second time this session, after `g32-01`'s two identical arms.

            What is NOT guaranteed, and is the real content of these rows: noise
            competes for those two slots and does not win. f1 at 0.69-0.78
            against `complete`'s 1.0000 is what that competition costs.

  P4  HELD IN OUTCOME, REFUTED IN MECHANISM, and the mechanism is the finding.
            Star bridging does collapse as spokes multiply -- 1.0000, then
            **0.3333**, then **0.1667** -- but NOT because the chance correction
            pruned the hub, which is what was predicted.

            Those numbers are exactly 1/3 and 1/6: **one spoke-pair joined out of
            three, and one out of six.** With `k` 2 the hub can keep two
            partners, so it links two spokes and every other spoke is cut off.
            It is the arithmetic of `k`, not the statistic.


THE FINDING: A SINGLE GLOBAL `k` CANNOT EXPRESS A STAR
-------------------------------------------------------
The hub needs `k` at least equal to its number of spokes. A spoke needs `k` 1.
`equivalence_classes` gives every surface the same `k`, so:

  - at `k` 2 the hub cannot reach its own spokes -- bridged 0.1667 at 5 surfaces;
  - at `k` 3 or more it can, and **every unrelated surface in the world also
    admits two or three noise partners**, so the graph chains into one component
    of 0.98 of everything.

There is no value in between, and the grid contains no `k` that works for a star
with three or more spokes.

**This is what `DECISIONS.md` 6's *bound the enumeration by the biggest
similarity gap* is for**, and it is now the thing that option has to solve rather
than a nicety: a per-surface bound, taken from where a surface's own scores fall
off, instead of one number applied to a hub and a leaf alike. That option is
already 🔀 with a measured crossover; this is a second, independent argument for
it from a completely different direction.

**And it matters for grounding specifically.** A word that names a concept is a
hub -- it co-occurs with the picture and the sound while those two may never meet
each other. So the star is not a corner case chosen to be hard; it is the shape
the motivating example has.


METRIC DEFECT, FOUND IN A FUNCTION WRITTEN THE SAME DAY
--------------------------------------------------------
`reached_together` is **recall over the nominated pairs and has recall's
failure**: a recovery that puts everything in one class satisfies every pair and
scores 1.0000.

That is not hypothetical. The first run of this grid reported `bridged` 1.0000
for chain and star at every `k` of 3 and above, and it read as a triumph. The
recovery in those cells is **one class holding 256 of 257 surfaces**. Only
`score_classes` saw it, at f1 0.0308 -- which is precisely the all-in-one-class
score for a three-surface concept in a 193-surface world.

`largest` is now printed beside it, the docstring says the function must never be
read alone, and `test_grounding` pins the behaviour so the warning cannot be
removed without the trap being visible.

**It is the same class of error as `g32-01`'s 0.5 floor, in a file written after
that lesson and by the same hand.** The standard that catches it is already in
CLAUDE.md -- compute what the metric returns for the degenerate case before
reading any number off it -- and it was not applied to a metric added mid-run.
