The last four mechanisms, re-checked against a composed readout
==============================================================

Script: experiments/g11_04_scaling_exponent.py, --components spec per cell
Status: RUN, 16 of 16 cells, run 30330836119. The FIRST dispatch had a
        disconnected write-gate arm -- `write_gate` does nothing without
        `corrective_writes` and the choice set only the gate -- which showed as
        +0.000 in every cell. See decision 79.

ANSWER: **Four of five mechanisms re-checked across g11-07 and g11-08 moved.**
        Only consolidation's verdict survived. Best cell in either grid is
        readout=hidden128 with write=gated at 5.149.


IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
We changed the part of the model that turns its internal state into a guess, and
then found that several earlier conclusions had only been true because that part
was weak. Two mechanisms we had written off turned out to be partly fine once the
guesser improved.

**This checks the rest of them.** Four settings were measured against the old
guesser and never re-measured: a gate on how much each write commits, a slow
store that rescues things from the fast one, a constant term on the output, and
one more that cannot be tested yet and is explained below.


WHY THIS EXISTS
----------------
Decision 74 established that every number in this project's comparison set was
measured beside a LINEAR readout. g11-07 re-checked four mechanisms and **two of
the two refuted ones moved**: pair keys recovered 60-80% of their penalty and
settling went from costing 0.13-0.16 bits to gaining 0.01-0.06.

Two of two is not a reassuring base rate, and decision 77 named the four
mechanisms still unchecked. This is those four.


WHAT IS NOT HERE, AND WHY
--------------------------
**`orthogonal_every` cannot be re-checked without implementing it.** The model
REFUSES it alongside a hidden layer, deliberately: it orthogonalises an update
whose shape is defined by the linear readout, and across two layers it would
silently orthogonalise a different matrix than the one it was measured on.

That refusal is right and it is also why this mechanism is now the MOST
interesting of the four rather than the least. Decision 54 refuted it because
"our rank collapse is the DATA being low-rank, not the rule failing -- a cure for
someone else's disease", and Boeshertz applied Muon **per layer in a deep
network**. With one linear map there was nothing to orthogonalise per-layer.
With two there is, and the argument that refuted it does not obviously survive.

Recorded in BACKLOG rather than guessed at. Implementing orthogonalisation for a
two-layer readout is a change, not a re-check, and mixing the two in one sweep is
how a result stops being interpretable.


PREDICTIONS
------------
Registered before dispatch. **These are weaker predictions than g11-07's** and it
is worth saying so: g11-07 had a mechanism-specific reason to expect recovery
(pair keys carry more information). Here the reasoning is generic, so a
confirmation is worth less than a refutation.

  P1  IDENTITY. `write=plain,readout=linear` lands at 5.525, the figure g11-07
      and three separate probes reached. Disagreement is a dispatch error.

  P2  THE READOUT STILL DOMINATES. Every `hidden128` cell beats its `linear`
      counterpart, as it did 9 of 9 in g11-07.

  P3  **THE WRITE GATE'S EFFECT SHRINKS.** g11-01 found the gate's apparent
      "trade" was a missing scalar rather than a property. A gate scales how much
      of each correction is committed, which changes what the store holds, and a
      composed readout is better able to use a store it did not shape. Weak
      reasoning, stated as such.

  P4  **CONSOLIDATION DOES NOT RECOVER.** It rescues bindings from a decaying
      fast store into a slow one, which is a claim about RETENTION rather than
      about information a linear map could not read. If the readout changes its
      verdict, the mechanism was never doing what its name says.

  P5  **READOUT BIAS STILL FAILS, AND FOR THE SAME REASON.** g11-02 refuted it
      because it imports the marginal and collapsed `reward_recall`. A hidden
      layer can already represent a constant, so the bias should be redundant
      rather than harmful under `hidden128` -- near zero, not negative.

WHAT WOULD REFUTE EACH: P1 by a baseline away from 5.525. P2 by any linear cell
winning. P3 by the gate's effect being the same or larger under `hidden128`. P4
by consolidation helping under `hidden128` when it did not under `linear`. P5 by
the bias helping or hurting materially under `hidden128`.

**P4 is the one that would teach the most if refuted**, because it would mean a
mechanism named for retention is actually doing something to representation, and
nothing in the record would have said so.


COST
-----
12 jobs: 3 write settings x 4 readout settings, two seeds each on 2 workers,
width 64, chunk 128, 60,000 characters, dense keys, plain retrieval.

    write     plain, gated (write_gate 0.5), consolidating
    readout   linear, hidden128, bias, hidden128+bias

`consolidating` carries `lasting_cap` 5.0 and `decay` 0.997 with it, because the
model refuses consolidation without them -- a salience gate with no cap diverges
and consolidation with a memory that never fades has no fast store to rescue
from. Carried in the component spec so the choice is one word rather than three
fields a caller has to remember and one of them silently omit.

g11-07's cells at these settings ran 18-45 s locally. Using the **7.6x
local-to-runner factor measured on g11-06**, the worst cell is ~6 minutes against
a 300-minute timeout, and the matrix is ~12 minutes of local-equivalent job time.


MEASURED ON
------------
Tiny Shakespeare, 64 symbols, 60,000 training characters, **the same protocol as
g11-07 and as the record being re-validated** -- 80/20 fit/calibration split,
temperature fitted on held-out training text, tested on the corpus's held-out
tail, two epochs. Changing the protocol while re-checking the readout would
confound the thing this exists to isolate.

Above the ~16,000 saturation point (decision 63), so this measures LEVEL and says
nothing about slope.
