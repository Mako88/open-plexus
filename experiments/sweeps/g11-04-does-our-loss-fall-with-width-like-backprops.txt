Does our loss fall with width the way a backprop model's does?
================================================================

Script: experiments/g11_04_scaling_exponent.py
Status: RUN, 12 of 12 cells. **THE CONTROL FAILED AND THE SWEEP ANSWERS
        NOTHING** about the exponent question it was built for. It produced two
        other findings on the way, which are real.

ANSWER: **Inadmissible.** The backprop baseline's exponent is -0.0021 with an R2
        of 0.13 -- flat. If the reference does not scale with width over this
        range, no comparison against it means anything, and the summariser says
        so itself rather than reporting three exponents as agreement.


COST
-----
12 jobs, 4 widths x 3 arms, two seeds each, chunk 128, 250k training
characters, 300-minute timeout. All 12 returned; none timed out.


MEASURED ON
------------
Tiny Shakespeare, 64 symbols. Training stream capped at 250,000 characters,
80/20 fit/calibration split within it, tested on the corpus's held-out tail.
Temperature fitted on held-out TRAINING text for our arms; the backprop arm's
logits are used raw, since fitting a temperature for it would flatter it.

PREDICTIONS, SCORED
--------------------
P1  the single-token arm is FLAT, |b| < 0.02.
    **REFUTED, barely.** b = -0.0213, just past the threshold I set. Note 035's

    but I wrote a number down and the number is on the other side of it.

P2  the backprop arm has a clearly negative exponent. THIS IS THE CONTROL.
    **REFUTED.** b = -0.0021, R2 = 0.13. The reference does not scale either.

P3  the context arm is steeper than the single-token arm.
    **REFUTED.** Context is SHALLOWER (-0.0176 against -0.0213), and worse in
    absolute terms at every width.

P4  neither of our arms reaches the backprop arm's exponent.
    **VACUOUSLY "confirmed" and worthless.** Both our arms are steeper than a
    baseline that is flat. Recording this as a win would be dishonest.

One of four, and the one that decided admissibility failed.


WHY THE CONTROL FAILED, AND IT IS MY DOING
-------------------------------------------
The backprop baseline is already at 4.20 bits by width 16 and does not improve.
It is **data-limited, not width-limited** -- 250,000 characters is not enough
text for a wider attention model to have anything more to learn.

And 250,000 characters is exactly the cap I introduced to stop g11-04 timing out
the way g11-03 did. **The fix for the cost problem removed the phenomenon the
experiment was built to measure.** The two failure modes traded directly against
each other and I did not notice the trade when making it.

The rule this earns, alongside g11-03's: **when re-scoping a sweep to fit a
budget, check that the control can still fire.** A cheaper sweep that cannot
resolve its own reference is worth less than no sweep, because it costs the same
and invites a conclusion.


WHAT IS REAL HERE ANYWAY
-------------------------
Two things, both worth having.

**1. Pair keys lose at every width from 16 to 128, and the gap does not narrow.**
Single beats context by 0.187 bits at width 16 and 0.209 at width 128. This
answers the question g11-03 could not: its P2 predicted the gap would narrow
with width, and across four widths it does not. **The pair key is not waiting
for a width we have not tried.** Note 034's ceiling result stands -- the ceiling
is real and it moves -- but paying for it needs something other than width, and
the exact cache is the current candidate.

**2. Backprop attention beats a unigram at width 16.** 4.20 bits against the
unigram bar of 4.829, using roughly 10,000 parameters. Our best is 5.49 at width
128. The reference is not a strawman and the gap is not small.


WHAT TO DO INSTEAD
-------------------
The exponent question needs DATA on the x-axis, not width, or width measured
where the baseline is not saturated. Fitting loss against training tokens at
fixed width would put both arms in a regime where the reference still moves, and
it is the axis Filipovich et al. actually used. That is the re-run.

A second bug, unrelated: `tools/summarise_g11_04.py` produced no output in the
CI aggregate step though it runs correctly on the same artifacts locally. The
numbers above were computed by re-running it on the downloaded artifacts. Worth
fixing before the re-run, since a silent summariser is how a sweep gets scored
by hand and mis-transcribed.
