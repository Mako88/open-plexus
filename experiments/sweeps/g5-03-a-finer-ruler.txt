How fast does the minimum machine size grow?
============================================

Script: experiments/g5_03_finer.py
Status: PREDICTIONS WRITTEN, NOT YET RUN


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
g5-02 asked how small each machine can be before the system stops working, and
whether that floor rises as problems get harder. It came back 0.50 give or take
0.50 -- a range so wide it contains both the good answer and the bad one.

The reason was a ruler marked in doublings. Every machine size tested was twice
the last, and the thing being measured moves by about a doubling across the whole
range. You cannot measure a 2x difference with 2x graduations.

This runs the same question with a finer ruler, and a longer run-up.


WHY THE RULER WAS COARSE, WHICH WAS NOT OBVIOUS
------------------------------------------------
Machine width has to divide total width exactly. g5-02 held the total at **256**,
and 256 has almost no divisors in the range that matters:

    divisors of 256 between 8 and 64:   8, 16, 32, 64        (factor-of-two steps)
    divisors of 240 between 8 and 64:   8, 10, 12, 15, 16, 20, 24, 30, 40, 48, 60

**The coarse grid was not a sampling choice. It was forced by picking a power of
two for the total**, which looked like a neutral, tidy default and quietly set the
resolution of the whole experiment. A round number is not a free choice when
something else has to divide it.

Switching the total to 240 -- almost the same capacity -- takes the worst step
from 2.00x to 1.33x.


AND A LONGER RUN-UP, AT THE CHEAP END
---------------------------------------
Resolution in the exponent is `log(width step) / log(sequence span)`, so the span
helps as much as the step. g5-02 spanned 96 to 384, a factor of 4.

Adding **seq_len 48** stretches that to a factor of 8, and 48 is the cheapest
row in the sweep rather than the most expensive.

    240 grid, span 4x  ->  +/-0.21     still contains 0.37; not decisive
    240 grid, span 8x  ->  +/-0.14     decisive against 0.6, tight around 0.37

The previous note said a longer lever arm mattered more than finer steps and
would cost roughly four times as much. **The first half was right and the second
was wrong** -- it assumed extending the span meant longer sequences, when
extending it downward works just as well and is nearly free.


QUESTION
--------
Hold total width at 240, cut it P ways, and locate the smallest machine width
that still reaches 0.9 -- then fit how that floor grows with sequence length.

  total width  240 (fixed)
  P            1, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 16, 20, 24, 30, 40
               -> machine width 240, 40, 30, 24, 20, 16, 15, 12, 10, 8, 6
  seq_len      48, 96, 192, 384
  lr           0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2
  seeds        1, 2, 3
  budget       8 epochs

220 jobs. Costed: 20.7 hours serial, worst job 12 minutes, about an hour of wall
clock. Under GitHub's 256-job matrix cap, which is worth stating because a matrix
that silently truncates would drop exactly the cells at the end of the grid.

**Pre-registered: the fit is on the `alone` criterion**, a single machine
answering without pooling, because that is the one C1 needs and because g5-02
found it the better resolved of the two. `pooled` is reported alongside. Choosing
between them after seeing which gave a tidier exponent would be the same error as
fitting through a bound.


PREDICTIONS -- written before the run
--------------------------------------
1. **The floor is located at every length, none at a grid edge.** *(Already
   partly established: the pre-dispatch control locates seq 48 in (6, 8].)* g5-02 bounded
   it to (8,16] at seq 96 and (16,32] at 192 and 384, and this grid has five
   points inside those intervals. If any row still lands at the floor, the total
   width of 240 is too small for that row and the design failed again.

2. **The floor rises with sequence length.** Guess: about 10-12 at seq 48, 14-16
   at 96, 20-24 at 192, 24-30 at 384.

3. **Therefore the exponent comes out near 0.4** -- close to g1-10's 0.37 for
   total width, so **the usable machine count is roughly FLAT with problem size,
   not shrinking.** From the guessed floors, log(27/11)/log(8) = 0.43.

   **That is the favourable answer and it is the one to distrust.** Pre-registered
   checks if it lands near 0.37: that the located floors are spread across
   distinct grid points rather than bunched on two; that P=1 is at ceiling for
   every length, so no row is capacity-limited rather than division-limited; and
   that the learning-rate grid is unpinned **per row**, since g5-02's pooled
   check called a pinned row clean.

4. **P=1 reaches 1.000 at every length.** The ceiling control. 240 undivided is
   five times g1-13's crossing of 46.8 at seq 384.

Most likely wrong: (2), specifically at seq 48. The floor there may sit below 8,
which was this grid's smallest machine, in which case that row is bounded rather
than located and the span collapses back to 4x -- taking the resolution with it.
**If that happens the honest report is the 4x number, not a fit through the
bound.**

> **That is exactly what happened, and it was caught before dispatch.** The
> control run put seq 48 at machine width 8 scoring **0.975** alone -- comfortably
> above the bar, so the floor was outside the grid and that row would have been
> unfittable. Extending downward found it: width 6 scores **0.777**, so the floor
> at seq 48 lies in **(6, 8]**. `P=40` was added and `P=4` dropped to stay under
> the 256-job cap.
>
> The prediction that named this as the likeliest failure was written before the
> control was run, and the control was run before 220 jobs were dispatched. The
> whole cost was three short runs on the cheapest row in the sweep.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- exponent clearly below 0.37     -> usable machine count GROWS with problem
                                     size. Better than hoped, so check hardest
- exponent 0.3 to 0.45            -> count is flat; partitioning scales. G5
                                     passes and the earlier wall was entirely an
                                     artefact of 16-wide machines
- exponent above 0.55             -> count shrinks; past some size capability is
                                     bought by bigger machines, not more of them.
                                     G5 fails, this time with the resolution to
                                     say so
- any row bounded, not located    -> report the reduced span honestly and say
                                     which row failed, rather than fitting


WHAT THIS STILL DOES NOT SETTLE
---------------------------------
The floor is measured at ONE total width. g4-01 hints the two interact -- 32-wide
machines scored 0.903 at total 64 and 0.992 at total 128 -- so "minimum machine
width" is really "minimum machine width at generous total capacity". Whether it
moves when capacity is tight is untested.

Nor does it touch the step RATE, still the last unmeasured term in note 009's
`d . rate` bandwidth product.
