IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
The previous run closed a problem that had resisted five attempts, and it did so
with a perfect score. That is exactly when to be suspicious.

Here is the specific way it could have been luck. The test asks whether the
ever-present hum sneaks into a word's top five. The hum scores about 0.28. If the
weakest genuine partner scored, say, 0.30, then the hum would be sitting at
number six with almost nothing separating it — and any small change would tip it
in. A pass in that situation is not a result, it is a coin balanced on its edge.

So this measures the distance rather than the verdict.

The hum is not at number six. It is at **number nineteen**, and the weakest
genuine partner outscores it by more than the hum's entire score. That is a wide
gap, not a near miss.

The second question is whether it holds up when there is more than one such
thing, since a real room has a hum *and* a lamp *and* the word "the". It does,
and for a reason that is pleasing rather than lucky: everything that is always
present has the same count, so it gets the same low score. They stack up together
at the bottom instead of one of them climbing.


WHAT IS BEING MEASURED
----------------------
    rank           where the best-placed distractor sits in a word's FULL
                   ranking. The pass/fail column in `g39-04` cannot show this
    want           how many image codes genuinely belong to that word. The
                   threshold `rank` has to clear
    weakest true   the lowest-scoring genuine partner
    best distr     the highest-scoring distractor
    margin         weakest true minus best distr. **The quantity that decides
                   whether `g39-04` is robust**
    admitted       `g39-04`'s own column, reproduced as a rail

`admitted` reproducing 0.0000 is what makes the other columns comparable with
that run rather than describing some other configuration.

SCORECARD
---------
  P1  **HELD, with room to spare.** `rank` is **19.1** against `want` **5.0** —
            fourteen positions clear, not one. The margin is **0.4464**, which
            exceeds the distractor's own score of 0.2802.

            So `g39-04` is not a near miss. A word's ranking would have to change
            substantially before the distractor entered its top five.

            **What sits between rank 5 and rank 19 is not scored here** and is
            worth knowing: it will be the word's own audio codes and foreign
            image codes. Neither affects `link`, which counts image codes only,
            but it means the top of a word's list is not purely its own pictures.

  P2  **HELD, and the mechanism is better than the prediction.** Every count from
            1 to 8 gives identical numbers to four decimals.

            **That is not a coincidence and it should generalise.** A surface
            present on every occasion has, by definition, the same count as every
            other such surface, so `conditional` gives them all the same score.
            Adding more of them adds more surfaces at 0.2802 — they accumulate at
            the same depth rather than any of them climbing.

            So the refusal does not weaken as clutter accumulates, which is the
            property a real stream would need.


WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE
-------------------------
**Distractors that are present ALMOST always.** Every one here is at 100%
presence, which is `g32-01`'s falsifier condition and the easiest case for
`conditional` to reject. A surface present on 90% of occasions scores higher and
sits closer to the true partners. **That is the untested case and it is the
realistic one** — a lamp is in most rooms, not all of them.

**One code count.** `want` is 5.0 because 50 codes over 10 digits gives 5 each.
At 100 codes `want` would be 10 and the distractor's rank would have to clear a
higher bar. Unswept.

**The `weakest true` figure is a mean over words and seeds.** A single bad word
could sit far lower without moving it, and nothing here reports the worst case.
`CLAUDE.md` rule 3's calibration is precisely that: a mean of 1.0000 hid a
0.7015.
