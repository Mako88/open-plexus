Does width fix retrieval fidelity on the task, and does that unblock composition?
=================================================================================

Script: experiments/g13_01_does_width_fix_fidelity.py
Status: DISPATCHED. Predictions below were registered before the matrix ran.

ANSWER: pending.


IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
The model answers questions about facts by looking things up in a memory. When
the person asked about appears in only one fact, the lookup is right. When they
appear in several, it is right about as often as guessing between them.

An earlier measurement suggested the fix was simply a bigger memory. This checks
that on the actual task rather than on a stripped-down version of it, and it
splits the score by how many facts the person appears in -- because there are two
different things that could be going wrong, and only one of them is fixed by
more memory.

If the memory is merely NOISY, a bigger one fixes it. If the question is
genuinely AMBIGUOUS -- several facts fit, and nothing in the question says which
-- then no amount of memory helps, and the model needs to try a possibility and
check where it leads. That second thing is called search, and this run decides
whether it is worth building.


WHY THIS EXISTS
----------------
Four mechanisms have failed against retrieval fidelity, each correct in itself:
the accumulator (decision 102), pair keys alongside hops (105), traversal (107)
and search (111). Decision 112 then ablated fidelity in isolation and found a
WIDTH limit -- 0.915 as configured, 1.000 at width 128 and 256.

Nothing had re-run the task at those widths. STATE.md made that the critical
path on the strength of the extrapolation, and this is the measurement that
either supports it or removes it.

**It is also the first committed instrument the relational line has ever had.**
Decisions 99 through 119 -- twenty-one entries, the whole live line -- were
measured by inline probes that left no script behind. `kinship` appeared only in
its task module and its unit test. That is why a churn probe from this same line
returned on 2026-07-28 with no condition string, no seed count and no
pre-registered prediction, and had to be set aside under rule 11b.


WHAT IT DECIDES
----------------
Decision 111 refused search on the grounds that "you cannot search your way out
of noisy primitives, because the verifier is built from the primitives."

That refusal is CONDITIONAL on the primitives being noisy. Decision 108 says the
residual at out-degree > 1 is not noise but AMBIGUITY: the store returns a
relation the subject genuinely holds 96% of the time, and "correct" tracks 1/k.
If both hold, width leaves the primitives reliable-but-ambiguous, which is
exactly the regime where search is right and its verifier is trustworthy.

John approved the direction in advance on 2026-07-28: "any functionality and/or
adjustments that get us closer to our goals ... as long as it doesn't contradict
with those." So the gate on building search is this measurement, not a decision.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
  P1  CONTROL. hop1-pair at out-degree 1 rises with width and clears 0.99 at
      256, reproducing decision 112 end-to-end. If it does not, this instrument
      disagrees with the record and nothing else in the run is readable.

  P2  hop1-pair overall accuracy rises with width but does NOT reach 1.000,
      because the out-degree > 1 sequences are ambiguous rather than noisy.

  P3  hop1-pair at out-degree >= 2 stays within 0.10 of its 1/k bound at EVERY
      width. This is the direct test of decision 108 and the one that decides
      search.

  P4  hop2-concat gains less from width than hop1-pair does, because composition
      compounds two lookups and the second is the ambiguous one.

  P5  hop2-concat at width 256 still does not clear shortcut_floor. Decision 102
      measured concat matching the one-hop model exactly, and width does not
      supply a mechanism that was missing.

P3 REFUTED would mean decision 108's ambiguity account is wrong, search is not
the next build, and the fidelity story is incomplete further back.


P1 WAS REFUTED BY A SMOKE SEED, BEFORE DISPATCH
------------------------------------------------
Recorded rather than quietly rewritten. Seed 0 was run first to check the arms
were wired up at all -- a broken arm inside a grid does not announce itself, it
produces a number and the number goes in a table.

  hop1-pair  d64   overall 0.760   k=1 1.000   k=2 0.549 (1/k 0.500)   k=3+ 0.448
  hop1-pair  d128  overall 0.775   k=1 1.000   k=2 0.577               k=3+ 0.483
  hop1-pair  d256  overall 0.780   k=1 1.000   k=2 0.592               k=3+ 0.483
  hop2-concat d64  overall 0.340   k=1 0.325   k=2 0.379               k=3+ 0.286
  hop2-concat d128 overall 0.335   k=1 0.333   k=2 0.364               k=3+ 0.214
  hop2-concat d256 overall 0.345   k=1 0.325   k=2 0.394               k=3+ 0.286

Out-degree 1 is ALREADY 1.000 at width 64, where P1 assumed 0.915 from decision
112. The prediction is refuted in the unexpected direction: there was nothing at
that out-degree for width to fix.

**The difference is a TRAINED READOUT, and neither measurement is wrong.**
Decision 112 ablated raw retrieval with no readout learning; this trains Wo for
400 x 4 sequences, and a linear readout over a noisy retrieval can recover the
argmax even when the retrieval itself is not clean. That is the same distinction
note 037 records on text -- the retrieval CARRIES the information and the
question is what can extract it.

So the honest restatement, which the matrix is being run to test at eight seeds:
**width moves the overall number by about 0.02 across a fourfold range, and
every bit of the remaining gap sits at out-degree >= 2.** That is P3's territory
and it points at search rather than at capacity.

One seed. The width effect is 0.02 wide and three-seed grids in this project
have twice failed to bound an effect of that size, which is why this runs at
eight.


COST
----
2 arms x 3 widths x 8 seeds = 48 cells, in 8 jobs of 6 cells.

Estimated FROM THE MOST EXPENSIVE CELL -- width 256, hop2-concat, where the d^2
store cost and the doubled hop count meet. The store is d x d and the per-step
work is a matvec, so width 256 is SIXTEEN times width 64, not four.

  measured by --cost:  21.0 ms per training sequence
                       0.6 min to train one cell (400 sequences x 4 epochs)
  worst job:           ~3 min if every one of its 6 cells were the dear one

g11-03 lost four of six cells to a 240-minute timeout by estimating from a cheap
cell that had been run locally. The probe exists so that cannot happen twice.


MEASURED ON
-----------
openplexus/tasks/kinship.py -- 12 people, 10 facts, hops 1 and 2, answers
balanced on the composed relation. Floors are measured per configuration by
shortcut_floors() rather than assumed: `majority` and `first` are floors,
`last` and `ends` are information bounds and are NOT floors, because reaching
the far end of the path is the work being asked for.

Both arms use derived_keys. hop1-pair adds context_keys; hop2-concat cannot,
because the model refuses hops > 1 alongside context_keys -- a hop re-encodes
through Wk while the store keys on pairs, measured cosine -0.069 (decision 105).
That refusal is correct and is not worked around here.
