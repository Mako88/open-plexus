Is there anything local for a tag to hang on?
=============================================

Script: experiments/g9_04_is_there_a_local_signal.py
Status: PRE-REGISTERED, with a pilot already seen. See DISCLOSURE below.


WHY THIS COMES BEFORE THE TAG
------------------------------
[g9-03](g9-03-is-the-cliff-reach-or-cost.txt) settled that a window has to be
MATCHED to a lag nobody knows in advance, which is what makes a tag a mechanism
rather than a saving. The obvious next move is to build it.

**Reading the generator first says one of the two things a tag could do is
impossible here, by construction.** `reward_recall` chooses which cues get
rewarded with `rng.sample(cues, n_rewarded)` -- uniformly, from cues drawn out of
the same alphabet as the filler. A rewarded binding and an unrewarded one are
statistically identical until the reward token arrives. **Nothing local can
predict reward**, and a tag that claimed to would be reading `position_kinds()`
in disguise.

That leaves the other thing a tag can do, and it is the one the biology actually
describes: set a cheap marker on everything worth marking, let it decay, and let
the late signal capture whatever is still tagged. The tag is not selective about
value. It is selective about BEING A BINDING AT ALL.

Which matters here more than it sounds, because of a number nobody had printed:

    body 744 steps / 24 bindings  ~=  31 steps per binding

A window of 64 steps holds about **two bindings and sixty-two steps of filler**.
That is the whole of g9-03's fall-off, and it says a span-based reach is doomed
at this density however it is tuned. A capacity of four BINDINGS spans ~124
steps; a capacity of four STEPS spans four.

So: can a node tell a binding-write from a filler-write, from its own signals?


DISCLOSURE
-----------
A one-cell pilot was run locally before these predictions were written, at width
32 seed 1. It is reported here as part of the record rather than described
afterwards as a hunch. The predictions are written to be about what the pilot
does NOT settle -- whether it holds across widths and seeds.

First at 8 sequences, then again at 32 after `hit` was added:

           signal     b-vs-f (8)  rew (8)     b-vs-f (32)  rew (32)
         surprise          0.464    0.453           0.487     0.497
         strength          0.306    0.466           0.298     0.490
    dev-from-mean          0.377    0.489           0.378     0.506
              hit              -        -           0.494     0.500
         position          0.480    0.617           0.480     0.541

    steps per binding: 31.0

**Four times the data moved `position` vs rewarded from 0.617 to 0.541**, which
is prediction 3 already half-answered and the reason the grid runs 32 sequences.
It is also why the 8-sequence row is left in: it is what an under-powered cell
looks like next to the same cell with more data, and the difference is larger
than several results this project has taken seriously.


THE GRID
--------
  width     32, 64            does the answer depend on how full the store is
  seed      1..6
  sequences 32 per cell       the pilot's shakiest number came from 32 positives

Signals scored: surprise, strength (the norm of the retrieved trace, which is
what competitive capture already ranks on), absolute deviation from the node's
running mean surprise, and POSITION -- a deliberate ringer, because a window
ranks on recency and nothing else, so position says how much of any separation
recency alone already had.

AUC is rank-based with ties at half, and is deliberately NOT folded to
`max(a, 1-a)`: a signal that separates the classes backwards is still a usable
signal, and hiding the direction would hide the mechanism.


PREDICTIONS
------------
1. **`strength` separates binding from filler, in the INVERTED direction, and it
   holds at both widths.** Guess: 0.25-0.40 at every cell. The reason is
   structural rather than empirical -- filler is drawn with replacement from 40
   spare cues over ~700 positions, so a filler key has been bound many times and
   retrieves strongly, while a binding's cue is fresh and retrieves weakly. So
   the admission rule a tag wants is **admit the WEAK retrievals**, which is the
   opposite of what competitive capture does, and that inversion is a finding
   about the six failed mechanisms rather than only about this one.

2. **`surprise` stays useless, within 0.45-0.55 everywhere.** It is the signal
   four previous mechanisms were built on. If it were the answer it would not
   have taken this long.

2b. **`hit` -- predict the future and compare, in its literal form -- carries
   nothing either, within 0.45-0.55 everywhere.** John asked whether this was
   worth revisiting given everything fixed since it last failed, and the answer
   is that it IS worth revisiting and this is the cheapest possible form of the
   revisit: score the raw signal against the label directly, instead of inferring
   its quality from a mechanism that failed for any of six other reasons.
   `consolidate-on-use` fires on exactly this quantity and has never been
   measured apart from the machinery around it.

   It is scored as a separate column from `surprise` on purpose. One is a binary
   hit on the argmax and the other is continuous over the whole prediction, and
   a signal can carry information in either without the other.

3. **`position` vs rewarded collapses to ~0.5 with more data.** The pilot's 0.617
   came from 32 positives against 160 negatives in a single cell. The generator
   shuffles cue order before assigning rewards, so there is no mechanism for a
   real effect. **If it does NOT collapse, this probe has a leak and every number
   in it is suspect** -- that is the outcome that stops the tag rather than
   starting it.

4. **Every `rewarded vs unrewarded` AUC sits within noise of 0.5, for every
   signal.** This is the control that says what the tag can and cannot be. It is
   near-tautological given the generator, and it is here because the generator is
   the thing being trusted -- a task that leaked reward identity into local
   statistics would make a tag look like it worked for the wrong reason.

Most likely wrong: (1), specifically the "holds at both widths" half. Retrieval
norm scales with how much is stored, and 64 dimensions over the same bindings is
a different signal-to-noise regime; the direction should survive, the size may
not.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- some signal separates binding from filler   -> a fixed capacity over ADMITTED
                                                 items reaches an arbitrary
                                                 delay. BUILD THE TAG, and admit
                                                 on that signal
- every signal near 0.5                       -> admission is a random subset, so
                                                 a small tag is WORSE than a
                                                 small window. That is a result
                                                 about this whole line of work,
                                                 arriving before the mechanism
                                                 instead of after it
- rewarded vs not away from 0.5               -> a leak in the probe or the
                                                 generator. Fix that first;
                                                 nothing else in the file counts


COST
-----
2 widths x 6 seeds = 12 jobs. The pilot ran 8 sequences at width 32 in a few
seconds; 32 sequences at width 64 is roughly 16x that, so well under a minute a
job. This is the cheapest thing dispatched in weeks, which is the point -- it is
a measurement standing in front of a build.


THE ORACLE, SAID TWICE
-----------------------
Labels come from `position_kinds()`. It scores signals the model computed without
it, and nothing measured is fed back into anything. That distinction is the
entire difference between this and cheating.
