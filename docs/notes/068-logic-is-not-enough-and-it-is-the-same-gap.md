068 — Logic is not enough, and it is the gap note 067 already measured
=====================================================================

**Status: REASONED, NOT MEASURED.** Nothing here was run. It is recorded because it
converges with note 067's measurement from a completely different direction, and that
convergence is the reason to trust the direction rather than either argument alone.

---

## IN PLAIN TERMS

John's objection, sent while note 067 was being committed: *"everything we're building
relies on facts, and it's all about logical reasoning and progression. But I think there's
much more to original thought than logical reasoning."*

**It is correct, and it names the same missing piece that note 067 measured.**

Everything running today is deduction over a table of rules: *father then sister is aunt*,
applied a step at a time. Note 066 measured where that stops — right almost always where
the rules exist (98.8%), and able to act on only about half the test set, because a table
knows only what it was told.

**Analogy is not deduction.** That a family tree and an org chart share a shape, that
`grandfather` behaves like `father` twice over, does not follow from any premise. It comes
from things *resembling* one another. Note 067 measured that relations here resemble
nothing: each is a **random** pattern, so held-out composition generalises at 0.056
against chance 0.050.

**So the requirement arrived at from accuracy and the requirement arrived at from
originality are the same requirement.** That is the finding worth recording.

---

## The two arguments, and why the convergence matters

    from ACCURACY      tabulation caps at 52% because a table cannot generalise;
    (measured, 066/067) binding over random vectors generalises at chance.
                       Requirement: relations that encode how relations resemble
                       one another

    from ORIGINALITY   deduction can only rearrange what it was given; analogy and
    (reasoned, here)   association are "move to something NEARBY", not "derive what
                       follows", and nearby is undefined without a similarity
                       geometry.
                       Requirement: the same one

**Two independent routes to one unbuilt mechanism.** Neither argument is evidence for the
other — one is a measurement and one is reasoning, and stacking them does not make either
stronger. What it does mean is that building it serves both goals, so it is not a
benchmark-chasing detour from GOALS §1.

## What a similarity geometry buys that a rule table cannot

    deduction     derive what follows        HAVE (98.8% where rules exist)
    closure       iterate deduction          HAVE (closure.py, G0 headroom 0.277)
    analogy       structural resemblance     needs relation similarity
    association   undirected drift to near   needs relation similarity
    abduction     guess the best explanation needs closure run backwards, untried

The first two are entailment and are built. **The rest all reduce to "nearby", which is
exactly what random relation vectors do not provide.**

## And what this does NOT reach, stated because it is the honest half

**Salience — noticing something is odd.** Entailment has no notion of surprise. There is a
candidate signal already present rather than invented: when closure derives a fact and the
store holds a different one, that is a **contradiction**, and decision 148's gate is exact
(1.0000/0.0000, structurally zero on an unwritten address) so it can tell a contradiction
from a blur. A contradiction is a defensible thing to be curious about. **Unbuilt, and no
measurement is claimed for it.**

**What makes something worth thinking about at all** — motivation, affect, wanting. GOALS
says nothing about this and nothing in the current architecture suggests a mechanism.
Recorded as unaddressed rather than deferred, because deferring implies a plan.

**And the sharpest form of John's objection stands:** a system whose only operation is
entailment can only rearrange what it was given. Composition producing bindings never
written is more than retrieval, but it is still *implied* by the facts held. Reaching
anything not implied needs the geometry, and the geometry is unbuilt.

## What would make this note wrong

If structured relation vectors are built and generalise on held-out composition **without**
producing anything analogy-like — similarity that predicts the right answer but never
transfers across domains — then the two requirements were not one requirement, and this
note's central claim was a coincidence of vocabulary. That is a real possibility and the
convergence above is not protection against it.
