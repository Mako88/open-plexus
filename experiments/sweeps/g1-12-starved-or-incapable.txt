Is attention undertrained at long sequences, or genuinely too small?
=====================================================================

Script: experiments/g1_12_starved.py
Status: PREDICTIONS WRITTEN, NOT YET RUN


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
The last experiment produced a result in our favour: measured by memory rather
than width, our approach uses a fifth of what the conventional one does on long
inputs.

Before that goes anywhere, it has to survive an obvious objection. The favourable
number came from the conventional model doing BADLY on long inputs. But we gave
it the same amount of training at every length -- and a longer input has the same
handful of questions buried in far more padding, so the useful part of each
training step is diluted.

So it might not be too small. It might just be under-trained, and we would be
taking credit for our own stopwatch.

This trains it four times longer and finds out. We wrote down before the previous
run that we would do exactly this if the result came out in our favour, and it
did, so here it is.


QUESTION
--------
g1-11 measured attention at width 8 scoring 0.985, 0.999, 0.850, 0.252 as seq_len
goes 48, 96, 192, 384 -- all at a fixed 3000 training steps. That degradation
drove its crossing from 7.4 to 15.1 and produced g1-11's 5.12x memory advantage.

Train attention at seq_len 192 and 384, widths 8 and 16, for 12000 steps with
checkpoints at 1500, 3000, 6000 and 12000. 3 seeds.


PREDICTIONS -- written before the run
--------------------------------------
1. **d=8 at seq_len=384 climbs substantially with more steps.** This is the
   uncomfortable prediction and it is the one I expect. The gradient signal is
   diluted rather than absent, and dilution is a rate problem.

2. It reaches 0.9 by 12000 steps. If so, attention's crossing at 384 is ~8 rather
   than 15.1, prediction 1 of g1-11 was right after all, and **g1-11's memory
   ratios are inflated and must be recomputed.** The corrected ratio at 384 would
   be 2*384*8 / 2256 = 2.72x rather than 5.12x -- still favourable, still growing,
   but half what was reported.

3. seq_len=192 at d=8 also climbs, from 0.850 to above 0.9. It is much closer to
   the line so this should be quick.

4. The local rule is NOT exposed to the same confound in the same way, but this
   is worth stating rather than assuming: g1-10 trained it for a fixed 8 epochs
   over 400 sequences at every seq_len, so it has the same fixed-budget structure.
   Its accuracy did NOT collapse at long sequences at adequate width -- 0.992 at
   d=48, seq_len=384 -- so it is not obviously starved. But if attention turns out
   to have been starved, the honest follow-up is to check the local rule at a
   longer budget too, and that is recorded here as owed.

Most likely to be wrong: (2)'s endpoint. d=8 may climb and plateau below 0.9,
which would mean the crossing genuinely grows and g1-11 stands. That is the
outcome I would prefer and therefore the one to hold most loosely.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- d=8 at 384 reaches 0.9    -> attention was STARVED. g1-11's memory ratios are
                               inflated, must be recomputed, and the width ratio
                               becomes the more reliable of the two numbers
- d=8 plateaus below 0.9    -> attention is genuinely capacity-limited at long
                               sequences. g1-11 stands as written
- d=8 still climbing at
  12000                     -> no plateau demonstrated; report a bound, not a
                               number, and say the budget was insufficient
