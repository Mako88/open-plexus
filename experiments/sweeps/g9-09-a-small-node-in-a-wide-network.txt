Does the gate get BETTER as the node gets smaller?
==================================================

Script: experiments/g9_05_the_tag.py, with --partitions swept
Status: RUN. 15 of 15 cells, three refused, node width chosen INTERIOR.
Run:    https://github.com/Mako88/open-plexus/actions/runs/30244075029

ANSWER: no, it does not get better as the node shrinks -- it degrades
        gracefully and then stops. But the FLATNESS survives at every node
        size, which is the property the tag exists for, and the smallest node
        that can run the task at all is EIGHT dimensions.

THE WINDOW'S CATASTROPHE IS A WIDE-NODE PHENOMENON
---------------------------------------------------
    node     8      16      32      64
    d20  -0.10   -0.23   -0.56   -1.62

Monotone across four sizes, and **backwards from what was predicted**. An
unmatched window is most destructive on the WIDEST node, not the narrowest. The
reading that fits: a wider readout leans harder on what the store holds, so
keeping the wrong nine writes costs more, not less. At node 8 it is -0.10 and
merely bad.

That is a four-point relationship rather than the single -1.62 cell g9-08
produced, and it turns that observation into something with a shape.


PREDICTIONS, SCORED
--------------------
1. **SHAPE CONFIRMED, LEVEL REFUTED, and the reason was registered.** Predicted
   recovery rises then falls, above 0.25 from 2 to 8 groups, peaking between 2
   and 4. It does rise then fall and does peak at 2 groups (node 32) -- but at
   +0.21, and it is never above 0.25 anywhere.

   The control implied 0.16/0.42/0.35/0.28/0.43 and the sweep gives
   0.18/0.21/0.16/0.11. **The control was one seed with reduced training and its
   floor arm ran 0.894 down to 0.406; the sweep's runs 0.648 down to 0.116.** The
   denominator is what differed, which is exactly what "most likely wrong" named
   before dispatch: *a ratio whose denominator is what changed is the shape that
   has already cost this project a result twice*.

2. **REFUTED, and the guard that replaced it worked.** Predicted
   `tools/recovery.py` would NOT refuse the node-4 cells, since its refusals are
   about the floor and the seed spread rather than the ceiling, and that the
   summary would print a number that should not be used. In fact the FLOOR broke

   refused on its own terms. The CEILING BROKEN flag added for this fired anyway
   and is visible beside all three cells, which is the outcome that mattered.

3. **CONFIRMED, strongly, and it is the finding.** Spread under 0.10 at every
   usable node size; under 0.04 at all four.

4. **REFUTED, exactly backwards.** Predicted the window's delay-20 collapse gets
   worse as the node shrinks, below -0.5 at 8 groups. It is -0.10 at node 8 and
   -1.62 at node 64.

5. **REFUTED.** Predicted `tag` beats `tag-strongest` by MORE as the node
   shrinks, above +0.10 at 8 groups and near zero at 1. The gap runs +0.05,
   +0.03, +0.02, +0.02 from node 64 down to 8 -- it SHRINKS, and never reaches

   The reasoning was named as the reason it might fail: *it treats "starved by
   capacity" and "starved by width" as the same kind of scarcity, and they are
   not obviously the same thing*. They are not. The direction's value in g9-06
   was spread across all four delays at a starved capacity; here it is
   concentrated almost entirely at delay 20 and is near zero at delays 1 and 8.

Two refuted outright, one refuted backwards, one confirmed strongly, one split.
The two whose reasoning was pre-registered as suspect are the two that failed.

WHAT THIS SETTLES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
----------------------------------------
**Settled: the tag is not better on small nodes.** The best available outcome
named in WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT does not happen. What happens is the second one:
recovery is roughly flat from node 64 to 16 and then declines, which is "the gate
is largely indifferent to node size" over a 4x range and "it degrades" below
that.

**Settled: eight dimensions.** The smallest node that can answer this task at
all, with the gate recovering +0.11 there and the flatness intact.

**Not settled: whether the working point is right for a small node.** `slots` 32
and `fade` 0.95 were frozen at values chosen for `d_model` 32 in one process, and
that was named as this grid's standing risk before dispatch. Every cell here may
understate a tag tuned for its own node size, and the decline from node 16 to 8
is exactly where a mistuned capacity would show up first.

**Not settled: anything about a real network.** This is one process with a split
readout. The testbed exists and has never run a gated model.


IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
The last attempt at this question shrank the whole network and found that a
four-dimensional network cannot do the task -- which is true and is not what was
being asked. A small device in a big network is a different thing entirely: the
network stays wide, it is cut into pieces, and each piece answers using only its
own share.

So this cuts a 64-wide network into 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 pieces and asks a single
piece to answer alone. The pieces get down to four numbers each, which is the
size of device this project is actually for.

The early sign is the opposite of what was feared. A smaller piece is a weaker
reader, and a weaker reader is hurt MORE by remembering junk -- so filtering
matters more, not less, as the device shrinks.

> **That paragraph was written before the run and is WRONG.** It is kept because
> it is what the pre-dispatch control implied and because the reasoning in it is
> the reasoning the sweep tested. Filtering does not matter more as the device
> shrinks: the benefit peaks at half a network, then declines, and below eight
> numbers the device cannot do the task at all. What DOES survive all the way
> down is the thing the filter was built for -- it works equally well whether the
> important thing happened four steps ago or forty, at every size measured.


WHY THIS RUN EXISTS
--------------------
[g9-08](g9-08-how-small-a-node-can-run-the-gate.txt) swept `d_model` and refused
nine of fifteen cells: below width 32 the ungated model scores below chance, so
the task was impossible rather than the gate failing. **A narrow NETWORK is not
a small NODE.**

[g7-02](g7-02-tiny-nodes-and-clusters.txt) had it right and this copies it:
`partitions` cuts `d_model` into groups, each carrying its own readout over its
own dimensions, and `run(partition=g)` asks group `g` alone. Training is
unchanged -- the delta rule reads every group's own error whatever `partition`
says -- so this is the DEPLOYMENT question, not a different training regime.

[Note 024](../../docs/notes/024-what-the-gate-costs-a-tiny-node.md) keeps the two
quantities apart and always did: it costs a node's slice `w` against a network
width `d`, and its crossover is `w * d`. This sweep varies `w` with `d` fixed,
which is what that note was about.

question: below a node of about eight numbers there is no ceiling left to recover
a fraction of.

PREDICTIONS
------------
Predictions 1 and 2 are readings of the control above and count as weak. **3, 4
and 5 are not in it** -- the control ran one delay, and neither flatness nor the
window nor the direction arm can be read off a single column.

1. **Recovery RISES as the node shrinks, then falls.** Guess: above 0.25 at
   every partition count from 2 to 8, peaking between 2 and 4, against 0.16 at
   1 partition. The reason is mechanical rather than hopeful: a narrower reader
   is hurt more by interference, so the floor arm falls faster than the gated
   arms and the same gate recovers a larger share of a larger gap.

2. **The 16-group cells are reported but not believed.** The oracle is 0.608
   there, so the ceiling is broken and the ratio is a fraction of an advantage
   that is itself failing. Predict `tools/recovery.py` does NOT refuse them --
   its refusals are about the floor arm and the seed spread, not the ceiling --
   which means the summary will print a number that should not be used. Naming
   it here is the only thing that stops it being quoted later.

3. **The tag stays flat across delay at every partition count.** Spread under
   0.10 wherever the cell is usable. Flatness is a property of counting in
   bindings, and splitting the readout does not change what is counted. This is
   the same prediction g9-08 confirmed at two widths, now asked where it might
   actually break.

4. **The window's delay-20 collapse gets WORSE as the node shrinks.** It is
   -0.24 at `d_model` 32 and -1.62 at 64. If an unmatched window's damage scales
   with how much the reader depends on what it kept, a narrower reader should
   suffer more. Guess: below -0.5 at 8 groups.

5. **`tag` beats `tag-strongest` by more as the node shrinks.** THE INTERESTING
   ONE. The direction was worth +0.003 at g9-06's working point, +0.22 at a
   starved capacity, and +0.22 at `d_model` 64 delay 20. A small node is a
   fourth kind of scarcity, so the same shape should appear. Guess: above +0.10
   at 8 groups and near zero at 1.

Most likely wrong: **1**, and specifically its ceiling. The control is one seed
with reduced training, and the arm that moved most between settings is the FLOOR
arm -- which is the denominator. A ratio whose denominator is what changed is
the exact shape that has already cost this project a result twice.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- recovery rises as the node shrinks  -> the mechanism is BETTER on the hardware
                                         this project is for than on the
                                         hardware it was measured on. The best
                                         outcome available and the one to check
                                         hardest
- recovery flat in node size          -> the gate is indifferent to node size,
                                         which is g7-02's oracle result holding
                                         for an implementable gate
- recovery falls                      -> it works and it is not for us, and the
                                         tiny-node story needs a different
                                         mechanism
- floor arm collapses at some split   -> the task stops being answerable by one
                                         node, and the honest report is where
                                         that happens rather than a ratio


COST
-----
15 jobs of (partitions, delay). Five arms x three seeds x one rate = 15 cells a
job. Same shape as g9-08, which ran comfortably inside 90 minutes; the wider
network makes each cell about four times a `d_model`-32 cell, so call it 10
minutes a job.


THE ORACLE, SAID TWICE
-----------------------
The `oracle` arm reads `position_kinds()`. Every recovery figure is a fraction of
an advantage no deployed system has -- and at 16 groups that advantage is itself
only 0.608, which is why prediction 2 exists.
