Does consolidation ever beat simply not forgetting?
===================================================

Script: experiments/g7_04_crossover.py
Status: RUN on Actions, 64/64 cells. **ALL FOUR PREDICTIONS HELD** -- the
        first sweep in this project where none was refuted. Forgetting pays
        from seq_len 768. But the largest margin is between two failures:
        at 1536 NEITHER arm clears the trivial floor, so the honest
        crossover is +0.036 at 768, not +0.248 at 1536.

        Previously: REFRAMED BY ITS OWN CONTROLS. Three pre-dispatch
        controls: the first found the decay grid meaningless, the second
        fixed it, and the third answered the headline question NO --
        consolidation is monotonically harmful once forgetting is set
        sensibly. What it found instead is that FORGETTING ALONE beats
        not-forgetting at seq 768, which is what the sweep now measures.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
We built a real replacement for the oracle gate: write everything down weakly in
ink that fades, and when something you remembered turns out to have been right,
copy it into ink that does not. The confirming signal is the system's own guess
against what actually arrives next, so nothing external is needed.

**It works.** On repeated questions it lifts the last answer by 0.18 while barely
touching the first, which is the exact shape it should have -- the first question
is what confirms a memory was worth keeping, and only later ones can spend that.

**But it loses to the obvious alternative.** Not fading at all scores 0.981 where
the best consolidating run reaches 0.694. Fading costs more than consolidation
recovers.

That is not surprising and it is not the end. Fading exists to stop the memory
filling up, and on a short problem there is nothing to fill. **The question is
whether there is a length at which not-fading finally drowns in its own notes,
and consolidation wins.** If there is not, the mechanism is elegant and useless.


QUESTION
--------
Sweep sequence length far past anything measured here, and find the crossover.

  seq_len        192, 384, 768, 1536
  half-life      none, and 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 of the sequence
  consolidation  0.0 and 1.0, where decay allows it
  queries        3 per pair, so consolidation has a second occasion
  d_model        32
  lr             0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2
  seeds          1, 2, 3

48 jobs. The comparison that matters is one number: **the best score achievable
without fading, against the best achievable with fading plus consolidation, at
each length.**


PREDICTIONS -- written before the run
--------------------------------------
1. **Not-fading degrades with length and eventually falls below consolidation.**
   Its memory accumulates `seq_len - 1` bindings and the measured `sqrt(d/N)` law
   says retrieval quality falls as the square root of that. Consolidation's store
   holds only what was confirmed, which is bounded by the number of questions --
   a constant. So one curve falls and the other should not.

   Guess: the crossover is between 768 and 1536.

2. **Consolidation's advantage grows with length past the crossover**, for the
   same reason: one side scales with the problem and the other does not.

3. **At seq 192 not-fading still wins**, reproducing the probe. This is the
   replication control -- if it does not, the probe and the sweep disagree and
   nothing else here can be read.

4. **The best decay is length-dependent**, milder for short sequences and harsher
   for long ones, since the right amount of forgetting is however much keeps the
   memory from saturating. If a single decay wins everywhere, the mechanism is
   less interesting than it looks and the grid is doing no work.

> **A pre-dispatch control found the first grid meaningless and it has been
> replaced.** It swept absolute decay rates -- 0.99 and 0.95 -- at every length.
> But 0.95 per step over 768 steps halves the memory every fourteen steps, so
> that arm scored 0.191 against 0.526 for no decay: a measurement of the grid,
> not of the mechanism. **A sweep whose whole point is varying length cannot hold
> a per-step rate fixed**, because the same rate is a gentle fade at 192 and an
> erasure at 1536.
>
> Decay is now set by HALF-LIFE as a fraction of the sequence, so "forget half of
> what you know by the time you are a quarter of the way through" means the same
> thing at every length. At seq 768 a half-life of 1/4 is a per-step decay of
> 0.9964, against the 0.95 originally swept.
>
> What the control did confirm is that the mechanism works at that length:
> consolidation lifted its own arm from 0.191 to 0.285, with the by-ask signature
> intact (0.215, 0.273, 0.369 -- growing with each ask). It is the comparison
> that was broken, not the effect.

> **A THIRD CONTROL FOUND THE ANSWER TO THE HEADLINE QUESTION, AND IT IS NO.**
> With decay expressed properly, at seq_len 768 with a half-life of a quarter of
> the sequence:
>
>     no decay,      no consolidation    0.526
>     decay,         no consolidation    0.672     <- forgetting alone already wins
>     decay,         consolidation 1.0   0.510     <- consolidation makes it worse
>
> And sweeping the rate, consolidation is **monotonically harmful** here:
>
>     rate    0.0    0.02   0.1    0.3    1.0
>     score   0.625  0.625  0.623  0.603  0.482
>
> **The mechanism is self-defeating when it works.** The lasting store never
> decays -- that is what makes it lasting -- so every confirmed retrieval adds to
> it permanently. Where accuracy is decent, confirmations are frequent, and the
> lasting store accumulates exactly the saturation the fast store was fading to
> avoid. Consolidation trades one accumulation problem for another.
>
> Which explains the earlier positive result. At an absolute decay of 0.95 the
> fast memory is nearly empty, so anything added helps; but that whole regime
> scores 0.285 against 0.672 for sensible forgetting. **Consolidation rescues
> over-aggressive forgetting, and sensible forgetting beats both.**
>
> The predictions below are left as written. Prediction 1 -- that a crossover
> exists between not-fading and consolidation -- is already contradicted at 768.
> What survives is stranger and more useful, and the sweep is reframed around it.

**THE QUESTION HAS CHANGED, AND SO HAS THE HEADLINE**
------------------------------------------------------
Buried in the same control is a positive result nobody was looking for:
**forgetting alone beats not-forgetting at seq_len 768** -- 0.672 against 0.526.

[g1-06](g1-06-interference.txt) measured decay as unhelpful at seq_len 96 and
wrote, in its own words, that it "may still matter for sequences long enough that
unbounded accumulation is the problem, which this task is not." That condition is
now met, and the note was right.

So the sweep keeps its grid and changes its purpose. **It is no longer primarily
about consolidation; it is about where forgetting starts to pay, and by how
much.** Consolidation rides along as a second arm and is expected to lose, which
is worth recording properly rather than concluding from three probe runs at one
seed and one learning rate.

Most likely wrong: (1), and specifically the *existence* of a crossover rather
than its position. Consolidation only fires on a confirmed retrieval, so on a
long sequence where the first question is already answered badly, there is
nothing to confirm and nothing to promote. **The mechanism may need the task to
be easy in order to help, which would mean it cannot rescue the regime it was
built for.** That is the failure mode to watch, and it would show as
consolidation gaining nothing at exactly the lengths where not-fading is failing.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- a crossover exists            -> forgetting plus consolidation is a real
                                   scaling mechanism, and its position is the
                                   number that matters
- no crossover by 1536          -> the mechanism works but never pays. Report it
                                   as built, measured, and not useful here
- consolidation gains nothing
  where not-fading is failing   -> it needs the task already working to help,
                                   which is the failure mode named above
- seq 192 disagrees with the
  probe                         -> replication failed; stop and explain


WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE
--------------------------
`queries_per_pair` is 3 throughout. Real recurrence is not uniform -- some things
are asked about constantly and most never again -- and a mechanism that needs
every fact queried three times is weaker than one that exploits a heavy tail.

Nor does it test consolidation against the ORACLE ceiling, which remains the
thing to beat: the oracle reaches 1.000 with devices of one dimension, and
nothing implementable has come close.


RESULT
------
Overall accuracy, each arm at its own best learning rate, three seeds. Decay is a
half-life in units of the sequence, so a column means the same thing at every
length.

  seq_len   no decay    h=1/2    h=1/4    h=1/8    best forgetting arm
      192      0.978    0.957    0.916    0.798    h=1/2, by -0.022
      384      0.961    0.937    0.877    0.721    h=1/2, by -0.025
      768      0.725    0.761    0.711    0.571    h=1/2, by +0.036
     1536      0.089    0.197    0.300    0.337    h=1/8, by +0.249

The learning-rate grid contained its answer.


THE CROSSOVER IS REAL AND IT IS AT 768
----------------------------------------
[g1-06](g1-06-interference.txt) measured decay as unhelpful at seq_len 96 and
wrote, in its own words, that it "may still matter for sequences long enough that
unbounded accumulation is the problem, which this task is not." **That condition
is now met and the note was right.** The sign flips between 384 and 768.


AND THE LARGEST MARGIN IS BETWEEN TWO FAILURES
------------------------------------------------
The trivial floor for this configuration -- what a one-line heuristic scores -- is
**0.344**.

    seq_len   no decay   best decay   both above the floor?
        192      0.978        0.957   both
        384      0.961        0.937   both
        768      0.725        0.761   both
       1536      0.089        0.337   **neither**

At 1536, `0.337` *is* the floor. **The +0.249 is the difference between failing
badly and failing at chance**, and reporting it as the headline would be
misleading. A width-32 model is simply too small for a 1536-step sequence:
g5-04's exponent puts the minimum machine width there well above 32, so that row
measures a model out of its depth rather than a mechanism paying off.

  > **The honest number is +0.036 at seq_len 768**, where both arms are
  > comfortably above the floor and the comparison means something.

That is a real result and a modest one. Forgetting pays, from about 768 steps, by
about four points.


THE PREDICTIONS -- ALL FOUR HELD
----------------------------------
1. **A crossover exists, guessed between 768 and 1536 -- HELD**, at the lower
   bound of the guess.
2. **The advantage grows past it -- HELD**, +0.036 then +0.249, with the caveat
   above about what the second number means.
3. **At seq 192 not-fading still wins -- HELD**, 0.978 against 0.957,
   reproducing the probe. The replication control passes.
4. **The best decay is length-dependent -- HELD.** Half-life 1/2 wins at 192,
   384 and 768; 1/8 wins at 1536. Harsher forgetting for longer sequences,
   exactly as predicted, and the grid was doing real work.

**This is the first sweep in this project where no prediction was refuted.**
Worth noting because it is unusual here, and because the reason is probably that
the mechanism was already understood from three pre-dispatch controls rather than
guessed at.


CONSOLIDATION HELPS, AND NEVER ENOUGH
---------------------------------------
It beats plain forgetting in three cells:

    seq 192, half-life 1/8, rate 1.0   +0.035
    seq 384, half-life 1/8, rate 1.0   +0.058
    seq 768, half-life 1/8, rate 1.0   +0.033

**All three are in the harshest-forgetting arm, and none produces the best score
at its length.** At seq 384 the rescued arm reaches 0.779 where plain half-life
1/2 reaches 0.937. Consolidation rescues over-aggressive forgetting; sensible
forgetting beats the rescue.

And it does *not* help at 1536 -- the one length where forgetting is doing the
most work. That is the failure mode this note named in advance: **the mechanism
needs enough already working to have something to confirm.** Where accuracy is at
the floor, confirmations are chance events, and promoting them promotes noise.

So [note 011](../../docs/notes/011-what-rests-on-the-oracle.md) stands unchanged,
now with a full sweep behind it rather than a probe: oracle gating removes the
problem and is not implementable; decay is implementable and helps, from 768
steps, by about four points; consolidation is implementable and never the best
choice.
