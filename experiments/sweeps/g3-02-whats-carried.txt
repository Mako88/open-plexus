What survives ablation? -- and the answer nobody was looking for
================================================================

Script: experiments/g3_02_whats_carried.py
Status: RUN (single seed, local smoke test); full sweep dispatched to Actions.
        The answer is NEITHER candidate. It is the initialisation scale, and it
        puts the headline number of this project back in doubt.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
A puzzle had built up over three experiments. A small model trained normally
scores 0.26. An identically-sized model that got there by having half of a bigger
model removed scores 0.96. Same size, same data, same everything -- except how it
was born.

We had two theories and ran the experiment that separates them. Both were wrong.

The answer turned out to be something neither theory was looking at: **how big
the random numbers were when the model was created.** Shrink them by 30% and a
"too small" model becomes a working one.

Which is bad news for the headline. The number this project has been reporting --
that running on ordinary machines costs four to six times more room -- came from a
curve measured with those random numbers at a size we picked and never checked.
It was measuring our setting, not the method.


QUESTION
--------
  from scratch at width 32, any budget   0.18-0.26   (g1-06, g1-07)
  ablated DOWN to width 32               0.924       (g3-01)

g1-07 ruled out convergence: from-scratch is flat from epoch 1 to epoch 64. Two
candidates remained, separated by one condition.

  native    a real width-32 model. The baseline.
  cold      d=64 ablated to 32 BEFORE any training. Has the ablated model\'s
            initialisation scale and the from-scratch model\'s cold readout.
  warm      d=64 ablated halfway. Reproduces g3-01.
  rescaled  a native width-32 model with its projections multiplied by
            sqrt(32/64) = 0.71, to pin the mechanism to SCALE specifically rather
            than to anything else about having been born wide.


RESULT (one seed; full sweep dispatched)
-----------------------------------------
condition                        key norm    accuracy
native   (width 32 born)            0.986       0.263
cold     (64 ablated at init)       0.700       0.963
warm     (64 ablated midway)        0.700       0.965
rescaled (width 32, keys x0.71)     0.698       0.960

**`rescaled` is a native width-32 model.** Nothing was ablated, nothing
pre-trained, its readout starts at zero. The only difference from `native` is
that its frozen random projections were multiplied by 0.71.

0.263 -> 0.960.


BOTH CANDIDATES REFUTED
------------------------
(a) was named "initialisation scale" and (b) "the trained readout". The result is
(a), and more completely than (a) was stated: `cold` and `warm` are
indistinguishable, so the four epochs of pre-ablation training contribute nothing
at all. The readout carries nothing. Ablation is irrelevant. The entire effect is
the scale of the frozen projections.


WHAT THIS BREAKS
----------------
The projections are drawn with standard deviation 1/sqrt(d_model), so a native
model has key norm ~1.0 at EVERY width. That was chosen to keep keys unit-norm
and it does exactly that. What it does not do is keep the LEARNING
well-conditioned: the delta rule runs at a fixed rate against a retrieved vector
whose magnitude goes as the cube of the key scale, so norm-1.0 keys with lr=0.05
are badly tuned together, and norm-0.7 keys are much better.

**The width curve in g1-05 and g1-06 is therefore substantially a curve about an
initialisation choice, not about how much room the mechanism needs.** The 4-6x
price of locality is back in doubt and must not be quoted.

g1-07 predicted this reading in advance and named the shape of the error:
"It is the initialisation, and the width curve is partly an artifact of how the
projections are scaled -- which would put the 4-6x number back in doubt from a
completely different direction."


WHAT HAS TO HAPPEN NEXT
------------------------
Re-run the width sweep with the scale handled properly. Two ways, answering
slightly different questions:

  1. Sweep width x key-scale and report the crossing at the BEST scale for each
     width. The honest answer to "how much room does the mechanism need", because
     it stops the answer depending on a hyperparameter nobody tuned.
  2. Or normalise the keys explicitly and remove the degree of freedom entirely.
     Cleaner, but it changes the model rather than the experiment.

(1) first, because it measures what is there rather than altering it. And the
same question then has to be asked of the ATTENTION baseline, which had its own
untuned initialisation -- otherwise the comparison is repaired on one side only,
which would be a worse error than the one being corrected.


WHAT SURVIVES
-------------
G2 and G3 are untouched. Bit-identical delivery below the bound is a property of
emission-time indexing and involves no scale at all. Churn tolerance was measured
as a fraction of a working model and remains one -- though the widths it was
measured at will need restating once the curve is corrected.

What is damaged is one number: the price of locality. It has now been in doubt,
resolved, and put back in doubt across three experiments, and that sequence is
left visible in all three sweep notes rather than tidied away.
