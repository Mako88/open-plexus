Does a gate work when the relevance signal is real?
===================================================

Script: experiments/g9_02_reward_gate.py  (NOT WRITTEN)
Status: RUN TWICE, 12/12 each. **The first mechanism to recover anything,
        and the delay is a CLIFF.** With the reach fixed at 8: recovery 0.21,
        0.20, 0.23 at delays 1, 4 and 8, and **-0.13 at delay 20** -- past its
        reach the gate is not useless but actively harmful. on-use and
        salience sit at 0.00 in every cell of both runs.

        The first run set the window to `task.delay`, which told the gate how
        far back to look and made the delay axis measure nothing. Both runs
        are recorded; the correction is at the end.

        Predictions were written before the mechanism existed.


WHY THIS IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS
---------------------------------
Five mechanism-configurations have now failed to recover the oracle: consolidate
on confirmed use, consolidate on surprise, both again under Zipfian filler, and
competitive capture at two pool sizes. Every one of them tried to work out, **from
the statistics of the stream alone**, which of its contents would matter later.

[Note 016](../../docs/notes/016-who-supplies-relevance.md) argues that is a
harder question than biology solves. Neuromodulatory signals are not derived by
cortex from its inputs; they arrive from elsewhere, driven by outcomes the
organism cares about. **The cortex does not infer what mattered. It is told.**

MQAR cannot test that, because a sequence of random symbols contains nothing that
is good or bad for anything. `openplexus/tasks/reward_recall.py` can: a reward
token arrives **in the stream**, **after** the binding it refers to, and only
rewarded bindings are ever queried.

So this asks the question the last five sweeps could not: **not whether a memory
can infer relevance, but whether it can use relevance when relevance is
actually present.**


THE ARM THAT DOES NOT EXIST YET
--------------------------------
`reward` — consolidate the binding that preceded a reward token.

It is not the oracle. The oracle reads `position_kinds()`, a property of the
generator. This reads a token, from the same input stream every node already
receives, using the same broadcast that carries everything else. A deployed agent
has exactly this and does not have the other.

**What makes it non-trivial is the delay.** At delay 1 the marker is adjacent and
the rule degenerates to "keep the previous step", which learns nothing about
value. At delay 8 or 20 the storage decision cannot wait for the marker — the
binding is long past by the time the reward arrives — and something has to have
been *tagged* in the meantime. **That is tagging and capture in the form the paper
describes, and this is the first task here that can pose it.**


THE ARMS
--------
  none        store everything, fade by decay                  the FLOOR
  oracle      store only rewarded bindings, from position_kinds the CEILING
  on-use      consolidate confirmed retrievals                 failed on MQAR
  salience    consolidate surprising steps                     failed on MQAR
  reward      consolidate what preceded a reward token         NEW

`on-use` and `salience` are carried over unchanged so that this grid contains the
mechanisms it is trying to beat, rather than a description of them.


THE GRID
--------
  delay          1, 4, 8, 20      the dial this task exists for
  reward rate    4/24, 12/24      the base rate note 013 blamed, as a dial
  lr             0.02, 0.05, 0.1
  seeds          1, 2, 3

Configuration is the verified one from `experiments/g9_01_answerable.py`:
24 pairs, 64 cues, 8 values, seq_len 768, d_model 32 — where an ungated model
sits AT the trivial floor on first asks (0.119 against 0.125) and the oracle
reaches 1.000. **The first configuration tried had a gap of 0.000 and would have
made every arm read 1.000**; that failure is recorded in note 017.

Reported quantity is recovery, `(arm - none) / (oracle - none)`, refusing to
divide when the oracle's advantage is not larger than the seed spread — and
reported **separately for first asks and repeats**, because in autoregressive
mode the first query of a cue re-binds it, so a repeat measures short-term echo
rather than retention. At one configuration that difference was 0.369 against
0.225.


PREDICTIONS -- written before the mechanism exists
---------------------------------------------------
1. **`reward` recovers substantially more than any stream-derived gate.** Over
   0.5 at delay 1, and above `on-use` and `salience` at every delay. This is the
   hypothesis. If a gate cannot work when the signal is handed to it in the
   input, the problem is not the signal and note 016 is wrong.

2. **`reward` recovery FALLS as delay grows**, and this is the interesting part
   rather than a caveat. At delay 1 the rule is "keep the previous step" and
   should be near the ceiling. At delay 20 the binding is long gone when the
   marker arrives, so recovery should drop sharply — **and the size of that drop
   is the measurement**, because it is exactly the gap that tagging and capture
   exists to close. Guess: over 0.5 at delay 1, under 0.2 at delay 20.

3. **`on-use` and `salience` are near zero at every delay**, reproducing five
   previous results on a different task. If they suddenly work here, something
   about this task is easier than intended and the answerability check missed it.

4. **Raising the reward rate helps every arm and helps `none` most.** More
   rewarded bindings means more queries answerable from recent memory. This is
   the trap: raw accuracy will rise for everything, which is why the reported
   quantity is a ratio. Same trap as g8-02's prediction 3, which was refuted
   backwards, so it is stated with less confidence than the others.

Most likely wrong: (1). Every prediction of the form "this mechanism will work"
made in this project so far has been refuted.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- reward high at delay 1, falling     -> the signal works and the LATENESS is the
                                         unsolved part. Tagging and capture is
                                         the right target and now has a
                                         measurable gap to close
- reward high at every delay          -> suspect the task. A marker 20 steps
                                         later should not be free, and if it is,
                                         something is leaking
- reward near zero everywhere         -> the gate cannot use relevance even when
                                         handed it. That is a finding about the
                                         ARCHITECTURE rather than about the
                                         signal, and it is much worse news than
                                         five failed inference mechanisms
- on-use or salience suddenly works   -> the task is easier than the
                                         answerability check established. Do not
                                         report anything until that is understood


WHAT THIS CANNOT SETTLE
------------------------
**Whether the reward signal is "the agent's own value system".** It is a token
supplied by a generator. Note 016 distinguishes an application flag from an
evaluative subsystem, and *this task does not tell them apart either* — it only
moves the signal from metadata into the input stream, which is a necessary step
and not the whole distinction.

**Anything about acting.** No policy, no choice, no cost for being wrong.

**Anything about language.** Still synthetic, still a small alphabet, and still
blocked behind the divergence in note 018.


RESULT
------
Recovery of the oracle's advantage, first asks, three seeds, at each delay's best
learning rate. Trivial floor 0.125.

  delay    none  oracle    gap  spread   on-use  salience   reward
      1   0.167   0.999  0.832   0.112    -0.00     -0.01     0.23
      4   0.175   0.999  0.824   0.100     0.00     -0.02     0.23
      8   0.168   0.999  0.831   0.094     0.00     -0.00     0.23
     20   0.163   0.999  0.836   0.088    -0.00     -0.01     0.19

**A gate has recovered something for the first time.** 0.19 to 0.23, against a
previous best of 0.05 across six mechanisms and two tasks.

And the reason to believe it is not the 0.23. It is that **on-use and salience
score 0.00 in the same cells** -- they reproduce their failure exactly, on a new
task, with the same code paths. A configuration in which everything suddenly
worked would be a configuration to distrust.


THE PREDICTIONS
----------------
1. **`reward` recovers substantially more than any stream-derived gate -- HELD in
   direction, REFUTED in magnitude.** It does beat them, by every margin the grid
   can resolve. The guess was "over 0.5 at delay 1"; it is 0.23.

2. **Recovery falls as delay grows -- UNTESTABLE, because of a flaw in the
   script.** See below. The measured curve is flat and that flatness is mine.

3. **`on-use` and `salience` near zero at every delay -- HELD**, to two decimals
   in all twelve cells.

4. **Reward rate helps every arm -- NOT TESTED.** The rate axis was dropped when
   the grid was costed, and dropping it was not recorded at the time. It should
   have been.


THE FLAW, WHICH IS THE SAME ERROR AS THE ORACLE
-------------------------------------------------
The script sets

    reward_window = task.delay

with the comment "the window reaches exactly as far back as the binding it must
keep". That is true, and it is the problem: **the gate was told how far back to
look.** At every delay the window was exactly the distance to the binding, so the
lateness that this whole mechanism exists to solve was removed by configuration.

`task.delay` is a property of the generator. A deployed node does not know how
long ago the thing that mattered happened -- **that is the entire difficulty**, and
it is what tagging and capture is a solution to. Passing it in is the same class
of error as `position_kinds()`, arriving through a parameter instead of a mask.

So the flat curve is not evidence that lateness is free. It is evidence that a
gate which is told the delay does not care what the delay is, which is
arithmetic.

**The pre-registration called this**: "reward high at every delay -> suspect the
task. A marker 20 steps later should not be free." The suspicion was right and
the cause was one line of my own script rather than the task.

What survives: at a *known* delay, a reward token recovers 0.19-0.23 where six
intrinsic mechanisms recover nothing. That is a real result about the SIGNAL. It
says nothing yet about whether a gate can handle a signal whose lag it does not
know.


THE CORRECTION
---------------
Re-run with the window FIXED across delays, so the gate has one reach and the
delay decides whether that reach is enough. A fixed window of 8 covers delays 1
and 4 comfortably, covers 8 exactly, and cannot reach 20.

That is the experiment prediction 2 was written for, and it is what the shape of
the curve was supposed to measure. Registered here before it runs.

Prediction, before the re-run: recovery falls with delay once the window is
fixed, and falls sharply past the window -- **above 0.2 at delays 1 and 4, and
near zero at 20.** If it stays flat even with a fixed window, then something else
is carrying the result and the reward token is not doing what this note claims.


THE RE-RUN, WITH THE REACH FIXED AT 8
=======================================
Same grid, one changed constant: `reward_window` is 8 at every delay rather than
`task.delay`. So the reach covers delays 1 and 4 comfortably, covers 8 exactly,
and cannot reach 20 -- and the delay decides whether the reach is enough, which
is what prediction 2 was written to measure.

  delay    none  oracle    gap  spread   on-use  salience   reward
      1   0.167   0.999  0.832   0.072    -0.00     -0.01     0.21
      4   0.175   0.999  0.824   0.091     0.00     -0.02     0.20
      8   0.168   0.999  0.831   0.094     0.00     -0.00     0.23
     20   0.163   0.999  0.836   0.088    -0.00     -0.01    -0.13

**Prediction 2, in its corrected form, HELD.** The registered guess before the
re-run was "above 0.2 at delays 1 and 4, and near zero at 20". Measured 0.21 and
0.20, and **-0.13** at 20 -- worse than near zero.

That negative is the interesting number. Past its reach the gate does not merely
fail to help: it **keeps the wrong nine steps and discards the right one**, so it
is worse than storing everything. A gate that cannot reach the thing it is
vouching for is not a weak gate, it is an anti-gate.


WHAT THE THREE RESULTS SAY TOGETHER
-------------------------------------
    six intrinsic mechanisms, present-tense signal     0.00
    reward gate, signal within reach                   0.20 - 0.23
    reward gate, signal beyond reach                  -0.13

The middle line is the first time anything in this project has recovered part of
the oracle. The third line says exactly what remains unsolved, and names it
precisely: **the gap between a signal arriving within a fixed reach and one
arriving after it.**

That gap is what tagging and capture is a solution to, in the form
[Lehr et al.](../../docs/notes/010-tagging-and-capture.md) describe it. A tag is
set at the moment of the binding, cheaply, and costs nothing to hold; the late
signal decides whether it is captured. **A fixed window is the crudest possible
substitute for a tag** -- it holds everything for a fixed time instead of marking
the one thing worth holding -- and the cliff at delay 20 is the price of that
crudeness, measured.

So the mechanism to build next is not another gate. It is the tag: a per-binding
marker that survives longer than the window and is resolved when a reward
arrives. That is a different data structure, not a different threshold.


WHAT IS STILL OWED
--------------------
**Prediction 4 was never tested.** The reward-rate axis was dropped when the grid
was costed and the drop was not recorded at the time. It should have been, and
the base rate remains a dial nobody has turned on this task.

**The magnitude is unexplained.** 0.2 is a real recovery and it is a fifth of the
oracle's advantage, not most of it. Where the other four fifths go -- filler kept
inside the window, bindings evicted by later rewards, or the readout simply not
learning from a smaller store -- is not measured, and the three candidates would
point at different next steps.
