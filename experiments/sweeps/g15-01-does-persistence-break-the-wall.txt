THE DIAGNOSIS, FROM THE ONE DIAGNOSTIC THAT WORKED
----------------------------------------------------
**The slow store's norm is 5.00 at every data size, including 4,000
characters.** `lasting_cap` is 5.0 and `scale_to` rescales the whole store to it.

So the persistent store **saturates almost immediately and stays saturated**. It
is a fixed-size bucket that is full before the smallest data point, and every
subsequent write rescales what is already there. More text cannot help a store
that cannot hold more, and the constant rescaling explains why it is worse than
no store at all: it is adding a fixed-norm blob of increasingly-overwritten
material to every read.

**This experiment did not test note 042's claim.** It tested a saturated store.
The claim is that a map needs somewhere to ACCUMULATE, and nothing accumulated.


WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SETTLE
------------------------------------
**Does not settle:** whether a persistent slow store breaks the wall. The
mechanism never got the chance.

**Does settle:** that `lasting_cap` is the binding constraint on persistence, and
that the consolidation gate is NOT the problem -- which was the registered risk
and is now eliminated. The gate fires tens of thousands of times.

**And it settles that three of these five predictions were badly specified**, all
in the same way: a statistic chosen for convenience rather than for what it would
detect. Total movement cannot see a wall; one pairwise difference cannot see a
trend. Both were written before the run, which is the point of registering them
-- and registering a bad test in advance is not the same as registering a good
one.

IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
The model stops getting better after about sixteen thousand characters of text,
no matter how much more it is shown. The claim being tested is that this happens
because it has nowhere to put what it learns: the part that holds relationships
is wiped clean every few hundred characters, and the only thing that survives is
a single flat table.

This gives it somewhere to put things and checks whether the wall moves. If it
does not, the explanation is wrong and a large piece of planned work is wrong
with it.


WHY THIS EXISTS
----------------
Note 042 argues that decision 62's finding -- `memory = np.zeros((d, d))` is
inside `run`, so nothing durable exists but `Wo` -- explains three separate

**One fact explaining three results is what makes it architectural.** It is also

THE ARMS
--------
    baseline      consolidation OFF, persistence OFF     decision 63's model
    consolidate   consolidation ON,  persistence OFF     the control that matters
    persist       consolidation ON,  persistence ON      the proposal

**The middle arm is why a positive result would be readable.** Without it,
`persist` beating `baseline` could be consolidation helping rather than
persistence -- two changes in one arm, the confound decision 79 was caught by.
The claim is specifically that the store must SURVIVE THE SEQUENCE.


THE RISK, REGISTERED BEFORE THE RUN RATHER THAN AFTER A NULL
--------------------------------------------------------------
Consolidation fires on `predictions[t-1] == token`. **It promotes what the model
already got right.** So a persistent store cannot bootstrap a model that predicts
badly, and a null here would have two completely different readings:

    the store did not help          persistence is the wrong idea
    the store stayed empty          the GATE is the problem, not the store

Those are the same number without a counter. `model.consolidations` was added
for this and P4 is the rail built on it. **This risk was written down before the
matrix ran** -- it is not a hedge composed after seeing a result.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
  P1  CONTROL. `baseline` reproduces decision 63 -- total movement from 4,000 to
      125,000 characters under 0.05 bits. If it does not, this instrument
      disagrees with the record and nothing else here is readable.

  P2  `consolidate` is also flat. Consolidation without persistence is a
      within-sequence mechanism, so more data cannot help it either.

  P3  THE GATE. `persist` keeps improving past 16,000 characters: its 62,500 to
      125,000 movement exceeds the 0.04 seed spread, where `baseline`'s does
      not.

  P4  RAIL. `consolidations` grows with characters seen and is well above zero
      at every point. If it is near zero, a refuted P3 says nothing about
      persistence.

  P5  `persist` does not reach the backprop baseline (4.049 at 1,000,000
      characters). A single linear readout plus a slow store is not a
      transformer, and a result at or past it would suggest a leak.
