What does use-based eviction throw away?
=========================================

Script: experiments/g25_01_dormant_facts.py  (to be written)
Status: FIRST RUN VOID -- P1, the control, failed. Predictions were registered
        and committed at 51b3b00 before the script existed and are unchanged.

ANSWER: pending. The dormant column is NOT reported, because the control that
makes it readable did not hold.


IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
The system cannot keep everything, so it throws things away. The rule it uses is
"discard whatever has gone longest without being asked about", and that rule
scored perfectly: every fact that keeps being queried survives, 100 out of 100.

But look at what that rule does to a fact **nobody has asked about yet**. It
throws it away first, by design.

For a system meant to learn things it will need LATER, that is the obvious way
for a good rule to be a bad one. And the existing measurement cannot see it,
because every fact in it gets queried at some point.

This adds the missing case: facts that are never asked about while the stream
runs, and asked about at the end.


WHY THIS EXISTS
----------------
`note 083` is C4's answer -- learning forever is reachable in fixed storage,
bounded in content and unbounded in time -- and it measured:

    policy                   persistent            abandoned
    random             0.717 (sd 0.045)     0.783 (sd 0.045)
    least-recent       1.000 (sd 0.000)     0.500 (sd 0.000)
    least-frequent     1.000 (sd 0.000)     0.500 (sd 0.000)

PERSISTENT is queried throughout; ABANDONED is queried during the first quarter.
**Both are queried.** `docs/options/use-based-eviction.md` names the gap in its
own words:

    A useful fact nobody asks about inside its window is gone before it can be
    promoted. Every fixture measuring this policy queries the facts it cares
    about, so none of them pays that cost.

**And the record names a second gap:** *"Recency and frequency are
indistinguishable here, because both are true of the same facts by
construction."* Two policies scoring 1.000 and 0.500 identically is not two
results, it is one result the instrument cannot split.

**There is also no script.** The option record's CONFIG says `script unrecorded`,
and nothing in `experiments/` or `tools/` reproduces it. A headline C4 result that
cannot be re-run is note 105's shape, and re-deriving it is half of what this
does.


THE ARMS
--------
    PERSISTENT   queried throughout                  note 083's arm
    ABANDONED    queried during the first quarter    note 083's arm
    DORMANT      NEVER queried during the stream,    NEW
                 asked about only at the end

    random           evict a uniformly random slot
    least-recent     evict the longest-unqueried
    least-frequent   evict the least-queried

Reproducing note 083's two arms is the control. **If PERSISTENT and ABANDONED do
not come back at 1.000 / 0.500 and 0.717 / 0.783, this harness is not that
harness** and the DORMANT column says nothing.

Splitting recency from frequency needs a case where they disagree, and DORMANT is
one: every dormant fact has been touched exactly once, at write, so recency
orders them by WRITE TIME while frequency ties them all at one.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
  P1  THE CONTROL. PERSISTENT and ABANDONED reproduce note 083 within 0.05:
      random 0.717 / 0.783, both content-aware policies 1.000 / 0.500.

      Refuted means the harness differs from the one that produced C4's answer,
      and nothing else here is readable. **This is the first time those numbers
      will have been re-run at all.**

  P2  THE TEST. On DORMANT, both content-aware policies score BELOW random.

      This is the cost the option record names and no fixture has paid. Refuted
      if they match or beat random, which would mean the policy's advantage is
      not bought at the expense of the unqueried.

  P3  On DORMANT, least-recent and least-frequent SEPARATE -- they differ by
      more than 0.05, where on the other two arms they are identical.

      Because a dormant fact is touched once at write: recency orders them by
      write time, frequency ties them all. **If they still do not separate, the
      instrument cannot tell the two policies apart at all and note 083's
      "indistinguishable" is a property of the measurement rather than of the
      policies.**

  P4  THE ONE I EXPECT TO BE UNCOMFORTABLE. DORMANT under least-frequent lands
      near ZERO, not merely below random.

      Every dormant fact has count 1 and every persistent fact has many, so the
      policy evicts the whole dormant class before touching a persistent one. If
      that is right, the rule this project calls C4's answer discards
      **everything it has not yet been asked about**, and "bounded in content,
      unbounded in time" needs the word USED in it.


WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS NOT COUNT
--------------------------------
**Slot arithmetic doing the work.** Note 083's 0.500 on abandoned is exactly
150 slots minus 100 persistent, over 100 abandoned. With three classes the
arithmetic changes, so the slot count and class sizes are reported beside every
number and the expected-by-arithmetic value is printed next to the measured one.

**Ties.** With frequency, the whole dormant class ties at one. How ties break is
then the entire result, so it is chosen explicitly -- oldest first -- and stated,
rather than falling out of dict order. `g23-02` is the calibration: an unsorted
set made a number move between runs of the same seed.

Pure Python over a bounded dict, 4,000 facts, no model and no training. Seconds.

-----------
A stream of 4,000 facts through 150 slots, 3 seeds, matching note 083's

**P1 is refuted, so nothing else here is readable** -- note 083 measures random at
0.717 / 0.783 and least-recent at 1.000 / 0.500, and this reproduces neither.
Recorded because a void run is a result about the harness and deleting it would

Instrumented, the persistent class decays monotonically: 100 held at index 149,
19 at 299, 1 at 999, 0 at 2000. **Once a fact is evicted, a query that misses does
not restore it**, so the population can only fall.

## And note 083's design cannot be reconstructed from note 083

Reading it properly is what settled this, and the note says two things that
matter:

    the fast store   a sharp recent window
    promotion        what proved right gets copied OUT of the window

**The 150 slots are a PROMOTED store, not the stream.** Only queried facts are
ever promoted, which is why filler never competes for a slot and why abandoned
settles at exactly 50 of 100 -- 150 slots minus the 100 persistent. This harness
wrote every streaming fact straight into the bounded store, which is a different
mechanism wearing the same name.

**So the DORMANT question has a structural answer rather than an empirical one,
and it is sharper than the horse race this file was designed around:** a fact
that is never queried is never promoted, so it never enters the durable store at
all. Not evicted from it -- never in it. That is note 082's cost, which note 083
names as *"unchanged and unpaid here"*, and it is a property of promotion gating
rather than of any eviction policy.

**P2 and P3 are therefore mis-specified**, not merely unmeasured: they compare
eviction policies on a class that eviction never sees. P4 was closer than it
deserved to be -- it predicted "near zero", and the reason is that the class is
absent rather than discarded.

**What the redesign has to be.** Two stores, promotion gated on use, and the
question becomes what a promotion gate costs rather than what an eviction policy
throws away. `LocalMemoryConfig.capture_slots` is the real mechanism and note 083
explicitly did NOT exercise it -- *"this reimplements the idea rather than
exercising that code"* -- so a rebuild should use it rather than write a third
fixture.
