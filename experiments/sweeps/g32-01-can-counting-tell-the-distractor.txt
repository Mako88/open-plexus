Can counting tell the distractor from the thing?
================================================

Script: experiments/g32_01_can_counting_tell_the_distractor.py
Status: PREDICTIONS COMMITTED, NOT YET RUN. Results appended after the run, and
        the git ordering is the evidence that these were written first.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
We want a machine to learn that a picture of a dog, a bark and the word "dog" are
one thing, purely by noticing they keep showing up together. The obvious way is to
count: whatever turns up with the picture most often must be what the picture is.

There is one thing that breaks it. Suppose something is in the room EVERY time —
a hum, a wall, the word "the". It turns up with the picture more often than the
bark does, because the bark is only there sometimes. Counting says the wall is
what the picture means.

This asks whether that actually happens, and whether the standard escapes work.
The escapes all amount to the same idea: don't ask how often two things met, ask
whether they met more often than they would have by chance. Something present all
the time meets everything by chance, so it should score nothing.

A no here is a real result and would kill the design as it stands: it would mean
watching is not enough and the missing ingredient is acting on the world rather
than observing it. A yes is smaller than it sounds — it says the arithmetic works
on one machine, and the whole reason this design exists is to work across many.


WHAT IS BEING MEASURED
----------------------
Streams of "occasions". Each occasion is a set of surfaces that co-occurred, with
no label saying which belong together. One concept is the subject; each of its
three surfaces shows up with probability `presence`; three surfaces belonging to
other concepts show up as noise; and `distractors` surfaces show up every time.

The mechanism counts co-occurrence at each surface, ranks each surface's partners
by one of four statistics, keeps an edge only where the ranking is MUTUAL, and
takes connected components. The component reached from a surface is its recovered
concept. That is the whole of "a concept is an equivalence class reached by
walking", with no store and no vectors.

    f1         mean over surfaces of F1 between the recovered class and the true
               one. Punishes a class that is too large as well as one too small
    captured   share of surfaces whose recovered class contains a distractor.
               The registered falsifier, scored directly
    largest    biggest recovered class as a share of all surfaces

ARMS, and all four are statistics over the same counts:

    count         how often they met. The mechanism exactly as designed
    weighted      c_xy / sqrt(c_y). Note 045's fix, which over-corrected on
                  Shakespeare
    conditional   P(x | y). How reliably the neighbour brings you
    ppmi          positive pointwise mutual information. Named as UNTRIED in
                  note 045 and untried since


MEASURED ON
-----------
Local, single process, Python 3.14, no numpy. Held constant in every cell, with
the provenance of each pin beside it:

    concepts       64      chosen for THIS grid. 192 surfaces against an occasion
                           of ~6. NOT inherited: test_occasions.ShuffledControl
                           found a small world hands out same-concept
                           co-occurrence by accident, which stops the shuffled
                           control being a floor
    surfaces        3      stands in for image, sound, word
    presence      0.7      below 1.0 on purpose; at 1.0 the distractor and the
                           true partner TIE by construction and no run is needed
    noise           3      surfaces from other concepts, present once
    occasions   8,000
    k               2      = surfaces - 1. The mechanism is TOLD the class size,
                           which is generous, so a failure is strong evidence
    seeds     0, 1, 2

Swept: `zipf` in {0.0, 1.0, 2.0}, `distractors` in {0, 1}, stream in
{real, shuffled}. 36 streams, four arms scored on each.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
P1  POSITIVE CONTROL, and nothing below is readable without it. In the easy world
    — zipf 0.0, no distractor — BOTH `count` and `ppmi` reach f1 above 0.80.
    REFUTED IF either fails there. That would say the instrument is unsolvable
    rather than the mechanism inadequate, and a low score in any other cell would
    mean nothing. This is the G0 shape applied to a task with no learning in it.

P2  THE FALSIFIER, DIRECT. Adding ONE distractor at zipf 0.0 costs `count` more
    than 0.30 of f1, and costs `ppmi` less than 0.05.
    REFUTED IF `count` survives the distractor. That refutation would be good
    news and would mean mutuality alone is a sufficient defence — plausible,
    because mutuality already caps a distractor's degree at k, and it is exactly
    what the unit-test world was too small to settle.

P3  THE ONE THE GRID EXISTS FOR. `ppmi`'s f1 FALLS as `zipf` rises, and falls by
    more than `conditional`'s does, because mutual information over-rewards rare
    co-occurrences and skew manufactures them.
    REFUTED IF `ppmi` is flat across zipf, or falls least of the four arms.

P4  `weighted` sits between `count` and `ppmi` at zipf 0.0 and OVER-CORRECTS at
    zipf 2.0 — reproducing on a designed stream what note 045 saw anecdotally on
    Shakespeare, where `1/sqrt(frequency)` sharpened `king` and destroyed
    `father`.
    REFUTED IF `weighted` tracks `ppmi` at every skew.

P5  CONTROL. On the shuffled stream every arm scores near zero f1 at every cell.
    REFUTED IF any arm scores appreciably there, which would mean the metric is
    reading frequency rather than structure and no number in this file means
    anything.

P6  METHOD, promoted from a unit test to a grid-wide claim. Wherever `count`
    loses to `ppmi` on f1, the f1 gap is LARGER than the `captured` gap — because
    mutuality caps a distractor's degree at k, so it displaces true partners far
    more widely than it joins their classes.
    REFUTED IF `captured` tracks `f1`. That would mean the registered falsifier's
    own metric is sufficient, and the caution in `score_classes` is wrong.


COST
----
Estimated before the run: under two minutes, single process. 36 streams of 8,000
occasions, each occasion about six surfaces, so roughly 5 million pair updates
in total plus four rankings over 193 surfaces per stream.

Recorded after the run: **12.7s wall**, one process, 36 streams. The estimate of
"under two minutes" was right in kind and 9x conservative. Local was correct.

**Costed rather than assumed, because "it is only counting" is how a control ends
up holding the machine for ten minutes** — CLAUDE.md's calibration on
`g8_05_which_advantage`. If the estimate is wrong by more than a few times, this
belongs in Actions and the estimate is the thing that was wrong.


RESULTS
-------
Status: RUN, local, 2026-07-31, 12.7s. Predictions committed at `9f0abd8` before
the script was executed.

ANSWER: **raw counting is refuted and normalising fixes it completely — but the
distractor was never the hard part. Frequency skew is, it costs every arm about
four times what the distractor costs counting, and it was registered nowhere.**

  f1, mean of 3 seeds. READ AGAINST 0.5, NOT AGAINST 0 -- see P5.

    real stream          count   weighted   conditional   ppmi
    zipf 0.0, no distr  1.0000     1.0000        1.0000  1.0000
    zipf 0.0, 1 distr   0.6956     1.0000        1.0000  1.0000
    zipf 1.0, no distr  0.8073     0.9969        1.0000  1.0000
    zipf 1.0, 1 distr   0.6163     0.8969        1.0000  1.0000
    zipf 2.0, no distr  0.5437     0.6208        0.6120  0.6120
    zipf 2.0, 1 distr   0.5212     0.5948        0.6120  0.6120

SCORECARD
---------
  P1  HELD. Easy world, all four arms at 1.0000. The instrument is solvable and
            every other number is readable.

  P2  HELD, and it is the falsifier answered. One distractor costs `count`
            0.3044 of f1 at zipf 0.0 (1.0000 -> 0.6956), just over the predicted
            0.30, and costs `ppmi` exactly 0.0000, under the predicted 0.05.
            **Mutuality alone is NOT a sufficient defence. Normalisation is, and
            all three normalised arms are perfect.**

  P3  REFUTED, and the refutation is arithmetic I should have done first.
            `ppmi` and `conditional` are IDENTICAL to four decimals in every one
            of the twelve real cells, so the prediction that one falls faster
            than the other could not have been true.

            For a fixed surface, `c_x` and `N` are constants, so PPMI's
            `log(c_xy*N/(c_x*c_y))` is monotone in `c_xy/c_y` -- which is
            `conditional`. **The two impose the same order on every pair that
            occurs more often than chance and differ only in that PPMI REFUSES
            the rest.** Verified directly: 0 of 40 above-chance rankings differ
            on a random index, 40 of 40 full rankings do.

            With k=2 and true partners far above chance, the difference never
            reaches the top of any list, so the arms produced the same classes
            everywhere. **Two of the four arms were one experiment**, which is
            rule 10's shape arriving inside a grid rather than inside a test: an
            outcome guaranteed by construction is not evidence however it comes
            out. A grid that probed BELOW chance -- small k, weak partners --
            would separate them, and nothing here does.

  P4  SPLIT, and honestly it is mostly refuted. `weighted` was predicted to sit
            between `count` and `ppmi` at zipf 0.0: it does not, it EQUALS `ppmi`
            at 1.0000, because that cell is saturated and cannot separate
            anything. The over-correction clause held: at zipf 1.0 it is between
            (0.8969 against 0.6163 and 1.0000) and at zipf 2.0 it falls BELOW
            `ppmi` (0.5948 against 0.6120) while staying above `count`. So note
            045's Shakespeare observation reproduces in direction on a designed
            stream, at a much smaller magnitude than the anecdote suggested.

  P5  REFUTED, AND IT IS THE MOST IMPORTANT LINE IN THIS FILE. The shuffled
            control does not score near zero. It scores 0.3189 to 0.5078.

            **The metric has a floor of exactly 0.5 and it is not a floor of
            zero.** A recovery that groups NOTHING -- every surface alone --
            scores precision 1.0 and recall 1/3 against a three-surface concept,
            which is f1 0.5. Confirmed directly rather than derived.

            So the reference points are:

                1.0000   perfect
                0.5000   recovered nothing at all, every surface alone
                ~0.35    the shuffled control, which is WORSE than nothing
                         because it groups surfaces that do not belong together

            **Every f1 in this file must be read against 0.5.** The zipf 2.0 row
            at 0.6120 is not "a moderate score", it is a fifth of the way from
            recovering nothing to recovering everything. Anything published off
            this metric without the floor stated would be badly overread, and
            the prediction was wrong about the instrument rather than the data.

  P6  HELD at every cell, and by one to two orders of magnitude. Where `count`
            loses to `ppmi`, the f1 gap is 0.3044 / 0.3837 / 0.0908 against
            `captured` gaps of 0.0174 / 0.0104 / 0.0156 -- a factor of 17x, 37x
            and 6x. **The registered falsifier's own metric would have reported
            the distractor as a rounding error while it was destroying a third
            of the recovery.** Mutuality caps the distractor's degree at k, so it
            almost never JOINS a class; what it does is DISPLACE, taking the top
            slot a true partner needed. Counting the joins measures the wrong
            thing.


WHAT THIS ACTUALLY SETTLES, AND WHAT IT MOVES TO
-------------------------------------------------
**The registered falsifier is answered and the answer is not the interesting
one.** Counting fails -- as both option records predicted -- and the failure is
completely repaired by dividing by the neighbour's own frequency. That repair is
reachable under amended C1: `c_xy` and `c_x` are already at `owner(x)`, and
`c_y` is one bounded message to one named peer, which the constraint permits
where a collective does not. So the design survives its own falsifier at the
cost of one hop, and **intervention is not required to separate a persistent
distractor.** The hypothesis that it might be is not supported by this run and is
not refuted either -- it was never tested here.

**What the run found instead was not registered anywhere.** At zipf 2.0 every
arm collapses to ~0.61 against a 0.5 floor, distractor or no distractor. Skew
costs about 0.39 where the distractor costs counting 0.30 and costs the
normalised arms nothing at all. That is the larger effect and the one nobody
predicted.

**And it has an obvious candidate explanation that this grid cannot separate.**
At zipf 2.0 over 64 concepts the weights are `1/i^2`, so the commonest concept
takes about 61% of 8,000 occasions and the rarest takes about one. A concept
seen once cannot be recovered by any statistic. **So the zipf axis may be
measuring stream length per concept rather than skew at all**, and the two are
confounded in every cell here.

That is the next question and it is a better one: **how many occasions does a
concept need before it can be recovered?** It replaces a confounded axis with a
number, it is minutes to run, and it bears directly on whether this mechanism
can work on a Zipfian world -- which every real one is, and where the tail never
gets its occasions.
