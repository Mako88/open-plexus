Do more modalities make a concept learnable from fewer occasions?
=================================================================

Script: experiments/g36_02_do_more_modalities_learn_faster.py
Status: RUN, local, 2026-08-01, 12.4s. Predictions committed at `6b3f429`
        before the script was executed.

ANSWER: **John's claim holds in direction, is modest in size, and has a hard
        condition.** More modalities is better at every stream length — 0.3542
        to 0.5677 at the sparsest — but it does NOT move the threshold on this
        grid. And under a FIXED bound it is catastrophic: 8 surfaces plateaus at
        **0.5527** and never learns, where 2 surfaces reaches **1.0000**. The
        derived bound from `g33-04` is what makes the claim true.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
John, 2026-08-01: *"inherently the more different types of inputs that can
co-occur, the more differentiation between things is possible, and the
quicker/better the model will be able to learn."*

There is a real reason that could be true. If a thing shows up as a picture, a
sound and a word all at once, that one moment gives you three facts — picture
goes with sound, picture goes with word, sound goes with word — where two kinds
of input would have given you one. Add a fourth and you get six. **The evidence
per moment grows much faster than the number of inputs does**, so a machine
should need fewer moments to be sure.

**And there is a measured reason it could go the other way**, which is why this
is being run rather than agreed with.

When we tested a concept shaped like a hub — one word in the middle, several
appearances that never show up alongside *each other* — adding more appearances
made it WORSE, not better. The machine keeps only a few best matches per thing,
and the word in the middle ran out of room to hold all of them. Bridging fell to
a sixth. Letting each thing decide its own number of matches fixed it.

So the intuition looks right, with a condition attached: **it holds only if the
machine adapts how many links it keeps.** This measures both, side by side, so
the condition is visible rather than assumed.


WHAT IS BEING MEASURED
----------------------
    connected   share of surfaces whose recovered class holds at least one TRUE
                partner. **Floor-free**, which is the whole reason it is used
                here: `f1`'s floor moves with the class size — 0.6667, 0.5000,
                0.3333, 0.2222 at 2, 3, 5 and 8 surfaces — so an f1 column read
                across THIS axis would be four scales printed as one. `g35-02`
                made that mistake and this is the metric that corrected it
    threshold   first occasions-per-concept reaching `connected` >= 0.95

**The bar is stated rather than eyeballed.** `g32-02` read its threshold off a
curve by inspection and called it "about 16"; a fixed bar makes the answer a
number that can be compared across rows.


MEASURED ON
-----------
Local, single process, Python 3.14, no numpy.

    concepts       64      carried from g32-01 onward
    presence      0.7      carried
    noise           3      carried
    distractors     1      carried
    statistic  conditional the C1-safe one
    seeds     0, 1, 2

Swept: `surfaces` per concept in {2, 3, 5, 8}, occasions per concept in
{2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64}, and bound in {fixed 2, derived}.

**Both bounds are run and that is the point**, not a robustness check: the fixed
bound is where `g33-02` measured more modalities HURTING, and showing the two
together is what makes the condition on John's claim legible.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
P1  UNDER THE DERIVED BOUND, MORE MODALITIES LEARNS FASTER. The threshold falls
    monotonically with `surfaces` — 8 surfaces reaches 0.95 at strictly fewer
    occasions per concept than 2 does.
    REFUTED IF the threshold is flat or rises. Flat would mean the extra
    pairwise evidence per occasion buys nothing, and the mechanism is limited by
    how often a CONCEPT appears rather than by how much each appearance says.

P2  THE EVIDENCE IS PAIRWISE, SO THE THRESHOLD SCALES LIKE 1/C(s,2). Two
    surfaces give one pair per occasion and eight give 28, so if a fixed amount
    of pairwise evidence is what is needed, the threshold at 8 should be roughly
    a twentieth of the threshold at 2 — bounded below by 1, since a concept
    cannot be learned from less than one occasion.
    REFUTED IF the fall is much shallower, which would say something other than
    pair count governs it — most likely `presence`, since a surface has to
    actually turn up to contribute.

P3  UNDER A FIXED BOUND OF 2, MORE MODALITIES DOES NOT HELP AND MAY HURT, at
    every stream length. A concept with 8 surfaces needs each of them to hold 7
    partners and a bound of 2 forbids it.
    REFUTED IF the fixed bound also improves with `surfaces`. **That would mean
    `g33-02`'s hub failure was specific to the star pairing rather than general
    to modality count**, and John's claim would need no condition at all.

P4  THE TWO BOUNDS CROSS. At 2 surfaces the fixed bound is as good or better —
    `g33-04` found a fixed bound wins where the class is small and the ranking
    is clean — and by 5 or 8 surfaces the derived bound is clearly ahead.
    REFUTED IF one dominates everywhere, which would retire the other and
    simplify a `DECISIONS.md` 🔀 that currently keeps both.

SCORECARD
---------
  P1  REFUTED AS STATED, AND THE GRID IS WHY. The threshold was predicted to
            fall with `surfaces`. It is **16 at every surface count** under the
            derived bound.

            **But the underlying claim is supported and the metric could not
            see it.** At every stream length below the threshold, more
            modalities scores higher — at 2 occasions per concept: **0.3542,
            0.4201, 0.4969, 0.5677**; at 8: 0.8073, 0.8125, 0.8354, 0.8730.
            Monotone in `surfaces` at every column.

            The grid steps by factors of two, so an effect smaller than 2x in
            occasions cannot move a threshold defined as *the first column to
            clear the bar*. **The range contained the phenomenon and the
            RESOLUTION did not**, which is `g33-01`'s grid failure one axis
            over. Occasion steps between 8 and 32 would settle it and are not
            run.

  P2  REFUTED, AND THE MODEL BEHIND IT IS WRONG. Evidence per occasion was
            argued to grow as `C(s, 2)` — 1 pair at 2 surfaces, 28 at 8 — so the
            threshold should have fallen about twentyfold. It falls not at all,
            and the gain at the sparsest column is a factor of **1.60**, not 20.

            The reason is visible in the metric: `connected` asks whether a
            surface reached **ANY** true partner. Extra pairs are largely
            redundant for that question, so the return on additional modalities
            saturates almost immediately. A stricter question — recovering the
            whole class — might scale differently and is not what was measured.

  P3  **HELD, AND IT IS THE FINDING.** Under a fixed bound of 2, more modalities
            does not merely fail to help: **it caps what can ever be learned.**
            5 surfaces plateaus at **0.7531** and 8 surfaces at **0.5781**, and
            neither improves from 16 occasions per concept to 64 — thirty-two
            times the data buys **nothing**.

            2 and 3 surfaces reach 1.0000 on the same axis. So under the
            mechanism as it stood before `g33-04`, **adding modalities was
            actively harmful and no amount of experience repaired it.**

  P4  REFUTED. The two bounds were predicted to cross, with fixed winning at
            small class sizes. The derived bound is **equal or better in every
            one of the 24 cells**, and at 2 surfaces and 2 occasions it is
            ahead 0.3542 to 0.2083.

            `g33-04` did measure a fixed bound winning — on CHAINS, where a
            mid-chain surface's neighbours score asymmetrically. **So the
            crossover is on the PAIRING STRUCTURE axis, not on the modality
            count axis**, and `DECISIONS.md`'s 🔀 stands for the reason it was
            recorded rather than the reason predicted here.


WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT THE CLAIM
--------------------------------
**Right in direction.** More modalities is better at every stream length tested,
with the derived bound, and never worse.

**Modest in size.** A factor of 1.60 at the sparsest data, and not enough to move
a threshold on a grid that steps by twos. The *"quicker to learn"* half is real
and small; the pairwise-evidence argument that predicted a large effect is wrong,
because reaching ANY partner is an easy question that saturates.

**Conditional on the bound, and that is the part worth carrying.** The intuition
would have been FALSE against this project's own mechanism two days ago. A hub
with many spokes needs to hold many links, a fixed budget forbids it, and the
failure does not wash out with more data — it is a ceiling, not a delay. The
per-surface derived bound is what makes more modalities safe, and it exists
because `g33-02` found the failure first.

**So the honest form of the claim is:** more modalities helps a little with
learning speed, helps a lot with robustness (`g35-02`: 0.5522 to 0.9596 connected
at half the network gone), and is safe only if the mechanism adapts how many
links each thing keeps.


WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE
--------------------------
**The resolution question.** Whether the threshold falls at all is unresolved,
and a finer occasion axis between 8 and 32 would answer it cheaply.

**A harder recovery question.** `connected` is deliberately floor-free and
therefore easy. Whether recovering the WHOLE class gets faster with more
modalities is a different measurement, and `f1` cannot answer it because its
floor moves with the class size — which is the trap `g35-02` fell into.

**And these are synthetic surfaces.** `g36-01` has real pictures against words;
this has neither. It is about the arithmetic of modality count, not about
whether a third real modality behaves like a second.
