What is step 2 of the traversal actually worth, and does search now pay?
========================================================================

Script: experiments/g13_02_what_the_traversal_step_costs.py
Status: DISPATCHED. Predictions below were registered before the matrix ran.

ANSWER: pending. A smoke seed is recorded below and it reproduces the record.


IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
To answer "how is Alice related to Carol", the model has to do three lookups:
find Alice's relation, follow it to the person in the middle, then find that
person's relation. The first and third are unreliable when someone appears in
several facts -- there are two answers and nothing says which.

The middle step is different. It asks "Alice, parent-of, who?" and that pair
names exactly one person almost every time.

This measures the middle step. It matters because the fix for the other two --
try a possibility and check where it lands -- is BUILT OUT OF the middle step.
If the middle step is reliable, checking is reliable, and the fix works. If it
is not, the checker is as unreliable as the thing it checks and there is no
point building it.


WHY THIS EXISTS
----------------
Decision 107 declined to build the traversal and derived the whole case from
three measured steps:

    step                      chained   given a perfect input
    1  key(FACT,S) -> R1        0.710                   0.710
    2  key(S,R1)   -> M         0.703                   0.960
    3  key(FACT,M) -> R2        0.497                   0.677

    "take the two entity-lookup steps to the 0.95 that step 2 already reaches
     and the product is 0.87"

**That 0.87 is why traversal-plus-search is worth building at all, and the 0.960
it rests on came from an inline probe that left no script behind** -- the same
gap that left decisions 99 through 119 with no committed instrument until
g13-01.

Decision 111 refused search on one ground: "you cannot search your way out of
noisy primitives, because the verifier is built from the primitives." g13-01
measured the primitive at 1.000 +/-0.000 at out-degree 1, so the refusal has
expired. **Every follow-and-check operation search performs IS step 2**, which
makes step 2's accuracy simultaneously the traversal's cost and the verifier's
reliability.


WHAT IS MEASURED
-----------------
`generate_object_question` asks `S R -> ?`. A fact is laid out `FACT S R O` and
the store binds the previous position's key to the current position's value, so
the write at `O` binds `key(S, R)`; the question ends `... S R` to read exactly
that binding.

No retrieval probe, same as g13-01 and for the same reason: reimplementing one
is the mistake `run()`'s own docstring records, where the 150/300 cap values came
from a reimplementation whose store never bound.

Split by how many stated facts share the asked `(S, R)`. `generate` rejects a
repeated `(subject, object)` but nothing stops a subject holding one relation to
two people, and that ambiguity is left in and counted rather than engineered
away.

Two mutations were added with the generator and both are caught:
`the-object-question-ends-on-the-wrong-pair` and
`the-object-question-follows-a-distractor`. `kinship.py` carried NO mutation
before this.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
  P1  CONTROL, on the RECORD rather than the instrument. Step 2 at width 64
      lands within 0.10 of decision 107's 0.960. If it does not, that number
      does not reproduce and the 0.87 ceiling it justifies goes with it.

  P2  At a unique (S, R) pair, step 2 clears 0.99 at every width, the way step 1
      did at out-degree 1.

  P3  Where several facts share (S, R), accuracy falls toward 1/m.

  P4  Step 2 gains less than 0.03 from width 64 to 256. g13-01 established width
      is not the axis and this should agree.

  P5  THE DECISION. The compounded traversal-with-search ceiling -- step 1 at
      out-degree 1, times step 2 at a unique pair, times step 3 at out-degree 1
      -- clears 0.87.


THE SMOKE SEED, RUN BEFORE DISPATCH
------------------------------------
  step2  d64   overall 0.970   unique n=191 1.000   shared n=9 0.333
  step2  d128  overall 0.965   unique n=191 1.000   shared n=9 0.222
  step2  d256  overall 0.975   unique n=191 1.000   shared n=9 0.444

**Decision 107's 0.960 reproduces**, on one seed, which is the first time
anything on this line has been reproduced from a committed script.

And the asymmetry is the finding waiting to be confirmed: **step 2's ambiguity
is RARE -- 9 of 200 sequences, 4.5%** -- where step 1's out-degree >= 2 was
100 of 200. That is exactly what pair keys were built for in decision 104, and
it is why the middle step is the reliable one.

One seed. Eight are running, because the shared bucket is 9 sequences and three
seeds have twice failed to bound an effect in this project.


COST
----
3 widths x 8 seeds = 24 cells, in 8 jobs of 3 cells.

Estimated FROM THE MOST EXPENSIVE CELL -- width 256. The store is d x d and the
per-step work is a matvec, so width 256 is SIXTEEN times width 64, not four.

  measured by --cost:  16.1 ms per training sequence
                       0.4 min to train one cell (400 sequences x 4 epochs)
  worst job:           ~1 min if all three of its cells were the dear one

g11-03 lost four of six cells to a 240-minute timeout by estimating from a cheap
cell. The probe exists so that cannot happen twice.


MEASURED ON
-----------
openplexus/tasks/kinship.py, `generate_object_question`, 12 people, 10 facts,
hops 2 so the asked relation is the first of a real path rather than an
arbitrary distractor.

Pair keys and derived keys, one hop -- this is a single retrieval, not
composition. What the ceiling does NOT include is the composition step on top of
clean retrievals; decision 102 measured that at 1.000 over the whole rule table
("sixteen rules in a wide space are linearly separable whatever the labels do"),
so it is not expected to bind, but it is a separate factor and is not measured
here.
