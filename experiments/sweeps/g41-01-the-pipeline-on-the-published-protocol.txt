Does 0.8578 survive being reported properly?
============================================

Script: experiments/g41_01_the_pipeline_on_the_published_protocol.py
Status: PREDICTIONS REGISTERED, run pending.

**A TIMING PROBE RAN FIRST AND ITS NUMBERS ARE VISIBLE TO ME.** One seed, width
64, both beam widths, about 12 seconds. It was run to cost the sweep and it
smoke-tested the script, and then it showed the whole result at that cell.

`CLAUDE.md` is explicit that a prediction written after a control has run is a
weaker test than it looks, and the honest response is to say which came first
rather than to write four confident predictions and collect a clean scorecard.
So every prediction below is labelled **SEEN** or **GENUINE**, and only the
GENUINE ones are evidence that anything was understood in advance.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
We have a number for this system on somebody else's reasoning benchmark: 0.8578.
It is a good number — the published systems on the same benchmark run from about
0.39 to 0.97.

But it is a single average over 1,146 puzzles of wildly different difficulty, and
this project has already written down, twice, why that is not allowed here. The
easy puzzles and the hard puzzles are mixed together. It was measured once, at
settings copied from an older experiment that nobody has since varied. And it was
never compared against the score you would get by ignoring the puzzle entirely and
always giving the same answer.

This run changes nothing about how the system works. It reports the same
machinery honestly: broken out by how hard each puzzle is, with the do-nothing
score printed beside it, at eight different random starts instead of one, and
with the two copied settings actually varied.

A good number that survives this is worth quoting. One that does not was never
real, and it is much cheaper to find that out now than after it has been built on.


WHAT IS BEING MEASURED
----------------------
    accuracy    per hop bucket, 2 to 10, NEVER pooled. Hops 2-3 are the only
                depths present in TRAIN, so they are recall and 4-10 are
                generalisation
    recovery    whether the walk commits to the true chain of relations. The
                diagnostic that separates "the walk lost the chain" from "the
                fold named it wrong"
    subsets     `all`, and `clean` = note 059's max_appearances <= 2. Reported
                BESIDE each other, never instead, because the floor differs
                between them and g35-02's confound is a column of several scales
                printed as one

The arms, all scored on the same puzzles:

    majority    the ACHIEVABLE floor -- always answer the commonest TRAIN
                relation. NOT the test bucket's own majority, which g37-03
                established is an ORACLE floor no model can reach
    gap         fold with no fill for a missing rule
    random      fill a gap with a random relation. Note 088's bar
    wrong-delta THE CONTROL. A deliberately wrong displacement. It must land at
                or below `random`, and if it does not, "filling helps" is the
                finding rather than "this displacement helps"
    delta       the mechanism
    contrastive the learned-vector fill from g23-01/02

Swept: `width` over {32, 64, 128} and `beam width` over {4, 8}, at 8 seeds.
Both are constants carried from note 065's configuration and neither has been
varied since. `CLAUDE.md` names sweeping one arm and not the other by name, and
g37-03 swept the reference while leaving exactly these untouched.

**Eight seeds rather than three, on purpose.** The probe returned 1.0000 in
several cells. A failure occurring in one draw of eight is missed by three seeds
about 67% of the time, so a clean sweep of three is the EXPECTED outcome even
when a one-in-eight failure exists (`CLAUDE.md`, the g36-01 calibration). The
report prints the WORST seed beside the mean for the `delta` arm and for
recovery.


WHAT IS NOT CHANGED, AND WHY IT IS NOT
--------------------------------------
**Both aids stay in.** The walk is handed `len(chain)`, so it is told its own
depth; and *"deltas add"* is arithmetic supplied by hand rather than discovered
(note 090 says so in its own text).

Keeping them is what makes this comparable to note 091's 0.8578. Removing them is
a separate run, because a fall would otherwise be unattributable between "the
protocol was generous" and "the aid was doing the work" — which is `g37-02`'s
mistake one level up.


MEASURED ON
-----------
Local, one process.

    data        CLUTRR gen_train23_test2to10, TEST split, 1,146 puzzles
    rules       97, learned from TRAIN (note 066's bootstrap)
    deltas      20, from TRAIN loop constraints, null space dimension 1
    search      beam, branches 4 fixed, width swept
    layout      kinship

The data does not move with the seed. CLUTRR is a fixed file, so a seed varies
model initialisation, the contrastive vectors and the fill draws only. Variance
here is a statement about the model, not about the task.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
P1  **GENUINE.** WIDTH DOES NOT MATTER AND THE CARRIED 64 IS NOT THE ISSUE. The
    `delta` arm at 10 hops moves by less than 0.05 across {32, 64, 128} at fixed
    beam.
    REFUTED IF the spread exceeds 0.05. Then a second carried constant was
    load-bearing and every earlier CLUTRR figure is at an untuned width.

P2  **GENUINE, and it is the one this run exists for.** THE CELLS AT 1.0000 DO
    NOT ALL SURVIVE EIGHT SEEDS. At least one cell reporting 1.0000 at seed 0
    has a worst seed below 1.0000.
    REFUTED IF every 1.0000 cell is 1.0000 on all eight. Then the perfection is
    real and may be written as "every" — which is the word the g36-01
    calibration exists to stop being written on three seeds.

P3  **SEEN, so this is a summary and not a commitment.** BEAM WIDTH 4 IS
    UNDERTUNED AT DEPTH. The probe gave `delta` at 10 hops 0.7059 at beam 4
    against 0.7815 at beam 8, subset `all`. This says only whether that holds at
    8 seeds and 3 widths.

P4  **SEEN in part.** THE WRONG-DELTA CONTROL DOES NOT FIRE AT 2 HOPS. Every arm
    scored 1.0000 there in the probe, so the bucket cannot separate the
    mechanism from filling anything at all. GENUINE half: it fires at every
    bucket from 4 hops up, at every width and beam, on all 8 seeds — meaning
    `delta` exceeds `wrong-delta` in all of them.
    REFUTED IF any bucket from 4 up has `wrong-delta` at or above `delta`. Then
    the mechanism is not separated from filling at that depth.

P5  **GENUINE.** THE POOLED 0.8578 IS NOT REPRESENTATIVE OF THE DEEP BUCKETS.
    The `delta` arm at 10 hops, subset `all`, is below 0.8578 at every width and
    beam.
    REFUTED IF any cell at 10 hops meets or beats the pooled figure. Then
    pooling did not flatter the result and the protocol complaint costs nothing.


COST
----
Measured, not estimated: **11.8s** for one seed at one width, covering both beam
widths and including the shared setup (floor, 97 rules, 20 deltas) and the
per-seed contrastive vectors.

Full grid is 8 seeds x 3 widths. Estimated **4 to 8 minutes**, local, one
process. **This does not go to Actions** — it is minutes, not a matrix, and
`CLAUDE.md`'s line is how long it holds the machine.

Recorded after the run: **240.4s** for the full grid, 8 seeds x 3 widths x 2
beams, one process. Well inside the estimate, and the estimate itself was made
from a measured cell rather than a guessed multiple.


RESULTS
-------
The full tables are in the run's own output. What follows is the load-bearing
part, and the file is the authority for anything not quoted here.

WIDTH DOMINATES EVERYTHING, and it was never varied.
`delta`, subset `all`, beam 8, mean of 8 seeds:

    hops    width 32   width 64   width 128
       4      0.7789     0.9092      0.9125
       5      0.7543     0.9375      0.9476
       6      0.6098     0.8937      0.9381
       7      0.4922     0.8646      0.9262
       8      0.3850     0.8458      0.9425
       9      0.2489     0.7962      0.9160
      10      0.1943     0.7185      0.8676

At width 32 the mechanism is gone: 0.1943 at 10 hops against the `random` bar's
0.1807 and `wrong-delta`'s 0.1702.

THE SEED SPREAD IS A SYMPTOM OF THE WIDTH. At 10 hops, subset `all`, beam 8:

    width    mean    worst   seed 0
       32  0.1943   0.1092   0.1943
       64  0.7185   0.6050   0.7815
      128  0.8676   0.8319   0.8676

**Seed 0 is the best of eight at width 64**, and it is the seed every published
CLUTRR figure in this repository was taken at. At width 128 the spread closes to
0.036, because the model is no longer near its capacity cliff.

BEAM WIDTH IS REAL AND SMALL. `delta` at 10 hops, subset `all`:

    width    beam 4   beam 8   difference
       64    0.6796   0.7185      +0.0389
      128    0.8015   0.8676      +0.0661

So the carried `beam 4` was undertuned, and it is worth about a fifth of what the
carried `width 64` was worth. **Sweeping only the beam would have found a real
effect and missed the one that mattered** -- which is the argument for the
inventory habit rather than for chasing the knob that looks most likely.

WHAT EACH PART CONTRIBUTES, and this is the most consequential row in the run.
At width 256, beam 8, 10 hops, subset `all`, 8 seeds:

    achievable floor (commonest TRAIN answer)          0.0588
    walk + learned rule table, gaps UNFILLED           0.3613
    + fill a gap with a random relation                0.4632
    + fill it with a LEARNED relation vector           0.6061
    + fill it by the hand-supplied additive invariant  0.9076

    chain recovery, for reference                      0.8960

**The invariant is the largest single term, and it is the one supplied by hand.**
`note 090` says so in its own text — *"the rule 'deltas add' is a design choice,
not read from data"* — and this puts a number on what that choice is worth on the
deepest bucket: the difference between 0.3613 and 0.9076.

**Against the best LEARNED filler it is worth the difference between 0.6061 and
0.9076**, which is the fairer comparison and still large. `contrastive` is the
g23-01/02 mechanism and is the only arm here that could exist in a domain with no
conserved quantity.

**This bears directly on the scope question** (`note 104`, `g23-03`: general
knowledge graphs mostly have no additive invariant). A domain without one does not
get the `delta` row. It gets the `contrastive` row, and on this instrument that is
0.6061 against a floor of 0.0588 — a real result, and not the headline one.

THE 2-HOP BUCKET SEPARATES NOTHING, at any width or beam or seed. `gap`,
`random`, `wrong-delta`, `delta` and `contrastive` are all **1.0000** there, and
the achievable floor is **0.0000**. The 3-hop bucket is flat between `delta` and
`contrastive` (both 0.9524) at every width above 32. `g37-01` predicted the first
of these from the data alone and it is confirmed by measurement.

THE FLOOR MOVES WITH THE SUBSET, as note 059 and g35-02 both say it must, and it
is printed per cell rather than once.


SCORECARD
---------
  P1  **REFUTED, and it is the expensive outcome.** Predicted spread across
            widths below 0.05 at 10 hops; measured **0.67**. Width is not a
            detail, it is the dominant term, and `width 64` was carried from
            note 065's configuration into every CLUTRR number this project has
            published. It costs **0.149** at 10 hops against width 128.

            This is `CLAUDE.md`'s g9-11 calibration recurring exactly: a
            constant carried from another configuration dominating every
            mechanism being compared. That calibration is now three instances
            old and each was found by inventory rather than by suspicion.

  P2  **HELD, and it is why the run used eight seeds.** Cells reading 1.0000 at
            seed 0 have worst seeds well below it -- width 64, beam 8, subset
            `clean`, 6 hops: recovery **1.0000** at seed 0 against a worst of
            **0.8095**. Three seeds would have missed a one-in-eight failure
            about 67% of the time, and this run had many more than one.

  P3  **HELD, and it was labelled SEEN, so it is a summary.** Beam 4 is
            undertuned at depth at both usable widths. Recorded as a summary
            rather than counted as evidence of foresight.

  P4  **THE SEEN HALF CONFIRMED; THE GENUINE HALF REFUTED BY ONE CELL OF 84.**

            The seen half: every arm is 1.0000 at 2 hops, control included, so
            that bucket cannot separate the mechanism from filling anything at
            all.

            The genuine half predicted `delta` above `wrong-delta` in every cell
            from 4 hops up. It holds in **83 of 84** and fails at **width 32,
            beam 4, subset `clean`, 10 hops: delta 0.1328 against wrong-delta
            0.1367.**

            **It was found by checking all 84 mechanically and would have been
            missed by reading the tables**, which is what the six printed
            tables invite. The failing cell is the most capacity-starved corner
            of the grid, so it is consistent with P1 rather than separate from
            it -- at width 32 the mechanism has collapsed far enough that its
            own control is indistinguishable from it.

  P5  **REFUTED, in the direction that flatters the project, which is the one
            to be careful about.** Predicted that no 10-hop cell would reach the
            pooled 0.8578. Width 128 at beam 8 gives **0.8676**.

            So reporting the figure honestly made it BETTER rather than worse,
            once the width was allowed to move. The pooled number was not
            flattering the deep buckets; it was being dragged down by an
            undertuned width that the shallow buckets could absorb and the deep
            ones could not.


WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE, AND ONE OF THEM IS DISQUALIFYING FOR NOW
-------------------------------------------------------------------
**THE GRID DOES NOT CONTAIN ITS OWN ANSWER.** Width 128 is the best cell at every
hop bucket, both subsets and both beams -- it is the TOP of the grid, so the
optimum lies outside it and **0.8676 is a lower bound rather than a measurement
of the mechanism's reach.** `CLAUDE.md`'s g4-01 calibration is explicit that this
is a sweep that has not swept, and `tools/grid.py` exists to catch it.

An edge probe at width 256 was dispatched immediately. **It returned 218.7s
later and the edge did not close, but it is decelerating.** `delta` at 10 hops,
subset `all`, beam 8, 8 seeds:

    width      mean     worst    gain over the width below
       32    0.1943    0.1092
       64    0.7185    0.6050                       +0.5242
      128    0.8676    0.8319                       +0.1491
      256    0.9076    0.8739                       +0.0400

Each doubling buys roughly a quarter of the last one.

**THE 512 PROBE CLOSES IT, and not by being better.** 887.2s, 8 seeds:

    width      mean     worst    gain over the width below
      256    0.9076    0.8739                       +0.0400
      512    0.9233    0.8655                       +0.0157

The mean rises by 0.0157 and **the worst seed FALLS**, 0.8739 to 0.8655. The two
widths are inside each other's seed spread.

**And 512 is no longer uniformly best, which is the part that settles it.** At 5
hops it gives 0.9468 against 256's 0.9504, and at 6 hops 0.9322 against 0.9439.
The optimum is INTERIOR in several buckets, so the grid now contains its own
answer and `CLAUDE.md`'s g4-01 objection is discharged: these are measurements
rather than floors.

**The reach of this mechanism at 10 hops is about 0.91-0.92**, and width 256 is
the operating point — 512 costs four times as much for a difference the seeds
cannot resolve. `g42-01` takes 256 from here, with the cell written next to the
pin.

**The 4x cost scaling held exactly**: 218.7s at 256 predicts 875s at 512 and it
came in at 887.2s. Worth recording, because `CLAUDE.md`'s standing calibration is
that local-to-runner timing multipliers are guessed badly — this one is a
local-to-local width multiplier and it is clean.

**A THIRD CARRIED CONSTANT WAS PINNED IN THIS RUN AND NOT SWEPT.** `branches = 4`
comes from `note 065`'s configuration exactly as `width` and `beam width` did.
Three axes was as much as one grid could hold, and the honest statement is that
this sweep corrected two of three.

It was found by `tools/check_constants.py`, written LATER THE SAME SESSION and
pointed at this file, which flagged it immediately. **So the experiment built to
correct carried constants was itself carrying one, with the rule fresh in mind** —
which is the strongest available argument that the rule needed to become a check
rather than a fifth calibration.

**BOTH AIDS ARE STILL IN.** The walk is handed `len(chain)` and *"deltas add"* is
supplied by hand. Nothing here is a claim about a system that finds its own
answer's shape. `g42-01` is that question and its predictions are already
committed.

**The 2-hop and 3-hop buckets are recall, not generalisation**, being the only
depths present in TRAIN. Nothing in the 4-10 range is undermined by their
flatness, and nothing about the mechanism should be argued from them.
