Does the store carry the relational tasks, or is that an inference too?
=======================================================================

Script: experiments/g18_05_does_the_store_carry_the_relational_tasks.py
Run:    30436450902, 6 of 6 jobs, 12 cells, three seeds.
Status: **THE CONTROL THE WHOLE TEXT LINE RESTS ON.** Decision 142.

ANSWER: **The store carries MQAR completely, and the readout bias -- which wins
        on text -- COSTS 0.279 accuracy here.** The two pathways compete, and
        which one pays depends entirely on whether the task has exploitable
        marginals.


MEASURED ON
------------
MQAR at g10-01's settings: 4 pairs, sequence length 64, 16 keys, 8 values,
`autoregressive=True`, `filler="random"`, vocabulary 25. Width 64, lr 0.05,
key_scale 0.5, decay 0.99, 200 training sequences, 6 epochs, 100 held-out
sequences, three seeds. Accuracy at query positions.

`nostore` writes nothing, so every retrieval is zero.

WHAT IT SETTLES
----------------
Five sweeps say the store contributes ~nothing on text, and every one of those
records protects itself with the same sentence: *"this does not touch the
relational line."* **That sentence was an inference and is now a measurement.**
The store does on this task exactly what it refuses to do on text, so the text
results are about TEXT being the wrong instrument for it.

**`nostore` sits at ZERO, not at the trivial floor**, and the distinction is
worth keeping: a model with nothing to retrieve does not guess uniformly, it
emits a constant. 0.3438 is what a smart guesser scores; a degenerate model
scores nothing at all.


AND THE HALF THE PREDICTION GOT WRONG
--------------------------------------
    bias 0    floor 0.9950
    bias 1    floor 0.7158        the prior COSTS 0.279

P3 expected the bias to be inert here, and reasoned that if it paid, the
generator must have a base rate. **It does not pay -- it costs**, and that is the
more interesting failure.

A prior with nothing to predict does not sit idle. It competes with the
retrieval for the same readout, and on a task with no exploitable marginals it is
pure interference.

**Which is the exact mirror of the text result:**

    on TEXT    the prior wins and the store adds nothing  (139, 141)
    on MQAR    the store wins and the prior costs 0.279   (here)

Two pathways into one readout, and which of them pays is decided by the task
rather than by the architecture.


THE INSTRUMENT FAILURES CAUGHT BUILDING THIS
---------------------------------------------
Both were note 046's mistake -- a constant inherited across a boundary -- and
both were caught by the trivial floor before anything was dispatched.

  decay 1.0             carried from the word-level work; MQAR uses 0.99.
  autoregressive off    the dataclass default. With it off the answer lives in
                        `sequence.targets` and is NOT the next token, so the
                        `np.roll(tokens, -1)` every MQAR script uses scores
                        against the wrong symbol. Both arms returned 0.055
                        against a 0.3438 floor -- below chance, which is the
                        signature of a misalignment rather than undertraining.

A third was caught after the run: the summariser applied the bits convention
(lower is better) to accuracy and printed P3's two explanations the wrong way
round. Corrected. **This file is the only accuracy-scored sweep in the g18 line,
and the exception is where a habit goes wrong.**
