Is the cliff about REACH, or about what reach costs?
====================================================

Script: experiments/g9_02_reward_gate.py, with --window swept
Status: PREDICTIONS WRITTEN. Not dispatched, no number seen.


WHY THIS COMES BEFORE THE TAG
-------------------------------
[g9-02](g9-02-a-gate-that-reads-its-own-input.txt) measured a cliff. With the
gate's reach fixed at 8:

    delay      1      4      8     20
    reward  0.21   0.20   0.23  -0.13

Within reach it recovers a fifth of the oracle's advantage — the first recovery
of anything in this project. Past its reach it is **worse than storing
everything**, because it keeps the wrong nine steps and discards the right one.

The obvious reading is that this is what tagging and capture exists to fix: mark
the binding cheaply when it happens, let the late signal decide. **But that
reading skips a question, and the question is cheaper than the mechanism.**

A window of 32 also reaches a binding 20 steps back. If that works, the cliff is
not about reach at all — it is about **what reach costs**, and a tag is a cost
optimisation rather than a new capability. If it does not work, then reaching
further is not enough and something has to mark *which* binding, which is a
mechanism.

Those are different projects, and the sweep that separates them is one changed
constant.

**And the cost is not incidental**, given the priority is minimum node size. The
current gate holds `(value, key)` per pending step — `2d` numbers, so reach 32 at
width 32 costs 2048 numbers, twice the fast store itself. A tag can be a **token
id**: keys are derived from `(seed, token)` and values from `wv[:, token]`, so
every pending contribution is re-derivable from one integer. Reach 32 would cost
32 numbers instead of 2048.

So if reach is what matters, the tag makes long reach affordable and the answer
is engineering. If reach is not what matters, no amount of cheap reach helps.


THE GRID
--------
  window     4, 8, 16, 32, 64      the reach, previously frozen at 8
  delay      1, 4, 8, 20           unchanged
  lr         0.05                  pinned; the delay grid showed little movement
  seeds      1, 2, 3

`none`, `oracle`, `on-use` and `salience` are unaffected by the window and carry
over as the same floor, ceiling and failures.


PREDICTIONS
------------
1. **Recovery is high whenever `window >= delay`, and negative whenever
   `window < delay`.** The cliff follows the reach rather than sitting at any
   fixed delay. Guess: above 0.15 on and above the diagonal, negative below it.

2. **A window well beyond the delay is WORSE than one that just covers it.** At
   delay 1 a window of 64 keeps 63 steps of filler along with the binding, which
   is most of a sequence and close to not gating at all. So each delay should
   have an interior best at roughly `window = delay`, and recovery should fall
   off on both sides.

   **This is the prediction that decides the next project.** If it holds, reach
   must be *matched* to a delay nobody knows in advance — and a tag, which marks
   one binding rather than a span, is the mechanism that removes the need to
   know. If instead a large window is simply always fine, the tag is only a cost
   saving.

3. **At `window = 64` and delay 20, recovery is positive but small** — it reaches
   the binding, and drowns it in filler.

4. **Control:** the `none`, `oracle`, `on-use` and `salience` arms do not move
   with the window, since none of them reads it. If they do, the window is
   reaching something it should not.

Most likely wrong: (2). It is the one that assumes filler inside the window costs
something measurable, and the fast store may be tolerant enough at this width
that thirty steps of filler barely register.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- cliff tracks the diagonal, interior best  -> reach must be matched to an unknown
                                               delay. **Build the tag**, and it is
                                               a mechanism rather than a saving
- large window always fine                  -> reach is free if you can afford it.
                                               The tag is a COST optimisation and
                                               belongs with the tiny-node work
                                               rather than the gating work
- large window never fine                   -> the gate cannot use a late signal
                                               at all, and 0.2 at short delays was
                                               about adjacency rather than reward
