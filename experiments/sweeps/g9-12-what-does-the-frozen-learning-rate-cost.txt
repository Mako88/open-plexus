What does the frozen learning rate cost?
========================================

Script: experiments/g9_05_the_tag.py, with --lr OMITTED so the script sweeps it
Status: RUN. 4 of 4 jobs, 54 cells each, no refusals.
Run:    https://github.com/Mako88/open-plexus/actions/runs/30251816417

ANSWER: NOTHING. The frozen constant cost nothing detectable, at any node width.
        Four predictions, none confirmed, and the negative result is the useful
        one -- but read the POWER section, because the bound is loose.

PREDICTIONS, SCORED -- NONE CONFIRMED
--------------------------------------
1. **REFUTED.** Predicted the best rate differs by node width, falling as the
   node narrows. Every width is TIED: leads of +0.03, +0.02, +0.03, +0.01
   against noise floors of 0.29, 0.18, 0.15, 0.12. The direction was flagged as
   the guess most likely to be backwards; it was not backwards, it was absent.

2. **REFUTED, and this is the one that mattered.** Predicted recovery at the
   best rate would differ from its value at 0.05 by more than 0.05 somewhere.
   The largest cost anywhere is +0.03 and the largest that beats its own noise
   floor is **+0.00**. Note 028's concern is real in principle and **negligible
   in practice**, and that deserves saying as plainly as the warning was said.

3. **REFUTED LITERALLY, CONFIRMED IN SUBSTANCE, and the distinction is the
   point.** Predicted the arm ordering is unchanged at every rate. The raw
   ordering changes at EVERY node width. But every swap is between arms sitting
   within the seed spread of each other -- `reward`, `tag` and `combined` are
   within 0.01-0.03 of one another throughout -- so it is one tie broken three
   ways, not a reordering. Note 028's reassurance survives; the naive version of
   the check would have reported it broken at all four widths.

4. **REFUTED.** Predicted the floor arm moves by more than 0.15 across rates
   somewhere, reproducing g8-01. Largest movement is **0.072** at node 16.
   g8-01's factor of three does not reproduce at this configuration, which is a
   different task, delay and sequence length -- so that finding is narrower than
   the general warning built on it.

POWER: CORRECTED, AND THE BOUND IS MUCH TIGHTER THAN FIRST WRITTEN
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The section below was written from `margin`, the seed RANGE divided by the gap.
Re-reading these same records with the ratio computed INSIDE each seed gives
standard errors of **0.01 to 0.06**, not 0.10 to 0.29:

    node    range-margin    paired SE    2 SE on a difference
      64        0.28         0.03-0.06        0.137
      32        0.18         0.01-0.02        0.041
      16        0.15         0.02-0.03        0.067
       8        0.10         0.03-0.05        0.103

**At node 32 the measurement is fifteen times tighter than the range said.** The
verdict does not change — every rate lead is inside 2 SE — but the bound does:
the rate effect is under **0.04** at node 32, not under 0.18.

The range was charging the mechanism for seeds whose data was simply harder.
Scoring each seed against its own floor and its own ceiling removes that, and
CLAUDE.md's *per-seed values not means* rule already required it; the ratio was
the one place it had not been applied.

**And the pairing exposed something the mean hid.** At node 8, lr 0.05 and 0.1,
one seed is DROPPED: its own oracle did not beat its own floor, so it measured
nothing. Averaged in, it was invisible. Node 8 is at the edge of where this task
works, which matters because node 8 is the width the tiny-node priority is about.

The original section follows, kept because it was published.

POWER: WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT RULE OUT (AS FIRST WRITTEN, SUPERSEDED)
------------------------------------------
**The noise floors are large and the bound is correspondingly loose.** A seed
spread of 0.29 recovery units at node 64 is not a detail: the tag's ENTIRE effect
there is 0.23, so this sweep could not have detected a rate effect smaller than
the whole mechanism.

    node 64   noise 0.29   the bound is nearly vacuous
    node 32   noise 0.18
    node 16   noise 0.15
    node  8   noise 0.12   the tightest, and still half the tag's effect

So the honest claim is **"no rate effect larger than the seed spread"**, not "no
rate effect". Three seeds is what every sweep in this line uses, and this is the
first time the cost of that choice has been visible as a number.

**The raw best rate sits at a grid EDGE at three of four widths** (0.1, 0.1, 0.1,
then 0.02). Under ties that means nothing, but it means the grid would not have
bounded an effect if there had been one -- so a future run that wants a tight
bound needs both more seeds and a wider rate range, and more seeds first.

WHAT IT COST AND WHAT IT BOUGHT
---------------------------------
Four jobs. It retires the largest standing methodological worry in the project --
seven sweeps of numbers taken at one unexamined rate -- and replaces it with a
smaller and better-specified one: **three seeds is not enough to bound a small
effect, anywhere in this line.**

AN OBSERVATION NOBODY ASKED FOR
---------------------------------
`tag-strongest` runs -0.51, -0.10, +0.02, +0.05 as the node narrows from 64 to 8.
The inverted signal's direction is worth nothing at a wide node and turns
positive at a narrow one, which is the third setting showing that shape (g9-08,
g9-09, and now here). It is the clearest statement of it so far because node
width is the only axis moving. One grid, three seeds, so an observation.


IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
Every score in this line is a fraction: how much of a cheating filter's advantage
a real one recovers. The denominator is how much better the cheat is than no
filter at all — and that depends on how fast the model learns, a setting picked
once for a different configuration and carried through seven experiments
untouched.

We know it matters. On an older experiment the same cell had a denominator of
0.20 at one learning rate and 0.61 at another. And last night a *different*
carried setting was measured at 0.58 of recovery — more than twice the largest
effect any mechanism here has produced.

So this asks the plain question: at the configurations actually being run, is
0.05 the right rate, and how much are the published fractions off if it is not?


WHY THIS RUN EXISTS
--------------------
[Note 028](../../docs/notes/028-the-learning-rate-has-been-frozen-for-seven-sweeps.md).
`lr 0.05, FIXED on every arm` appears in the grid of g9-05 through g9-11 — every
sweep in the line. It was chosen for g9-03's workflow at `d_model` 32 in one
process, and carried through every change of width, node count, capacity, fade
and reach since.

[g8-01's re-summarisation](../../GOALS.md) measured the rate moving the FLOOR arm
— the denominator of every ratio — by a factor of three (gap 0.196 at lr 0.02
against 0.612 at lr 0.1). GOALS was corrected for exactly that.

And [g9-11](g9-11-how-far-should-the-union-reach.txt) measured what one carried
constant costs when it is wrong: **0.58 of recovery at delay 8**, from `slots`
alone.

**The cost here is unusually low.** `g9_05_the_tag.py` sweeps
`LEARNING_RATES = (0.02, 0.05, 0.1)` inside a job whenever `--lr` is omitted, so
dropping the flag triples the compute PER JOB and adds no jobs at all.

PREDICTIONS
------------
1. **The best rate differs by node width.** Guess: it FALLS as the node narrows
   — 0.1 at node 64 down to 0.02 at node 8 — because a narrower reader retrieves
   more noisily and a smaller step averages more of that away. This is reasoning
   rather than measurement and is the guess most likely to be backwards.

2. **THE ONE THAT MATTERS: recovery at each node's best rate differs from its
   value at 0.05 by more than 0.05, at at least one node width.** If it does not,
   note 028's concern is real in principle and negligible in practice, and that
   should be said as plainly as the warning was.

3. **The ORDERING of arms is unchanged at every rate.** `combined` >= `tag` >
   `tag-strongest`, and `reward` above all of them at this matched reach. Note
   028 asserted that ordinal findings survive a wrong rate because every arm in a
   cell shares it. That is an argument, not a measurement, and this checks it.

4. **The floor arm moves by more than 0.15 across rates at some node width**,
   reproducing g8-01's finding at a configuration four sweeps away from it.

Most likely wrong: **1**, specifically its direction. The opposite story — a
narrow node needs a LARGER step because it has fewer dimensions to accumulate
evidence in — is just as plausible and I cannot distinguish them from the desk.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- best rate varies and recovery moves  -> every published fraction in the g9 line
                                          is off by an unknown amount, and the
                                          line needs re-baselining at a tuned
                                          rate before any of it is quoted
- best rate varies, recovery does not  -> the ratio is robust to the rate even
                                          though the floor is not, which would be
                                          a genuinely reassuring result and a
                                          non-obvious one
- 0.05 is best everywhere              -> the carried constant was lucky. Say so,
                                          and stop treating it as a risk
- the arm ORDERING changes with rate   -> note 028's central reassurance is
                                          wrong, and every comparison in the line
                                          needs re-checking, not just the scales


COST
-----
4 jobs. Tripling the cells per job rather than the number of jobs is the whole
reason this is cheap, and it is why there was never a good excuse for not doing
it.


THE ORACLE, SAID TWICE
-----------------------
The `oracle` arm reads `position_kinds()` and is a CEILING. It is also the
numerator's other end: if the rate moves the oracle as well as the floor, the
ratio can be stable while both ends move, and the summary prints both.
