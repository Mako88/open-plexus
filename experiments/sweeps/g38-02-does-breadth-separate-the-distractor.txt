Does breadth separate the word from the distractor?
====================================================

Script: experiments/g38_02_does_breadth_separate_the_distractor.py
Status: RUN, local, 2026-07-31, 8.3s.

ANSWER: **NO, and the proposal is dead before anything was built.** Breadth
        separates the word from the distractor by **1.24x**. Plain frequency —
        which the deployed statistic already uses — separates them by
        **3.56x**. So the quantity proposed as *better than frequency* is
        roughly three times WORSE at the one job it was proposed for.

        Cost: one probe and about eight seconds, against a mechanism, its
        tests, its mutations and a sweep.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
After the last run, the picture was this. There is one thing that is present in
every single moment — call it the hum of the room. It ruins the machine's
judgement, because it turns up alongside everything and so appears to be about
everything.

The current fix is to divide by how often something occurs. The hum occurs
constantly, so it gets divided down to nothing. That works. **But the word "dog"
also occurs quite often**, and it gets divided down too, which is the problem we
have been chasing all week.

So the idea was: surely the hum and the word are different in a way that has
nothing to do with how *often* they occur. The word is common but **fussy** — it
mostly turns up around dogs. The hum is common and **indiscriminate** — it turns
up around everything, evenly. If we measured fussiness instead of frequency, we
could keep the word and drop the hum.

It is a nice idea. **It is wrong, and one measurement was enough to show it.**

The word turns out to be nearly as indiscriminate as the hum. Not because words
are indiscriminate in general, but because of how this test data is built:
alongside the right word, two random other words are spoken in every moment. So
over three thousand moments, every word ends up having been present alongside
almost every picture at least once.

Measured properly — not "how many things has it ever met" but "how evenly is its
attention spread" — the hum is only about **a quarter** more spread out than a
word. Frequency, the thing we already use, tells them apart more than **three
times** better.

So the proposal is not a small improvement or a promising direction. It is worse
than what it was meant to replace, at the exact job it was meant to do.

**Writing this down is the point.** The idea was mine, it sounded principled, and
it would have cost a day. It cost eight seconds instead because a claim about
behaviour was checked before a mechanism was built on it.


WHAT IS BEING MEASURED
----------------------
    frequency    how many occasions a surface was present on
    partners     how many DISTINCT surfaces it has ever met
    effective    `exp(entropy)` of its partner counts — how many partners it
                 BEHAVES as though it has. Mass spread evenly over a hundred
                 scores about a hundred; mass sitting on three scores about
                 three, however many it has touched once
    SEPARATION   distractor divided by word, for each column. **The only number
                 that decides anything**, since the proposal is precisely that
                 one column separates them better than another

`effective` rather than `partners` because the raw count cannot express the
difference the proposal rests on. Both are reported so the failure is attributed
to the right one.


MEASURED ON
-----------
Local, one process. Streams IMPORTED from `g36_04`, so the distributions
measured are the ones the failure was measured on.

    images       4,000   MNIST prefix
    recordings   3,000   FSDD, six speakers
    occasions    3,000
    codes           50   pinned from g36-04
    noise            2   OTHER WORDS per occasion, drawn UNIFORMLY at random.
                         **This is the mechanism that kills the proposal** and
                         it is a property of the generator, not of language
    distractors      1   present EVERY occasion
    seeds      0, 1, 2

Arms: `together` and `alternating`.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
P1  BREADTH SEPARATES THEM BETTER THAN FREQUENCY. The distractor's `effective`
    partner count is more than 3.56x the word's — beating what frequency already
    achieves.
    REFUTED IF the separation is smaller. **Then the proposal is worse than the
    thing it was meant to replace and nothing should be built.**

P2  RAW PARTNER COUNT DOES NOT SEPARATE THEM. `partners` is close for both,
    because a surface meeting something once is not the same as depending on it.
    REFUTED IF raw count separates them well, which would make the entropy
    unnecessary and the mechanism cheaper still.


COST
----
Estimated from `g36-06`'s 25.5s: this builds the same streams and does no walk at
all, so it is strictly cheaper. **Under 30 seconds.**

Recorded after the run: **8.3s**.

SCORECARD
---------
  P1  **REFUTED, and not narrowly.** Breadth separates by **1.24x** and
            **1.26x**; frequency separates by **3.56x** in both arms. The
            proposed quantity is about three times worse at the job it was
            proposed for.

            **The mechanism of the failure is identified rather than guessed.**
            A word's `partners` count is **109.2** of a possible 110 — it has met
            essentially every surface. That is the uniform noise draw: two random
            other words in every occasion means each word appears alongside every
            digit's codes many times over three thousand occasions.

            So the word is not fussy in this data. It is nearly as indiscriminate
            as the distractor, and the only thing separating them is how much
            more often the distractor turns up — which is frequency, which we
            already use.

  P2  **HELD, and it is the one useful thing here.** `partners` separates by
            **1.01x** and **1.02x**, which is nothing at all. Raw partner count
            is completely blind to the difference, so the entropy was the right
            refinement of the idea — it just was not enough of one.


WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE, AND IT IS THE HONEST CAVEAT
--------------------------------------------------------
**The refutation is conditional on THIS noise model, and the noise model may be
unrealistic.** `NOISE = 2` draws other words uniformly at random, so a word is
equally likely to be uttered in any scene. Real speech is not like that: *dog* is
not said uniformly across every situation, and in a realistic stream a word's
partner distribution would be far more concentrated.

**So what is refuted is: breadth cannot rescue the distractor problem ON THE DATA
THE FAILURE WAS MEASURED ON.** That is the correct scope, and it is sufficient to
stop the build — a mechanism cannot be justified by a stream nobody has run.

**It also identifies a real defect in the instrument**, which is worth more than
the refuted proposal. If a word meeting 109 of 110 surfaces is an artefact of
uniform noise, then every co-occurrence statistic measured on this generator is
being asked a slightly wrong question, and `g32-02`'s `zipf` knob exists to make
frequencies uneven but nothing makes *which* words appear together uneven.

**That is registered and not fixed here.** Changing the generator would
invalidate the comparison set, so it is a decision rather than a cleanup.
