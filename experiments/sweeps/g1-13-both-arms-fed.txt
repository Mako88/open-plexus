The price, with both arms actually finished training
====================================================

Script: experiments/g1_13_both_fed.py
Status: PREDICTIONS WRITTEN, NOT YET RUN


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
Last time we compared the two approaches, we gave each of them a fixed amount of
training regardless of how hard the problem was. Then we discovered the
conventional one had simply not finished -- double its training and a result we
had read as "too small to do this" became perfect.

That retracted our favourable number. But it also means our OWN side was measured
the same way, and nobody has checked whether it had finished either. Fixing one
side and not the other is exactly the mistake we made once already and wrote a
rule against.

So this measures both, with enough training to be sure, and checks along the way
that each has actually stopped improving before reading anything off it.


QUESTION
--------
g1-12 found attention starved at a fixed 3000 steps. The local rule was measured
in g1-10 at a fixed 8 epochs, and g1-07 verified its convergence only at
seq_len=96 -- where it was flat from epoch 1 to epoch 64.

Re-measure both crossings across seq_len, with checkpoints, at budgets four times
what was used before.

  ATTENTION  seq_len 48/96/192/384 x width 2/4/6/8   x 12000 steps
             checkpoints 1500 3000 6000 12000
  LOCAL      seq_len 48/96/192/384 x width 16/24/32/48/64 x 32 epochs
             checkpoints 1 2 4 8 16 32

3 seeds. A crossing is only read from a checkpoint where the two largest budgets
agree; otherwise the report is a bound, not a number.


PREDICTIONS -- written before the run
--------------------------------------
1. **The local rule is NOT starved**, at any sequence length. g1-07 found it flat
   from epoch 1 to 64 at seq_len=96, and there is a mechanical reason: only the
   readout learns, by a delta rule over frozen features, which is a convex fit
   that converges almost immediately. Attention has to discover a circuit
   through a non-convex search, which is exactly the kind of thing that takes
   longer at longer sequences.

   So I expect the local crossings to come back roughly where g1-10 put them:
   21.8, 26.0, 34.0, 47.5.

2. **Attention crosses at or below 8 at every length.** g1-12 showed width 8
   solving seq_len 384 completely by 6000 steps. Whether it is 8, 6, 4 or 2 is
   what this pins down. Guess: 4-6, roughly flat.

3. Therefore the WIDTH ratio grows with sequence length, and by more than the
   retracted g1-11 claimed and more than the corrected table says -- if attention
   crosses at 5 rather than 8, the ratios become 4.4x, 5.2x, 6.8x, 9.5x.

4. And the MEMORY ratio shrinks correspondingly. At attention width 5 the
   memory ratio at seq_len 384 falls from 2.72x to 1.70x -- still in our favour,
   still growing with length, but modest.

5. **So the honest expectation is that both numbers move against us again.**
   Stating that in advance, because it is the third time this comparison has been
   revised and the previous two revisions were also unfavourable. A pattern of
   corrections all running one way is itself evidence about the original
   estimates, and the direction is that this project's first guesses flatter it.

Most likely to be wrong: (1). If the local rule IS starved at long sequences,
its crossings drop and both ratios improve. That would be the favourable
direction, so per the rule this project earned from g1-12, it gets the same
scrutiny attention just received -- specifically, the local checkpoints must show
a genuine plateau and not merely a slower climb.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- local flat, attention crosses
  below 8                        -> both numbers move against us again. Report
                                    them and note the pattern
- local also starved             -> both ratios improve. Scrutinise hard: check
                                    the local plateau is real before believing
- either still climbing at the
  largest budget                 -> report a bound rather than a number, and say
                                    the budget was insufficient
- crossings match g1-10 and
  g1-12 exactly                  -> the fixed-budget confound only ever affected
                                    attention at long sequences, and the earlier
                                    numbers stand


WHY THIS IS THE LAST REVISION THAT SHOULD BE NEEDED
-----------------------------------------------------
Every previous version of this comparison had an unswept parameter doing hidden
work: the initialisation scale (g3-02), then grid-rounded crossings (g1-11), then
the training budget (g1-12). This sweeps the budget on both arms, uses the tuned
scale on both arms, and interpolates rather than rounds.

If a fourth confound turns up, the honest conclusion will not be another
correction but that this comparison is harder to make fairly than it looks, and
that any single ratio quoted from it should carry that caveat permanently.
