Does storing by CONCEPT rather than by surface make word-level text learnable?
=============================================================================

Script: experiments/g18_01_does_storing_by_concept_make_words_learnable.py
Run:    30434436216, 32 of 32 jobs, 128 cells, three seeds.
Status: **THE PRE-REGISTERED SWEEP, ON THE CORRECTED HARNESS.** Decision 141.

Dispatched after decision 138 voided the numbers that had refuted this mechanism
and after decision 140 confirmed the premise behind it still stands.

ANSWER: **Collapsing the address space is worth 0.540 bits -- and it does not
        need to mean anything.** A grouping built from SHUFFLED content does as
        well or better. And once the readout has a prior, none of it helps at
        all.


MEASURED ON
------------
Tiny Shakespeare at word level, `corpus.words`, min_count 10, vocabulary 1733,
90,000 training words, width 128, pair keys, `derived_keys`, cap 5.0, decay 1.0,
lr 5e-6, one epoch, chunk 256. **Three seeds.** Bits per word, temperature fitted
on held-out TRAINING text.

The rate survives the target correction: g18-02 corrected puts 5e-6 at 9.186
against 9.210 and 9.221 either side, still an interior optimum.

WHAT IT SAYS, IN THREE PARTS
-----------------------------
**1. Address density is worth real bits, when the store is the only learner.**
At bias 0 the floor is 10.700 -- 0.06 below uniform, a model that has learned
almost nothing -- and concept-64 reaches 10.159. **That is 0.540 bits, and it is
the largest effect any addressing change has produced in this project.**

**2. It does not need to mean anything.** `shuffled-64`, whose grouping comes
from a content index fitted on a SCRAMBLED corpus, reaches 10.143 -- slightly
BETTER than the learned grouping. The semantic content of the grouping is worth
nothing; the collapse of the address space is worth everything.

    concept-64    10.159      grouping from real co-occurrence
    shuffled-64   10.143      grouping from scrambled co-occurrence
    permuted-64   10.329      matched sizes, random membership

Note the controls disagree with each other, which is why there are two.
`shuffled` clusters a structureless space and lands on a size distribution that
happens to be slightly better than the real one; `permuted` forces the real
distribution onto random members and does worse than both. **So even the SHAPE
of the grouping matters more than its meaning.**

**3. And a prior subsumes all of it.** At bias 1 the floor is 9.186 and no
grouped arm beats it -- the best is 0.093 behind. The 0.540 bits that grouping
buys with no prior is less than the 1.514 bits a prior buys on its own, and the
two do not add.


SO THE MECHANISM IS NEITHER REFUTED NOR VINDICATED
----------------------------------------------------
Decisions 136 and 137 said grouping HURTS at every K, monotonically in how much
resolution it destroys. **That was the mistrained harness and it had the sign
backwards.** The explanation is clean: on a mistrained readout the retrievals are
noise, so denser addresses make the noise more consistent and the readout fits it
harder -- worse. Corrected, denser addresses carry more usable signal -- better.

What replaces it is narrower and stands: **grouping helps only where the store is
the only learner, only as a counting effect, and a prior does more for less.**
