How many occasions does a concept need?
=======================================

Script: experiments/g32_02_how_many_occasions_does_a_concept_need.py
Status: PREDICTIONS COMMITTED, NOT YET RUN.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
g32-01 asked whether a machine can learn that a picture and a word mean the same
thing by noticing they turn up together. It can — as long as it asks "did these
two meet MORE OFTEN than you would expect by chance" rather than just "how often
did these two meet".

But it also found something nobody was looking for. When some things in the
world are far commoner than others — which is true of every real world; a few
words are used constantly and most are rare — the whole thing got much worse, for
every method, whether or not the distractor was there.

There are two completely different reasons that could happen, and g32-01 cannot
tell them apart:

  1. The rare things simply were not seen enough times. Nothing can learn a thing
     it met once. That is dull, and it converts to a number: how many times is
     enough.

  2. The common things are actively drowning out the rare ones — crowding into
     everyone's list of likely partners. That is a real problem with the design
     and it would not go away by running longer.

This tells them apart by scoring each concept separately and asking how many
times that particular concept came up. If a rare concept in a lopsided world does
just as well as an equally-rare concept in an even world, it is reason 1 and we
learn a threshold. If it does worse, it is reason 2.

Reason 2 would be the more serious finding, because the world this system is
meant to run on is lopsided and cannot be made even.


WHAT IS BEING MEASURED
----------------------
f1 per CONCEPT, against the number of occasions that concept was the subject of.

Two arms only — `count` and `ppmi` — because g32-01 established that `ppmi` and
`conditional` impose the same order on every above-chance pair and differ only in
what they refuse, so running both again would re-measure one thing twice.

**The floor is 0.5, not 0.** A three-surface concept recovered entirely alone
scores precision 1.0 and recall 1/3, which is f1 0.5. Every number below is read
against that, which is the correction g32-01's P5 forced.

No distractor in any cell. g32-01 settled that normalisation removes it entirely,
so carrying it here would only add a term already understood.

**The bottom of the range is deliberate.** CLAUDE.md requires probing the bottom
and confirming the arm is still moving there, because a grid that starts above
saturation guarantees a flat line; g11-05 lost fifteen jobs to exactly that. Four
occasions per concept is far below anything that can work, so if the curve is
flat at the bottom the grid is wrong rather than the answer.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
P1  The uniform curve RISES with occasions per concept and is still rising
    somewhere inside the grid — it is neither flat nor saturated at both ends.
    REFUTED IF f1 is flat across all seven lengths, which would mean the grid
    does not contain the phenomenon and no threshold can be read from it.

P2  THE ONE THE PROBE EXISTS FOR. Concepts from the SKEWED streams score within
    0.10 of uniform concepts seen a comparable number of times, at every bucket
    where both have concepts.
    REFUTED IF skewed concepts score materially below matched uniform ones. That
    refutation is the serious outcome: it would mean a common concept degrades a
    rare one directly, running longer does not fix it, and a Zipfian world — the
    only kind there is — is a standing problem for this mechanism rather than a
    sampling inconvenience.

P3  `ppmi` needs FEWER occasions than `count` to reach any given f1. Chance
    correction is a variance reduction as well as a bias correction, so it should
    buy sample efficiency and not only distractor resistance.
    REFUTED IF the two curves coincide, which would say normalisation helps only
    against a persistent distractor and nothing else.

P4  Concepts seen fewer than about 8 times sit at or near the 0.5 floor for both
    arms. A concept met a handful of times cannot be distinguished from noise by
    any statistic over counts.
    REFUTED IF recovery is appreciable below 8 occasions, which would mean the
    mechanism is far more sample-efficient than expected and the Zipfian tail is
    reachable after all.

SCORECARD
---------
  P1  HELD. The uniform curve rises 0.7468 -> 1.0000 across 4 to 31 occasions
            per concept and saturates inside the grid. The bottom of the range
            was probed and the arm is moving there, so a threshold can be read.

  P2  REFUTED, and this was the probe's reason for existing. Skew is NOT only
            starvation. At MATCHED subject count, skewed concepts score far
            below uniform ones: under `count` at zipf 1.0, concepts seen 16-31
            times score 0.5056 against uniform's 0.9984 — a gap of 0.49, at
            identical rarity. At zipf 2.0 `count` sits at the 0.5 floor out to
            127 occasions where uniform is perfect from 32.

            **But the magnitude is completely different between arms, and that
            is the useful half.** `ppmi` is untouched by zipf 1.0 — 1.0000 in
            every bucket including the rarest. At zipf 2.0 it carries a
            mid-range penalty of 0.17 to 0.26 that closes by 32-64 occasions.
            So normalisation does not merely resist a designed distractor, it
            resists frequency skew as well.

  P3  REFUTED, AND BACKWARDS, which is the more interesting direction. `ppmi`
            was predicted to need FEWER occasions than `count`. On uniform
            streams it needs MORE: 0.5439 against 0.6248 at 2-3 occasions,
            0.7332 against 0.8322 at 4-7, 0.9503 against 0.9714 at 8-15. PMI is
            a ratio of two estimates, so it is higher-variance where counts are
            small, and chance correction costs sample efficiency rather than
            buying it.

            **So there is a crossover and neither statistic dominates.** Raw
            counting wins in the uniform, data-poor, distractor-free regime;
            normalisation wins the moment there is a distractor or any skew.
            That is `DECISIONS.md` 14c's shape — two options kept behind a
            switch — and not a winner to be chosen.

  P4  REFUTED, and the mechanism is far more sample-efficient than predicted.
            Concepts seen only 4-7 times reach 0.8322 under counting, well above
            the 0.5 floor rather than at it. The threshold for near-perfect
            recovery is about **16 occasions**, and useful recovery starts
            around 8.

            One line worth keeping: at a single occasion `ppmi` scores 0.3991,
            BELOW the 0.5 floor. With one observation it does not merely fail to
            group, it groups wrongly.


THE UNIFICATION, WHICH IS THE FINDING
--------------------------------------
g32-01 built a distractor by hand — a surface present on every occasion — and
found counting ranked it above every true partner. This probe did not build one,
and counting failed anyway at zipf 2.0.

The direct probe says why: **at high skew the commonest concept is present often
enough to BE that distractor.** Concept 0 is the subject of 62% of occasions, so
its surfaces turn up beside everything, and for a concept seen a handful of times
they turn up beside it far more often than its own partners do. It is not a hub
anyone designed. It is what a Zipfian world produces on its own.

So the two findings are one: **raw co-occurrence counting is defeated by anything
that is simply common, whether it was put there deliberately or arose from the
frequency distribution.** Dividing by the neighbour's own frequency is the fix in
both cases and it is the same fix — which is why one hop repairs both, and why
this is a property of the statistic rather than two separate problems.

**And the consequence for the project is a real constraint, not a tuning note.**
Every world this mechanism will actually meet is Zipfian. The tail is not an edge
case, it is most of the concepts. Two things follow and only the first is
comfortable:

  - Normalisation is MANDATORY, not an optimisation. Raw counting at zipf 2.0
    recovers nothing at all below 128 occasions.
  - Normalisation is not sufficient at zipf 2.0 either. `ppmi` still loses 0.17
    to 0.26 in the mid range, so the tail costs something even with the fix.


WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE, STATED SO IT IS NOT OVERREAD
--------------------------------------------------------
**The bucket comparison confounds subject count with stream length.** Uniform
concepts with few subject occasions come from SHORT streams (256, 512), so they
have little exposure to noise. Skewed concepts with the same subject count come
from an 8,000-occasion stream, so they have a great deal. The two are not matched
on that axis and the comparison alone cannot separate skew from exposure.

**The direct probe is what carries the conclusion**, and it is not subject to
that: it shows the specific partners a rare concept acquires, and they are the
commonest concept's surfaces. In a uniform stream of any length no concept can be
met 57 times by a stranger, because no concept is present that often. The clean
version would be one rare concept in an otherwise uniform world at fixed stream
length, and that is the control to run if this conclusion is ever load-bearing.

**Nothing here is distributed**, and none of it says anything about C1.
