Is the cliff about REACH, or about what reach costs?
====================================================

Script: experiments/g9_02_reward_gate.py, with --window swept
Status: RUN. 20 of 20 cells returned.
Run:    https://github.com/Mako88/open-plexus/actions/runs/30233412937

ANSWER: reach, and it has to be MATCHED. Build the tag.


THE RESULT
-----------
Recovery of the reward gate, as a fraction of the oracle's advantage. Rows are
how far the gate can reach; columns are how far back the binding is. On and above
the diagonal the reach covers the delay.

      window        1        4        8       20    best delay
           4     0.24     0.25    -0.22    -0.22             4
           8     0.23     0.23     0.23    -0.24             4
          16     0.20     0.21     0.19    -0.23             4
          32     0.14     0.16     0.16     0.17            20
          64     0.09     0.10     0.09     0.09             4

Two things are in that table and they point the same way.

**The cliff is the diagonal, exactly.** Every cell where the reach covers the
delay is positive; every cell where it does not is about -0.22. There is no
delay at which the gate is inherently bad — there is only reach that does or does
not cover it. g9-02's -0.13 at delay 20 was not a property of delay 20; it was a
window of 8 pointed at a binding 20 steps back.

**And more reach is worse, monotonically.** Read down any column. At delay 1:
0.24, 0.23, 0.20, 0.14, 0.09. The best reach is always the SMALLEST one that
covers the delay, and every doubling past that costs about a fifth of what is
left.

      delay   best window   recovery   at largest window
          1             4       0.24                0.09
          4             4       0.25                0.10
          8             8       0.23                0.09
         20            32       0.17                0.09

A window of 64 recovers 0.09 whatever the delay. It reaches everything and
resolves nothing, because 63 steps of filler come with the binding and the gate
is close to not gating at all.


PREDICTIONS, SCORED
--------------------
1. **Positive when `window >= delay`, negative when `window < delay`.**
   CONFIRMED, and cleanly. Guessed "above 0.15 on and above the diagonal";
   true everywhere except window 64, which is above the diagonal at 0.09 for the
   reason prediction 3 gave.

2. **A window well beyond the delay is worse than one that just covers it.**
   CONFIRMED. Named as most likely wrong, on the grounds that the store might be
   tolerant enough that thirty steps of filler barely register. It is not. The
   fall-off is monotone in every column and costs more than half the recovery
   between the best window and 64.

3. **At window 64, delay 20, positive but small.** CONFIRMED: 0.09.

4. **Control: the other four arms do not move with the window.** CONFIRMED to
   0.0000 — every arm, every delay, every seed, exactly unchanged. Worth saying
   plainly that this one is close to tautological: none of those arms reads the
   window, so this is a wiring check rather than a finding. It earns its place
   because the reward arm's numbers are ratios against those arms, and a floor
   that moved with the axis would have made the whole table meaningless.

Four for four is not a comfortable score, and the reason it is not suspicious
here is that (4) is a wiring check and (3) follows from (2). The substance is one
prediction — reach must be matched — and it was the one flagged as most likely
wrong.


WHAT THIS DECIDES
------------------
The two readings written before the run wanted different next projects:

- large window always fine -> reach is free if affordable, and a tag is a COST
  optimisation belonging with the tiny-node work
- interior best -> reach must be MATCHED to a lag nobody knows in advance, and a
  tag is a MECHANISM

It is the second. A window is a span, and a span has to be the right length: too
short and it misses the binding, too long and it drowns it. **Nothing in the
world tells a node how long the lag will be** — that is the whole problem, and a
gate parameterised by a guess at it is not a solution, it is the guess.

A tag marks *one binding* rather than a span. It does not need to know the lag,
because it does not need to cover it. That is the difference between a saving and
a capability, and this table is what makes it a capability.

The cost argument still holds and now comes for free: the gate holds
`(value, key)` per pending step — `2d` numbers, so reach 32 at width 32 costs
2048 numbers, twice the fast store itself. With derived keys a tag is a **token
id**, and every pending contribution is re-derivable from one integer.


WHAT IT DOES NOT SETTLE
------------------------
- **The grid's lower edge was never probed.** At delay 1 the best window is 4 —
  the smallest window in the sweep. The true optimum may be 1 or 2, and the
  fall-off may start below where anything was measured. The monotone decline is
  unaffected; the claim "best is roughly `window = delay`" is only supported down
  to 4.
- **Delay 1 is the weakest column.** The reward arm moves 0.125 across windows
  there against a seed spread of 0.094 — real but not much larger than noise. At
  delay 8 it moves 0.356 against 0.088, which is where the fall-off is decisive.
- **0.25 is still only a quarter of the oracle.** The best cell in the table
  recovers a quarter of the advantage a gate with foresight gets. A tag has to
  beat 0.25, not 0.
- **The trivial floor is 0.125 and no cell was refused for sitting on it**, so
  every ratio above is measured between two arms that both work.
