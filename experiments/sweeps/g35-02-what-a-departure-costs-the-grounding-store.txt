What does a departure cost, when nothing is replicated?
=======================================================

Script: experiments/g35_02_what_a_departure_costs_the_grounding_store.py
Status: PREDICTIONS COMMITTED, NOT YET RUN.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
This project's premise is that machines leaving is **normal**, not an accident.
So the question is not whether the system survives a departure but what one
costs.

The older store kept three copies of everything, so losing a machine mostly cost
redundancy. The grounding store keeps **one**. When a machine goes, everything it
knew about its share of things is gone for good, and nothing anywhere has a copy.

There is a second cost that is easy to miss. To judge whether two things belong
together, a machine has to ask how common the *other* thing is — and if the
machine holding that answer has left, nobody can score it. So a departure damages
things it never held, by making them unjudgeable.

That means a concept is in trouble if **any** of its appearances lived on the
departed machine. A dog known by a picture, a sound and a word has three chances
to be hit rather than one.

**Which raises the uncomfortable question this run is really about.** The whole
point of multiple modalities is that a concept can be reached several ways. Does
that make it more robust to a machine leaving, or less? More ways to reach it, or
more ways to lose part of it? The answer is not obvious and it matters, because
multimodality is a requirement here rather than a feature.


WHAT IS BEING MEASURED
----------------------
    ring share      the departed nodes' share of the network, `lost / nodes`
    surfaces gone   share of surfaces whose owner has left
    concepts hit    share of concepts with AT LEAST ONE surface gone
    f1              recovery, scoring a lost surface as **zero** rather than
                    skipping it. Skipping would average over the survivors,
                    which is a different question and a flattering one
    largest         biggest recovered class, to catch a collapse

An unreachable candidate is **dropped** from a ranking rather than scored zero. A
zero marginal is an ordinary count and would make a departed peer look like a
surface nobody ever saw; dropping is local, graceful, and the only option that
gives a smaller answer rather than a wrong one.


MEASURED ON
-----------
Local, single process, Python 3.14, no numpy. `Federation`, so ownership and the
crossings are real; no sockets, because this is a question about what
INFORMATION is lost and transport cannot change that.

    concepts       32
    nodes           8      carried from g33-03
    occasions   4,000      125 per concept, above g32-02's ~16 threshold
    presence      0.7      carried
    noise           3      carried
    distractors     1      carried
    bound     derived      `g33-04`'s winner, so this measures the arrangement
                           actually in use rather than a superseded one
    statistic conditional  the C1-safe one
    seeds     0, 1, 2

Swept: `surfaces` per concept in {2, 3, 5}, nodes lost in {0, 1, 2, 4}.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
P1  CONTROL, near-arithmetic. `surfaces gone` tracks `ring share` to within
    about 0.06 — consistent hashing spreads keys evenly but a ring is lumpy, and
    `Ring.balance` already exists because of it.
    REFUTED IF the two diverge widely, which would mean ownership is not
    spreading and every other row is about a different arrangement.

P2  A DEPARTURE COSTS MORE THAN ITS SHARE. `concepts hit` is far above
    `surfaces gone`, because a concept is damaged if ANY of its surfaces is
    lost: about `1 - (1 - share)^surfaces`. At 3 surfaces and one node of eight
    that is roughly **0.33 against 0.125**.
    REFUTED IF the two are close, which would mean surfaces of one concept land
    on the same node — and that would be worth knowing, because it would make
    the ring's placement correlated in a way nothing has checked.

P3  IT DEGRADES RATHER THAN COLLAPSING. `f1` falls roughly in step with
    `concepts hit`, and `largest` stays small at every loss level.
    REFUTED IF `largest` rises or `f1` falls off a cliff — either would mean
    dropping unreachable candidates destabilises the walk rather than shrinking
    its answer, and the graceful-degradation claim would be wrong.

P4  MORE MODALITIES MAKES CHURN WORSE, NOT BETTER. At a fixed loss, `concepts
    hit` RISES with `surfaces` — 2, 3, then 5 — because more surfaces is more
    chances one of them was on the departed node. At one node of eight:
    roughly 0.23, 0.33, 0.49.
    REFUTED IF more surfaces helps. **That refutation would be the good news
    and it is worth wanting**: it would mean the redundancy intuition holds and
    multimodality buys fault tolerance for free. If it does not, then
    `GOALS.md`'s multimodal requirement and C3 pull against each other, and
    replication stops being an optimisation and becomes a prerequisite.
