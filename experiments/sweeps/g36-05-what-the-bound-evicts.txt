What does the bound evict when two senses arrive together?
===========================================================

Script: experiments/g36_05_what_the_bound_evicts.py
Status: RUN, local, 2026-07-31, 9.8s. **This is a DIAGNOSTIC and its prediction
        is WEAK** -- see the section below, which says exactly what had already
        been seen when the prediction was written.

ANSWER: **The word is evicted, almost completely.** When a picture and a sound
        share every occasion, the word survives the derived bound for **2.00%**
        of image codes, against **98.67%** when the picture is alone and 97.97%
        when the two senses alternate. Of the 2.05 partners the bound keeps,
        **2.03 are audio codes.**


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
The previous run found something odd. If the machine sees a picture and hears the
digit spoken *at the same moment, every time*, it learns the connection to the
written word **worse** than if it sees the picture and hears the sound at
*different* times.

That is backwards from what anyone would guess, so this run asks why, rather than
leaving a guess in the record.

The answer is a budget problem. Each thing keeps only a handful of connections --
the ones that stand out clearly above the rest. When the picture and the sound
always arrive together, they become each other's most obvious connection, and
they use up the whole budget between them. **The word gets pushed out.**

So the machine ends up knowing that this picture goes with that sound, and losing
track of what either of them is called.

That matters beyond this experiment. It says the natural fix for a related
problem -- letting two things that share a name connect directly to each other --
is not free. It is paid for with the connection to the name.


WHAT IS BEING MEASURED
----------------------
    word in list    share of image codes whose OWN word survives the bound
    mean rank       where that word sits in the FULL ranking, 1 being best. A
                    word that fell out of the bound is still located, rather
                    than being scored as absent, so the two columns say
                    different things
    mean bound      how many partners the derived cliff keeps
    audio in list   audio codes kept, per image code

**This is the quantity the claim is about, not a downstream proxy for it.**
`g36-04` already reported the end-to-end loss; a second end-to-end number could
not have said which of several mechanisms produced it.

PREDICTIONS
-----------
P1  THE WORD IS EVICTED. `word in list` in `together` is far below the other two
    arms -- below 0.30 against something near 1.0 -- and `audio in list` is
    close to `mean bound`, meaning the kept partners are almost entirely the
    other sense.
    REFUTED IF `word in list` is comparable across arms. The eviction account
    would then be wrong, `g36-04`'s explanation would have to be withdrawn, and
    the loss would have to be sought in the walk rather than the bound.
    **WEAK: a single-seed version of this was seen before it was written.**

P2  THE BOUND DOES NOT SIMPLY GET BIGGER. `mean bound` in `together` is under 3
    -- the cliff does not respond to a second sense by keeping more partners, it
    responds by keeping DIFFERENT ones.
    REFUTED IF `mean bound` roughly doubles in `together`, which would mean the
    derived rule is accommodating the extra modality and the loss is elsewhere.

SCORECARD
---------
  P1  HELD, by a margin that leaves nothing to interpret. `word in list` is
            **0.0200** in `together` against 0.9867 and 0.9797 -- one image code
            in fifty keeps its own word, where essentially all of them do
            otherwise. The word's mean rank falls from 1.04 to **6.70**, so it is
            not marginally excluded, it is sixth or seventh in line.

            And `audio in list` is **2.03 of a 2.05 bound**: the kept partners
            are the other sense and virtually nothing else.

  P2  HELD. `mean bound` moves 1.09 -> 2.05, not 1.09 -> 6.7. **The cliff keeps
            roughly one partner per modality present.** It does not widen to
            accommodate a second sense; it substitutes.

            That is the derived rule behaving exactly as `g33-04` described --
            it finds the biggest gap in the ranking, and when two senses are
            locked together the gap falls immediately below them.


WHAT THIS CHANGES, AND IT IS A DESIGN DECISION
-----------------------------------------------
**Do not build spoke-to-spoke connection as a fix for the hub problem.**

The natural response to a hub with many spokes is a mechanism letting two spokes
notice they share a hub and link directly. `together` IS that arrangement,
arrived at from the data side rather than the mechanism side, and this measures
what it costs: the direct spoke-to-spoke edge is bought with the spoke-to-hub
edge, because the bound is a budget rather than a threshold.

`g36-04` measures the consequence end to end -- the bridged route beats the
direct route at every code count, 0.9580 against 0.8853 at 50 codes -- and this
measures the cause.

**What remains genuinely open** is the middle of the axis. `together` puts both
senses in 100% of occasions and `alternating` in 0%. Nothing here says what
happens at 10% or 30%, and the realistic answer is somewhere in there. That is
one parameter and it is not swept.


WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE
-------------------------
**One code count and one statistic.** 50 codes and `conditional`. The eviction
argument should hold for any bounded rule and any statistic that ranks a
perfectly-co-occurring partner highly, but that is reasoning rather than
measurement.

**Two senses, not many.** With five senses locked together the budget argument
predicts the word is evicted harder. Not run.

**The bound is the derived one.** A fixed `k` large enough to hold both senses
and the word would not evict -- at the cost `g33-02` measured, which is that a
`k` large enough for a hub admits noise everywhere else.
