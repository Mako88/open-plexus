How far does pooling actually stretch?
======================================

Script: experiments/g5_03_finer.py (same script, wider grid)
Status: PREDICTIONS WRITTEN, NOT YET RUN


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
There are two ways to get an answer out of the network: ask one machine, or add
up what all of them say. Everything measured so far has used the strict version --
one machine, alone, no help -- and by that measure the news was bad: harder
problems force bigger machines faster than they force more total capacity, so the
number of machines you can usefully split across falls.

The lenient version looked far better. On a problem where a lone machine needs 20
slots, the pooled answer worked with 6.

But "worked with 6" is where the last grid ran out. **Six was the smallest machine
tested, so at three of the four problem sizes the pooled answer was never actually
broken.** That is a bound, not a measurement, and this project has now twice
mistaken one for the other.

So: keep going down until pooling breaks, and find out whether it holds up as
problems get harder or merely starts from a better place.


QUESTION
--------
g5-03 located the `alone` floor at every length and fitted seq_len^0.67. The
`pooled` floor was located at only one length -- (20, 24] at seq_len 384 -- and
sat below the grid at the other three.

Same design, grid extended downward to machine width 3.

  total width  240 (fixed)
  P            240, 120, 60, 40, 30, 24, 20, 16, 12, 10, 8, 6
               -> machine width 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 20, 24, 30, 40
  seq_len      96, 128, 192, 256, 384
  lr           0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2
  seeds        1, 2, 3

240 jobs, under GitHub's 256-job matrix cap.

**THE PRE-DISPATCH CONTROL REDESIGNED THIS SWEEP.** The first version ran
seq_len 48/96/192/384 with machine widths down to 4, and would have wasted more
than half the matrix. The control found that at seq_len 48 and 96 pooling is
**1.000 at machine width 1** -- 240 machines each holding a single number, and
still perfect. Those two rows have no locatable floor at all; the constraint does
not exist there. Fitting them would have meant two points of "1" and no
information.

So seq_len 48 is dropped and 128 and 256 added, putting four of five rows in the
range where a floor actually exists. `lr` drops to four values, losing 0.01 --
which g5-03 selected only on rows that were at ceiling, where the choice was
tie-breaking rather than evidence.

**Pre-registered: the fit is on `pooled` this time**, since that is the quantity
in question. `alone` is reported alongside and its g5-03 values should reproduce
where the grids overlap -- which is a free replication check on a result that has
already changed twice.


WHY THE ANSWER MAY BE WORSE THAN IT LOOKS
-------------------------------------------
Pooling uses every dimension regardless of how the width is split, so on signal
grounds it should not care about P at all. It does care, and the reason is
learning rather than signal: **each group fits its readout to its own slice from
its own error**, so P groups produce P redundant estimates rather than one joint
fit. Adding them helps, but it is not the same as never having split.

That predicts pooling degrades with P for a reason that has nothing to do with
sequence length -- and the g5-03 numbers hint the two interact badly:

    seq_len 192   pooled still passing at 40 groups (width 6), 0.904
    seq_len 384   pooled failing by 12 groups (width 20), 0.853

If those are near the true floors, the number of groups pooling tolerates fell
about fourfold across one doubling of sequence length. **That would be a steeper
collapse than the `alone` criterion's, not a gentler one** -- and it would retract
the optimistic reading given when g5-03 was reported.


WHAT THE CONTROL ALREADY SHOWS, at one seed and one learning rate
------------------------------------------------------------------
    seq_len 48    pooled 1.000 at width 1
    seq_len 96    pooled 1.000 at width 1
    seq_len 192   pooled 0.800 at width 4, 0.831 at width 5, 0.904 at width 6
    seq_len 384   pooled floor in (20, 24]   (g5-03)

Against the `alone` floors of 8, 10, 20, 30, that gives pooling an advantage of
roughly 10x at seq 96, 3.3x at 192, and **1.2x at 384**.

  > **Pooling's advantage shrinks as problems get harder, and by 384 steps it has
  > nearly vanished.** That is the opposite of an escape hatch, and it directly
  > contradicts what was said when g5-03 was reported -- that pooling was "the most
  > promising direction". On one seed, at one learning rate. This sweep is what
  > decides whether that stands.


PREDICTIONS -- written before the run
--------------------------------------
1. **The pooled floor is located at 128, 192, 256 and 384, and unlocatable at 96.**
   The control already puts seq 96 at width 1 with no room below it, so that row
   is expected to be reported as a bound and excluded from the fit rather than
   forced into it.

2. **The pooled exponent is STEEPER than the `alone` exponent of 0.67.** Guess:
   1.8 to 2.6, from the control's local slopes -- log(6/1)/log(2) = 2.6 between
   96 and 192, log(24/6)/log(2) = 2.0 between 192 and 384. This contradicts what was said when g5-03 was reported -- that
   pooling was "the most promising direction" -- and if it holds, that statement
   was wrong and gets retracted.

3. **But pooling still wins in absolute terms at every length tested**, because
   it starts from a much lower floor. A worse exponent from a better starting
   point can still be the better option over the range anyone cares about, and
   conflating "worse slope" with "worse choice" would be its own error.

4. **`alone` floors reproduce g5-03's where the grids overlap**: (6,8], (8,10],
   (16,20], (24,30]. The replication control. The grids share ten of twelve P
   values, so any disagreement is seed noise or a bug rather than a difference in
   design.

Most likely wrong: (2). If the pooled exponent comes back at or below 0.67,
pooling is the escape hatch it appeared to be, the machine-count limit is much
looser than reported, and G5's verdict softens. **That is the favourable
direction and it is the one to distrust** -- check that the pooled floors are
genuinely located rather than resting on a marginal cell, since pooling sits near
1.000 across most of the grid and falls off a cliff, which is exactly the shape
that makes a crossing sensitive to noise.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- pooled exponent > 0.67          -> pooling is not an escape. G5's verdict
                                     stands and the optimistic remark is retracted
- pooled exponent <= 0.67         -> pooling IS the escape. Distrust, then check
                                     the floors are not marginal
- pooled floor below width 3
  anywhere                        -> grid failed again; report the bound
- `alone` floors disagree with
  g5-03                           -> a bug or seed sensitivity; nothing else here
                                     can be read until it is explained
