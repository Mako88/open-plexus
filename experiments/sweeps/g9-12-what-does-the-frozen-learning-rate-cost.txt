What does the frozen learning rate cost?
========================================

Script: experiments/g9_05_the_tag.py, with --lr OMITTED so the script sweeps it
Status: PRE-REGISTERED.


IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
Every score in this line is a fraction: how much of a cheating filter's advantage
a real one recovers. The denominator is how much better the cheat is than no
filter at all — and that depends on how fast the model learns, a setting picked
once for a different configuration and carried through seven experiments
untouched.

We know it matters. On an older experiment the same cell had a denominator of
0.20 at one learning rate and 0.61 at another. And last night a *different*
carried setting was measured at 0.58 of recovery — more than twice the largest
effect any mechanism here has produced.

So this asks the plain question: at the configurations actually being run, is
0.05 the right rate, and how much are the published fractions off if it is not?


WHY THIS RUN EXISTS
--------------------
[Note 028](../../docs/notes/028-the-learning-rate-has-been-frozen-for-seven-sweeps.md).
`lr 0.05, FIXED on every arm` appears in the grid of g9-05 through g9-11 — every
sweep in the line. It was chosen for g9-03's workflow at `d_model` 32 in one
process, and carried through every change of width, node count, capacity, fade
and reach since.

[g8-01's re-summarisation](../../GOALS.md) measured the rate moving the FLOOR arm
— the denominator of every ratio — by a factor of three (gap 0.196 at lr 0.02
against 0.612 at lr 0.1). GOALS was corrected for exactly that.

And [g9-11](g9-11-how-far-should-the-union-reach.txt) measured what one carried
constant costs when it is wrong: **0.58 of recovery at delay 8**, from `slots`
alone.

**The cost here is unusually low.** `g9_05_the_tag.py` sweeps
`LEARNING_RATES = (0.02, 0.05, 0.1)` inside a job whenever `--lr` is omitted, so
dropping the flag triples the compute PER JOB and adds no jobs at all.


THE GRID
---------
  partitions  1, 2, 4, 8      node holds 64, 32, 16, 8. g9-09's shape, because
                              node width is where the floor arm moves most
                              (0.648 down to 0.171) and therefore where a
                              mis-chosen rate does the most damage
  lr          0.02, 0.05, 0.1 SWEPT, inside each job
  delay       8, ONLY         because `slots` 16 is correct at delay 8 and NOT
                              at delay 20 (g9-10: best is 32 there). Running
                              delay 20 would measure a mistuned capacity and
                              confound the question
  slots       16, FIXED       from g9-10, at delay 8, at every node width it
                              tested
  fade        0.95, FIXED     from g9-06, at d_model 32 in one process. STILL
                              carried, still a risk, and named rather than
                              hidden
  width       64, FIXED       so the node is 64 // partitions
  reach       8, FIXED        from g9-11, which measured 8 as the MATCHED reach
                              at delay 8 (the window's cliff sits exactly there:
                              -1.53 below it, +0.23 at it). This happens to equal
                              `REWARD_WINDOW`, so it would have arrived by import
                              anyway — and it is pinned on the command line
                              regardless, because a constant that is right by
                              luck is still a constant nobody checked
  arms        none, oracle, reward, tag, tag-strongest, combined
  seeds       1, 2, 3

4 jobs, 54 cells each. g9-09 ran 15 cells a job at this width in about ten
minutes, so call it forty minutes a job against a 90-minute timeout.


PREDICTIONS
------------
1. **The best rate differs by node width.** Guess: it FALLS as the node narrows
   — 0.1 at node 64 down to 0.02 at node 8 — because a narrower reader retrieves
   more noisily and a smaller step averages more of that away. This is reasoning
   rather than measurement and is the guess most likely to be backwards.

2. **THE ONE THAT MATTERS: recovery at each node's best rate differs from its
   value at 0.05 by more than 0.05, at at least one node width.** If it does not,
   note 028's concern is real in principle and negligible in practice, and that
   should be said as plainly as the warning was.

3. **The ORDERING of arms is unchanged at every rate.** `combined` >= `tag` >
   `tag-strongest`, and `reward` above all of them at this matched reach. Note
   028 asserted that ordinal findings survive a wrong rate because every arm in a
   cell shares it. That is an argument, not a measurement, and this checks it.

4. **The floor arm moves by more than 0.15 across rates at some node width**,
   reproducing g8-01's finding at a configuration four sweeps away from it.

Most likely wrong: **1**, specifically its direction. The opposite story — a
narrow node needs a LARGER step because it has fewer dimensions to accumulate
evidence in — is just as plausible and I cannot distinguish them from the desk.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- best rate varies and recovery moves  -> every published fraction in the g9 line
                                          is off by an unknown amount, and the
                                          line needs re-baselining at a tuned
                                          rate before any of it is quoted
- best rate varies, recovery does not  -> the ratio is robust to the rate even
                                          though the floor is not, which would be
                                          a genuinely reassuring result and a
                                          non-obvious one
- 0.05 is best everywhere              -> the carried constant was lucky. Say so,
                                          and stop treating it as a risk
- the arm ORDERING changes with rate   -> note 028's central reassurance is
                                          wrong, and every comparison in the line
                                          needs re-checking, not just the scales


COST
-----
4 jobs. Tripling the cells per job rather than the number of jobs is the whole
reason this is cheap, and it is why there was never a good excuse for not doing
it.


THE ORACLE, SAID TWICE
-----------------------
The `oracle` arm reads `position_kinds()` and is a CEILING. It is also the
numerator's other end: if the rate moves the oracle as well as the floor, the
ratio can be stable while both ends move, and the summary prints both.
