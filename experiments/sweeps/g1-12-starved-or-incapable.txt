Is attention undertrained at long sequences, or genuinely too small?
=====================================================================

Script: experiments/g1_12_starved.py
Status: RUN on Actions, 12/12 cells. PREDICTIONS 1, 2 AND 3 ALL HELD.
        ATTENTION WAS STARVED. g1-11's favourable result is an artifact of
        undertraining and BOTH of its conclusions are retracted.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
The last experiment produced a result in our favour: measured by memory rather
than width, our approach uses a fifth of what the conventional one does on long
inputs.

Before that goes anywhere, it has to survive an obvious objection. The favourable
number came from the conventional model doing BADLY on long inputs. But we gave
it the same amount of training at every length -- and a longer input has the same
handful of questions buried in far more padding, so the useful part of each
training step is diluted.

So it might not be too small. It might just be under-trained, and we would be
taking credit for our own stopwatch.

This trains it four times longer and finds out. We wrote down before the previous
run that we would do exactly this if the result came out in our favour, and it
did, so here it is.


QUESTION
--------
g1-11 measured attention at width 8 scoring 0.985, 0.999, 0.850, 0.252 as seq_len
goes 48, 96, 192, 384 -- all at a fixed 3000 training steps. That degradation
drove its crossing from 7.4 to 15.1 and produced g1-11's 5.12x memory advantage.

Train attention at seq_len 192 and 384, widths 8 and 16, for 12000 steps with
checkpoints at 1500, 3000, 6000 and 12000. 3 seeds.


PREDICTIONS -- written before the run
--------------------------------------
1. **d=8 at seq_len=384 climbs substantially with more steps.** This is the
   uncomfortable prediction and it is the one I expect. The gradient signal is
   diluted rather than absent, and dilution is a rate problem.

2. It reaches 0.9 by 12000 steps. If so, attention's crossing at 384 is ~8 rather
   than 15.1, prediction 1 of g1-11 was right after all, and **g1-11's memory
   ratios are inflated and must be recomputed.** The corrected ratio at 384 would
   be 2*384*8 / 2256 = 2.72x rather than 5.12x -- still favourable, still growing,
   but half what was reported.

3. seq_len=192 at d=8 also climbs, from 0.850 to above 0.9. It is much closer to
   the line so this should be quick.

4. The local rule is NOT exposed to the same confound in the same way, but this
   is worth stating rather than assuming: g1-10 trained it for a fixed 8 epochs
   over 400 sequences at every seq_len, so it has the same fixed-budget structure.
   Its accuracy did NOT collapse at long sequences at adequate width -- 0.992 at
   d=48, seq_len=384 -- so it is not obviously starved. But if attention turns out
   to have been starved, the honest follow-up is to check the local rule at a
   longer budget too, and that is recorded here as owed.

Most likely to be wrong: (2)'s endpoint. d=8 may climb and plateau below 0.9,
which would mean the crossing genuinely grows and g1-11 stands. That is the
outcome I would prefer and therefore the one to hold most loosely.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- d=8 at 384 reaches 0.9    -> attention was STARVED. g1-11's memory ratios are
                               inflated, must be recomputed, and the width ratio
                               becomes the more reliable of the two numbers
- d=8 plateaus below 0.9    -> attention is genuinely capacity-limited at long
                               sequences. g1-11 stands as written
- d=8 still climbing at
  12000                     -> no plateau demonstrated; report a bound, not a
                               number, and say the budget was insufficient


RESULT
------
3 seeds per cell, attention at init_scale 0.4.

  seq_len  width     1500     3000     6000    12000
      192      8    0.074    0.850    1.000    1.000
      192     16    0.642    0.987    1.000    1.000
      384      8    0.006    0.252    1.000    1.000
      384     16    0.036    0.987    1.000    1.000

The 3000 column is what g1-11 measured.


ATTENTION WAS STARVED, DECISIVELY
----------------------------------
At seq_len=384, width 8: **0.252 at 3000 steps, 1.000 at 6000.** Double the
budget and a number read as a capacity limit becomes perfect. Every cell reaches
1.000 by 6000 steps and none moves after.

So attention at width 8 handles a 384-step sequence completely. It was never too
small. g1-11 stopped training before it got there and read the result as a
property of the architecture.


WHAT THIS RETRACTS
-------------------
Both of g1-11's conclusions, and its own predictions 1 and 2 -- which it had
recorded as REFUTED -- turn out to have been right.

Recomputed with attention crossing at <=8 at every length:

  seq_len  d_local  d_att   width x   local mem  att mem   memory x
       48     21.8      8      2.7x         475      768      1.62x
       96     26.0      8      3.2x         676     1536      2.27x
      192     34.0      8      4.2x        1156     3072      2.66x
      384     47.5      8      5.9x        2256     6144      2.72x

  g1-11 said the WIDTH ratio was flat at 3.1x. It GROWS, 2.7x to 5.9x.
  g1-11 said the MEMORY ratio reached 5.12x. It tops out at 2.72x.

**Both corrections go against the local rule.** The width price is worse than
g1-11 reported and grows with sequence length; the memory advantage is roughly
half what it claimed.


WHY THE CHECK EXISTED, AND WHY IT FIRED
-----------------------------------------
g1-11 wrote this before running:

  "That would be a better result for us, which is exactly why it deserves the
  most suspicion. If attention's crossing grows, check the attention training is
  not simply undertrained at long sequences before believing anything."

The condition occurred, the check ran, and the check was right. Had the rule been
"investigate surprising results", g1-11's outcome was not surprising -- it was
exactly what the arithmetic had predicted, arriving in the direction hoped for,
which is the least likely thing to get questioned.

**The rule that worked was specifically: distrust the favourable direction, and
write down the check before you know which direction you will get.**


WHAT IS STILL OWED, AND IT IS NOT OPTIONAL
--------------------------------------------
g1-12's prediction 4 recorded it in advance: the LOCAL rule has the same
fixed-budget structure. g1-10 trained it for 8 epochs over 400 sequences at every
sequence length, exactly as attention got a fixed 3000 steps.

g1-07 found the local rule converges within one epoch and is flat to 64 -- but at
seq_len=96 only. Nothing has checked it at 384.

**Fixing attention's budget and not the local rule's would be repairing one arm
of a comparison, which CLAUDE.md now forbids by a rule that this project earned
the hard way.** g1-13 re-measures both crossings at budgets verified adequate on
each side. Until it runs, the corrected table above is the best available and is
itself provisional -- it uses g1-10's local crossings, which were measured under
exactly the condition that just invalidated attention's.
