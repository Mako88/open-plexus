It makes an 8-slot pool WORK where the absolute version sat at zero, and it does
essentially nothing at 32 where the tag already works. That is the third time
this line has produced the same shape: **every improvement to the SIGNAL pays
only when the pool is starved.** g9-06 found it for the ranking's direction
(+0.222 at slots 16 fade 0.99, +0.003 at the working point); this is the same
curve for the ranking's units.

That is not a disappointment. It is the tiny-node result: the things that look
like decoration at a comfortable capacity are exactly what make a small pool
viable, and a small pool is the only kind this project can deploy.

**And at fade 1.0 it is transformative without being enough.** -0.19/-0.22/-0.18
becomes -0.00/-0.01/-0.00 at slots 32: the cold-start confound was worth about
0.19, and removing it takes an actively harmful arm to exactly neutral. It does
not take it positive at any capacity. So the fade is doing something the
normalisation cannot, which is prediction 3 and which keeps note 023's reach
argument standing.

IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
The marking rule was "mark the writes whose lookup came back weak", because a
fresh piece of information has nothing stored under it yet. That works, but it
was measuring the wrong thing: a lookup also comes back weak when the *memory* is
nearly empty, and the memory is nearly emptiest right after it has just been
cleared out. So the rule spent its whole budget marking the first few things
after every clear-out, every time, regardless of what they were.

The fix is one division. Ask not "was this lookup small" but "was this lookup
small **for a memory this size**". No new signal, no new dial, no clock.

If it works, the method finally does the thing it was built for: work well AND
not care how long ago the important thing happened. Every version so far has done
one or the other.


WHY THIS EXISTS
----------------
[g9-05](g9-05-a-tag-that-fades.txt) is the reason and its result was clean:
**the tag's rows that are flat are flat at zero, and its rows that are positive
carry the window's cliff.** No cell is both.

The un-faded tag is the extreme case, at -0.18 to -0.22 everywhere -- worse than
not gating. [Note 023](../../docs/notes/023-two-signals-and-only-one-of-them-is-about-value.md)
diagnosed why: retrieval magnitude scales with store size, the store is smallest
just after a capture, so an absolute ranking reads the opening writes of every
interval as the weakest it will ever see.

Measured on a three-interval stream, an absolute tag of four marks the final
interval at offsets **0.00, 0.01, 0.03 and 0.05**. Not approximately the start.
The start. `tests/test_tag.py` pins it.

`tag_decay` was hiding that rather than fixing it -- a fade ages the cold-start
marks out, which is why fading rescued the mechanism and why the fade ends up
doing two jobs and can be tuned for only one at a time.

`tag_relative` divides the strength by the norm of the store **that produced the
retrieval**, one step before the write being ranked. CLAUDE.md rule 7 read in the
direction it usually is not: a criterion that fails to normalise against
something moving with it is blind the same way as one that cancels its own input.

against a guess of 10), 3 held.

PREDICTIONS
------------
1. **Relative beats absolute at every delay at fade 0.99.** The control says it
   captures more at every delay, and g9-05 says capture and recovery move
   together everywhere except the un-faded arm. Guess: +0.05 or better against
   g9-05's +0.00/-0.02/-0.03 at slots 8.

2. **There is a row that is both flat and positive.** THE PREDICTION THIS RUN
   EXISTS FOR. Guess: at fade 0.99 and some capacity, mean above +0.10 with a
   spread under 0.20, against the window's spread of 0.47. If no row manages
   both, the tag is not a gate that can ignore the delay and note 023's
   combined-gate conclusion stands unchallenged.

3. **Relative with NO fade is still not usable, and stays at or below zero.**
   The control has it at 31/28/19 against a faded 66/59/44. Normalising removes
   the store-size confound; it does not give a mark any reason to expire. If
   fade 1.0 comes out positive, the fade was ONLY ever a cold-start corrector
   and the reach argument in note 023 is wrong.

4. **Delay 20 is positive somewhere, for the first time in this line of work.**
   Nothing has managed it: the window is -0.24, the best absolute tag -0.03.
   The control puts relative+fade at 44% capture there against the window's 0%.
   Guess: above +0.05 in at least one cell.

5. **`tag` still beats `tag-strongest`**, mean above +0.05 at every delay.

6. **Registered after g9-06 and before dispatch, so it is a weaker test than
   1-5 and is marked as one: the relative tag reaches g9-06's +0.16 at a
   SMALLER capacity than 32.** Guess: +0.14 or better at slots 16. This is the
   tiny-node question. If a better signal does not buy a smaller pool, then the
   signal is not worth its division and bounded capacity plus a fade is the
   whole mechanism.

Most likely wrong: **4.** Capture is not recovery -- g9-05's un-faded arm
captured 9% and scored -0.20, and its fade-0.95 arm captured 100% at delay 1 and
scored exactly what the window scored. A capture rate at delay 20 that beats zero
is a low bar when the alternative kept nothing at all.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- flat AND positive at some cell     -> the tag is a gate that does not need to
                                        be told the delay, which nothing in this
                                        project has been. Take it forward
- relative helps but still trades    -> the store-size confound was real and was
  flatness against height               not the binding constraint. Note 023's
                                        combined gate is the next build
- fade 1.0 comes out positive        -> the fade was only ever correcting the
                                        cold start, and the reach story in note
                                        023 is wrong about its own mechanism
- relative changes nothing at all    -> the divisor is not connected. Nothing
                                        else in the run counts; check the
                                        mutation first
