What is the chain length worth, and can the walk do without it?
===============================================================

Script: experiments/g42_01_the_walk_is_not_told_its_own_depth.py
Status: PREDICTIONS REGISTERED BEFORE THE MECHANISM EXISTS. Nothing has been run.

**This is the strong form of the pre-registration and it is deliberate.** g41-01's
predictions were written after a timing probe had shown that cell, and its record
says so. Here the depth-free mechanism has not been written yet, so nothing about
its behaviour has been observed by anyone.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
When this system answers a CLUTRR puzzle it walks along a chain of relationships:
*A is B's father, B is C's sister, so A is C's ...*. To do that it has to know
when to stop walking.

At the moment we tell it. The puzzle is read ahead of time, the true chain is
counted, and the walk is handed that number. Published systems on this benchmark
are not given it.

That is a real advantage and nobody has measured how large it is. It might be
worth almost nothing — the walk might be finding the right answer for the right
reasons and the number is just a convenience. Or it might be doing most of the
work, in which case our headline figure is a measurement of a hint rather than of
the system.

So this run does two things. It measures **what the hint is worth**, by handing
the walk a deliberately wrong length and watching what happens. And it tries to
**do without it**, by letting the walk decide for itself when it has arrived.

The rule for deciding is not new information: a CLUTRR question names both people
it is asking about, so the walk already knows where it is trying to get to. It can
simply stop when it gets there. Whether that works is the measurement.

A large fall here would be the most useful bad news available, because it would
mean the number this project is about to quote was propped up by something the
comparison systems do not get.


WHAT IS BEING MEASURED
----------------------
Every quantity from g41-01, unchanged, so the two are directly comparable:
per hop bucket 2-10, never pooled; subsets `all` and `clean` (note 059's
max_appearances <= 2); the ACHIEVABLE floor as an arm; 8 seeds.

Added here:

    chosen length   how long a walk the depth-free arm actually commits to, and
                    how often that equals the true chain length. **The
                    diagnostic**, because end-task accuracy alone cannot say
                    whether a fall is the walk stopping early or the fold
                    naming it wrong
    fold arm        `delta` throughout, with `random` and `wrong-delta` carried
                    as the bar and the control

THE DEPTH ARMS

    told            depth = len(chain). The incumbent. Reproduces g41-01
    wrong-1         depth = len(chain) + 1. **The instrument that prices the
                    aid.** Not a mechanism anyone would ship -- it exists to
                    turn "the walk is told its depth" into a number
    free-10         depth is a MAXIMUM of 10, the deepest chain in the data.
                    The walk scores its own endpoint against the query's object
                    at every hop and selects across lengths
    free-15         the same at a maximum of 15, so the budget overshoots the
                    data. Separates "it works" from "it works because the
                    budget happened to be the answer"

**`free-*` still receives a budget, and that is a residual aid, named.** A
maximum is much weaker than an exact length, and `free-15` is what says whether
the maximum is load-bearing at all. It is not claimed to be aid-free.

**The stopping rule uses nothing the task does not supply.** A CLUTRR query names
its object, and `openplexus/search.py` already selects the final walk by scoring
its endpoint against that object -- its own docstring calls the object "the
disambiguator". What changes is only that the score is consulted at every hop
instead of at one.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
P1  **THE AID IS WORTH A LOT.** `wrong-1` end task falls below the `random` fill
    bar at 6 hops and deeper, subset `all`.
    REFUTED IF `wrong-1` stays within 0.05 of `told` everywhere. Then depth was
    never load-bearing, removing it is cheap by construction, and g41-01's number
    was not flattered by it -- which is the cheap outcome and the one that would
    let the incumbent figure stand as it is.

P2  **THE DEPTH-FREE ARM RECOVERS MOST OF IT AND NOT ALL.** `free-10`'s `delta`
    accuracy at 10 hops, subset `all`, lands strictly between the `random` bar
    and `told`.
    REFUTED IF it meets or beats `told`. Then the exact length was never adding
    anything and the honest report is that the aid was cosmetic.
    ALSO REFUTED IF it falls at or below `random`. Then the walk cannot find its
    own end and the composition result depends on being told the answer's shape.

P3  **ENTITY REPETITION IS WHAT COSTS THE DEPTH-FREE ARM.** The gap between
    subset `clean` and subset `all` is LARGER for `free-10` than for `told`, at
    8 hops and deeper. The reasoning: a repeated entity is what creates a short
    path that lands on the target without being the true chain, and only an arm
    free to stop early can take one.
    REFUTED IF the two gaps agree within 0.03. Then short spurious paths are not
    the failure mode and whatever `free-10` loses is lost for another reason.

P4  **THE BUDGET IS NOT THE ANSWER.** `free-15` agrees with `free-10` within
    0.03 at every bucket.
    REFUTED IF `free-15` is markedly worse. Then the maximum is doing real work,
    the arm is not depth-free in any useful sense, and the residual aid named
    above is the whole mechanism.

P5  **THE CHOSEN LENGTH IS RIGHT MORE OFTEN THAN THE END TASK IS.** `free-10`
    picks the true chain length more often than it answers correctly, at every
    bucket from 4 hops up.
    REFUTED IF the end task is more often right than the length is. That would
    mean walks of the wrong length are still folding to the right answer, which
    would make the length a poor diagnostic and P3's reasoning unreadable.

P1 is written expecting the EXPENSIVE outcome, which is the opposite of g37-03's
P1 and is deliberate: the cheap reading here is that the aid is worthless, and
nothing about a walk that scores 1.0000 recovery at 2-5 hops when handed its
depth suggests that.

SCORECARD
---------
  P1  **HELD, and far more strongly than written.** The prediction was that
            `wrong-1` falls below the `random` bar at 6 hops and deeper. It falls
            below it at EVERY depth, and lands near the achievable floor
            throughout: **0.1649 at 10 hops against `told`'s 0.9076.**

            **One extra hop takes the mechanism from 0.91 to 0.16.** The walk is
            not mildly assisted by being told its depth; it is almost entirely
            dependent on it. This reproduces decision 85's *"overshoot scores
            0.000 in every direction"* for the model's own `hops` on a different
            object, which the record said could not be inherited and had to be
            measured. It has now been measured.

  P2  **HELD ON THE ARITHMETIC AND VOIDED BY P4.** `free-10` at 10 hops is
            0.8666, which does land strictly between the bar (0.4643) and `told`
            (0.9076), exactly as predicted.

            **It holds for a reason that makes it worthless.** At 10 hops the
            budget IS the answer — `free-10` cannot walk further than 10 and the
            true chain is 10 — so the arm is not finding its own end there, it is
            being handed it by the grid. `free-15`, which has no such coincidence,
            gives **0.4254** in the same bucket.

            Recorded as held because that is what the prediction said, and
            recorded as uninformative because that is what it is.

  P3  **REFUTED.** The clean/all gap is larger for `free-10` than for `told` in
            **1 of 3** buckets from 8 hops up, not 3 of 3, and the two do not
            agree within 0.03 either. So entity repetition is not what costs the
            depth-free arm, and the reasoning behind the prediction — that a
            repeated entity offers a short spurious path only a length-free arm
            can take — is not supported.

  P4  **REFUTED, and it is the finding.** `free-15` differs from `free-10` by
            more than 0.03 in **9 of 9** buckets, by as much as **0.4412** at 10
            hops and 0.375 at 2. The two budgets do not agree anywhere.

            **So the budget is doing the work, and the arm is not depth-free in
            any useful sense.** The residual aid this record named in advance
            turned out to be the whole mechanism.

  P5  **REFUTED, and in the direction that says the diagnostic is sound.**
            `free-10` names the true chain length far LESS often than it answers
            correctly — 0.3508 against 0.8666 at 10 hops, 0.0654 against 0.4416
            at 6. Walks of the wrong length routinely fold to the right answer.

            That is the additive invariant absorbing the error: a wrong route
            with the right total displacement still names the right relation. It
            also means end-task accuracy on the `free` arms cannot be read as
            evidence that the walk found the chain, which is why the length
            column was collected.


A HYPOTHESIS RAISED AND REFUTED IN THE SAME HOUR
------------------------------------------------
The obvious explanation for `free-10` preferring long walks is that `beam` ranks
by `endpoint @ target`, **unnormalised**, so a larger-norm endpoint wins on
magnitude rather than direction. Same-length walks share a scale and it cancels;
across lengths it need not.

Measured, 40 puzzles at 3 hops, budget 10:

    walk length   1      2      3      5     10
    mean |endpoint|   0.4086  0.4263  0.5369  0.6079  0.6602

The norm does grow with length, and the beam picked length 10 in **28 of 40**
puzzles whose true chain is 3. So the hypothesis looked right.

**Ranking by COSINE instead changes nothing.** True-length accuracy, raw against
normalised:

    hops       3       6      10
    raw     0.1500  0.0500  0.4750
    cosine  0.1500  0.0500  0.4250

**So the norm correlates with length without deciding the choice**, and
normalising is not the repair. `CLAUDE.md` calls a diagnosis a claim about
behaviour needing the same evidence as any other; this one cost two minutes to
refute and would have become the next thing built.


WHAT THIS MEANS FOR KILL-LIST #1
--------------------------------
**`g41-01`'s 0.9076 depends on being told the answer's shape, and this project
currently has no way to remove that aid.** The end-task figure and the aid are
not separable by anything measured here.

That is the expensive outcome and it was the one P1 was written expecting.

**What survives.** The mechanism composes a chain and names it, at 0.9076 on the
deepest bucket of a published split, GIVEN the chain's length. That is a real
capability and it is not nothing — but it is a statement about a system with an
oracle attached, and the comparison systems in `g37-01`'s borrowed table do not
have one.

**What is now open, and it is a better question than the one this run asked.**
Not *"can the walk find its own end by scoring endpoints"* — measured, no — but
*"is there any signal available at a hop that says this is the end"*. The endpoint
score is one candidate and it has been refuted. `halt_gate` is a second, learned
rather than computed, and it has never been pointed at a traversal.
