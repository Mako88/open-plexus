================================================================

Script: experiments/g9_05_the_tag.py, with --slots swept upward
Status: RUN. 23 of 24 cells (slots 64 fade 0.95 delay 8 did not return).
Run:    https://github.com/Mako88/open-plexus/actions/runs/30240589408

        that is both flat and positive. It is also the row where the signal's

------------------------------------------------------------------

works" from "any capacity works":

    slots 32 fade 0.99   +0.146   [+0.12, +0.12, +0.16, +0.19]
    slots 16 fade 0.95   +0.087   [+0.01, +0.01, +0.07, +0.26]
    slots 64 fade 0.99   +0.044   [+0.02, +0.05, +0.04, +0.06]

    slots 32 fade 0.95   +0.003   [-0.01, +0.00, +0.02, +0.00]

**The flat-and-positive row is the row where admitting the strongest scores the
same as admitting the weakest.** g9-05's own refutation table named this outcome:
*capacity is the whole mechanism and the signal is decoration*. Here it is true
of one cell and false of others -- at slots 16 fade 0.99 the direction is worth
+0.22, which is the difference between working and not.

So the honest split, and it is not the one either sweep set out to test:

    the MECHANISM   bounded capacity over writes, plus a fade
                    -> flat across delay and positive. Real, and new
    the SIGNAL      g9-04's inverted retrieval strength
                    -> buys height where capacity is SCARCE, and buys
                       nothing at the capacity where the mechanism works best

A gate does not get to choose scarce capacity for the sake of the signal, so the
operative reading is that the signal is not carrying the result. What it is
carrying is a way to do more with less, which is John's tiny-node question and
not this sweep's.

THE FADE AXIS PINNED THIS TIME
-------------------------------
`tools/grid.py`:

    slots: (clean -- an interior optimum at 16 to 32)
    fade:  all 4 arms chose an edge of the grid [0.95, 0.99] (4 at the bottom,
           0.95); the optimum is outside it and every arm is under-tuned

Fixing one pinned axis exposed another, which is what dropping fade 0.9 and 1.0
from this grid cost. The +0.16 is therefore a lower bound in the same way
g9-05's numbers were. [g9-07](g9-07-a-tag-that-knows-how-big-its-store-is.txt)
carries fade 0.9 for this reason as well as its own.


IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
Last run, the model was given a budget of "remember 4 things" or "remember 8
things", and 8 was better every single time. That means we never found out what
the right number is -- we only found out it is at least 8. Any conclusion about
how good this method can get was really a conclusion about how good it is when
starved.

So this raises the budget to 16, 32 and 64 and asks the one question that
decides whether the method is worth keeping: **is there a budget at which it
works well AND works equally well whether the thing to remember was four steps
ago or forty?** Last time we could get one or the other and never both.

If a bigger budget buys both, the method survives and does something no
alternative here does. If it buys neither, the idea is finished and we go and
build the thing the last run pointed at instead.


WHY THIS RUN EXISTS
--------------------
[g9-05](g9-05-a-tag-that-fades.txt) returned 32 of 32 cells with no refusals, and
`tools/grid.py` says the capacity axis **did not contain its own answer**:

    slots: all 4 arms chose an edge of the grid [4, 8] (4 at the top, 8);
           the optimum is outside it and every arm is under-tuned

Every delay picked `slots 8`. So g9-05's tag numbers are **bounds, not values**,
and CLAUDE.md is explicit that a caveat printed next to a number does not attach
to the number -- the code has to refuse to use a bound as one. The fade axis is
clean by the same check.

The direction is already visible within the grid. At fade 0.99 the row mean goes
-0.10 at slots 4 to -0.02 at slots 8; at fade 0.95 it goes +0.02 to +0.11. More
capacity helped at every fade tested.

THE GRID
---------
  slots     16, 32, 64        the axis that pinned; 8 was the old top
  fade      0.99, 0.95        the flat-at-zero row and the best positive row
  delay     1, 4, 8, 20       unchanged, so cells are comparable with g9-05
  arms      none, oracle, reward, tag, tag-strongest
  seeds     1, 2, 3
  lr        0.05, FIXED on every arm

`fade 1.0` and `fade 0.9` are dropped. 1.0 is refuted as harmful at every
capacity tested and is not worth 12 more jobs; 0.9 and 0.95 differ by less than
the seed spread everywhere they are both positive, and 0.95 is the interior
value. Dropping them is a deliberate narrowing and is recorded here rather than
being invisible in the matrix.

**This grid can pin too.** If every delay chooses slots 64, the answer is again
outside, and the honest report is another bound -- which at that point becomes a
finding about the mechanism rather than about the grid, because a tag needing 64
of ~186 writes per interval is not a small store and the tiny-node argument for
it has evaporated.


PREDICTIONS
------------
1. **Recovery rises with capacity and then falls.** A tag holding every write is
   not a gate, so recovery must approach 0 as slots approach the interval length
   (~186 writes). Guess: the fade-0.95 mean peaks at slots 16 or 32 and is lower
   at 64. If it is still rising at 64 the mechanism wants a pool that is not
   small, which is a different result and a worse one for the tiny-node case.

2. **The flat row stays flat and stays near zero.** fade 0.99 spans 0.03 at
   slots 8; predict under 0.10 at every capacity here, and a mean between -0.05
   and +0.10. This is the prediction the whole run is about. **If the fade-0.99
   mean goes above +0.15 while the spread stays under 0.10, the mechanism is
   vindicated and g9-05's refutation was a capacity artefact.**

3. **The cliff at fade 0.95 does not close.** Delay 20 stays negative at every
   capacity. Reason: the fade sets reach in steps, and no amount of room changes
   how far back a mark survives. Guess: delay 20 within [-0.20, 0.00] throughout.

4. **The window is unchanged at 0.23/0.23/0.23/-0.24**, because none of these
   dials touches it. It is a wiring check, not a finding: if it moves, the arms
   are not independent and nothing else in the run counts.

5. **`tag` still beats `tag-strongest`**, by a mean above +0.05 at every delay.
   The four ties in g9-05 were all at fade 1.0, which is not in this grid.

Most likely wrong: **prediction 3.** The fade and the capacity are not as
separable as that reasoning assumes -- with more room, a mark that would have
been displaced early survives longer in practice, so capacity may buy reach
after all.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- flat AND positive at some capacity  -> the mechanism works and g9-05 measured
                                         a starved version of it. This is the
                                         outcome that keeps the tag alive as
                                         something a window cannot do
- rises then falls, never flat        -> capacity is not the missing piece. The
  and positive together                  tag and the window are two halves of
                                         one gate (note 023) and the next build
                                         is the gate that reads both signals
- still rising at slots 64            -> the tag needs a pool that is not small,
                                         which costs the tiny-node argument that
                                         motivated a capacity over a span
- window moves from 0.23/-0.24        -> the arms share state. Nothing in this
                                         run counts and the wiring is the bug


COST
-----
24 jobs of (slots, fade, delay). Five arms x three seeds x one rate = 15 cells a
job. g9-05 ran the same cell shape in about 7 minutes a job, 32 jobs, well inside
its 90-minute timeout. This is smaller.


THE ORACLE, SAID TWICE
-----------------------
The `oracle` arm reads `position_kinds()` and is a CEILING, not a result. Every
recovery figure is a fraction of an advantage no deployed system can have.
