ANSWER: **NO, ANYWHERE MEASURED.** Three learning rates, two widths and two key
        schemes. The store never earns a bit; addressed as a bigram it costs

MEASURED ON
------------
Tiny Shakespeare, WORD level via `corpus.words`, min_count 10, vocabulary 1733,
90,000 training words, held-out tail for test. `derived_keys`, readout bias ON,
cap 5.0, decay 1.0, one epoch, chunk 256, one seed. Bits per word, temperature
fitted on held-out TRAINING text and the rate chosen the same way.

`nostore` writes nothing at all, so every retrieval is zero, `Wo` receives a
delta proportional to zero and never moves, and the logits are the learned bias
alone.

WHAT THE THREE AXES EACH KILLED
--------------------------------
**The learning rate is not it.** Three rates spanning two orders of magnitude,
and the best any store-bearing arm manages is +0.002 -- noise.

**The width is not it.** Quadrupling the store from 128 to 512 moves the pair
arm by 0.001 bits. "The store is too small to hold anything useful" is refuted
as flatly as the rest: a store four times the size holds four times nothing.

**And the key scheme is not it, in the direction that matters most.** Single
keys make the store a BIGRAM in vector form -- note 033's ceiling, and here the
ceiling was the target, because a word bigram at 7.848 beats the bias-only model
by 1.34 bits. Addressed exactly the way a bigram is addressed, with a brake that
keeps it stable, **the store is 0.68 bits WORSE than not existing.**

So the problem is not that the store is addressed badly. It is that whatever the
store retrieves, the readout cannot turn into a better prediction than the prior
it already has -- and mixing it in costs accuracy.


THE RAIL IS WHY THIS IS READABLE
---------------------------------
`nostore` returns 9.213 / 9.187 / 9.221 at the three rates and is IDENTICAL to
three decimals across both widths and both key schemes -- spread 0.000. It has no
store, so nothing about the store should move it, and nothing does. Every
comparison above is against a baseline that is demonstrably measuring the bias
and only the bias.


WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY
-----------------------
**Not that the store is useless generally.** MQAR, the kinship line and the
relational tasks are all solved through it, and those are what it was built for.

**Not that character level is affected.** That is the next question rather than
an assumption -- see below.

**Not that word-level text is impossible for this architecture.** It says the
current store contributes nothing to it, which is a statement about the store's
contribution and not about the ceiling.


WHAT IT OPENS, AND IT IS THE SHARPEST QUESTION LEFT
-----------------------------------------------------
**Has the store ever contributed anything on TEXT at all?**

At character level the model reaches 5.17 bits against a 6.00 uniform, and that
looks like the store working. But every character-level run had `readout_bias`
OFF -- so the store's contribution there was never measured against a model that
could express a prior, and `Wo` can carry a prior by itself.

If a character-level `nostore` with the bias on lands near 5.17, then the store
has never contributed on text and the entire text line has been measuring a
learned prior. That is a bigger claim than anything here and it is one run away.
