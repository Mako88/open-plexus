The difficulty curve -- do the dials bite a model that can actually do the task?
================================================================================

Script: experiments/g1_04_dials.py
Status: RUN. Predictions 1, 2 and 3 held exactly. The task dials are FLAT --
        56 runs, every one at 1.000. A follow-up found what does bite.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
Our memory test has knobs: how many pairs to remember, and how long a stretch of
junk sits between seeing a pair and being asked about it. Turning them up is
supposed to make the test harder.

We have never checked that against something that can actually do the test. We
only ever checked it against things that fail regardless -- an untrained network,
and a one-line heuristic. Those get worse in the obvious way.

A capable model might not care at all. If it looks things up by content, it does
not matter whether there are four things to look through or sixteen, or whether
they are near or far. If that is what we find, then our knobs separate weak from
strong -- which is what they were for -- but they do NOT give us a way to make a
competent thing struggle. And we will need that shortly, because the whole point
of the next stage is finding where a restricted learner breaks.


QUESTION
--------
Note 001's property P3 asked for a CURVE, not a point: G0's output should be
performance as a function of difficulty, so an operating point can be chosen
where the gap is widest. That was measured for the floor (g0-01) and for a frozen
substrate (g0-02). It has never been measured for a trained model.

Sweep n_pairs and seq_len against the trained attention model. 8 seeds per cell,
reporting SOLVED / STUCK counts rather than means -- g1-03 established outcomes
are bimodal, so a mean describes a mixture of two populations and no actual run.

Run with value_offsets=(1,), the hint given. That is deliberate: g1-03 showed the
no-hint configurations add a search-failure mode of their own (8/20 stuck at one
setting), and mixing that in would make it impossible to tell task difficulty
from optimisation luck. With the hint the model solved 20/20, so any failure here
is attributable to the dial.


PREDICTIONS -- written before the run
--------------------------------------
1. n_pairs barely bites. The model does content-addressed lookup, which is
   insensitive to how many items there are to look through. Guess: 8/8 solved at
   every setting from 2 to 16.

   Note 006 called n_pairs "the load-bearing dial" and g0-01 confirmed it sets
   the trivial floor. Both remain true. But those are statements about what a
   WEAK strategy scores. This asks a different question and both can be right.

2. seq_len barely bites either. More junk between a pair and its query is more
   things to not attend to, which costs an attention model very little.
   Guess: 8/8 at 32, 64 and 128.

3. Therefore THE CURVE IS FLAT. This is the real prediction and it is
   uncomfortable: it would mean we do not currently possess a dial that makes
   this task harder for something competent.

4. If anything does bite, seq_len bites before n_pairs, because attention scores
   are spread over more positions as the sequence grows and the correct match
   must win against more competitors.

Most likely to be wrong: (3), in the direction of something biting at the largest
settings. n_pairs=16 with d_model=64 starts to press on the width available to
represent distinct keys, so a failure there would be about MODEL CAPACITY rather
than task difficulty -- and that distinction needs stating in the result rather
than being read as the dial working.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- a dial drops solved counts     -> we HAVE a working difficulty dial and note
                                    001's P3 curve exists; pick the operating
                                    point where it starts to bend
- everything stays 8/8           -> THE CURVE IS FLAT. The existing dials cannot
                                    make a competent model struggle, and G1 needs
                                    a different one before it can find where a
                                    local rule breaks
- failures only at n_pairs=16    -> likely capacity, not difficulty. Distinguish
                                    by re-running that cell at larger d_model:
                                    if it recovers, it was the model


RESULT
------
3000 steps, 8 seeds per cell, value_offsets=(1,).

--- n_pairs (seq_len fixed at 96) ---
setting           floor    solved     stuck   between   worst   best
---------------------------------------------------------------------
n_pairs=2         0.562       8/8       0/8       0/8   1.000  1.000
n_pairs=4         0.344       8/8       0/8       0/8   1.000  1.000
n_pairs=8         0.234       8/8       0/8       0/8   1.000  1.000
n_pairs=16        0.180       8/8       0/8       0/8   1.000  1.000

--- seq_len (n_pairs fixed at 4) ---
seq_len=32        0.344       8/8       0/8       0/8   1.000  1.000
seq_len=64        0.344       8/8       0/8       0/8   1.000  1.000
seq_len=128       0.344       8/8       0/8       0/8   1.000  1.000

56 runs. Every single one scored exactly 1.000.


THE CURVE IS FLAT
-----------------
Predictions 1, 2 and 3 all held, and 3 was the uncomfortable one.

**The task dials do not make this task harder for a model that can do it.** Not
slightly harder. Not harder at the extremes. Identical, to three decimals, across
an eightfold change in n_pairs and a fourfold change in seq_len.

This is not a contradiction of g0-01 or docs/notes/006, and the distinction is
worth keeping straight. Those established that n_pairs sets the TRIVIAL FLOOR --
from 0.562 at two pairs to 0.180 at sixteen, visible in the floor column above --
and that multi-query is what makes the task discriminating at all. Both remain
true. They are statements about what a WEAK strategy scores.

What is now measured is that the same dials do nothing to a STRONG one. The
dials separate weak from strong, which is what G0 needed them for. They do not
grade strong.

**The mechanism is not mysterious.** Content-addressed lookup is insensitive to
how many items there are to look through, and to how far away they are. That is
the whole point of retrieval as opposed to storage -- g0-03 saw the same thing
when a hand-written lookup stayed at 1.000 while n_pairs doubled.


WHAT DOES BITE: MODEL WIDTH
----------------------------
Follow-up at n_pairs=8, seq_len=96, 8 seeds, varying d_model:

d_model      solved   stuck  between   worst   best
----------------------------------------------------
4               0/8     8/8      0/8   0.011  0.028
8               0/8     8/8      0/8   0.043  0.143
16              8/8     0/8      0/8   0.969  1.000
32              8/8     0/8      0/8   1.000  1.000
64              8/8     0/8      0/8   1.000  1.000

**A sharp threshold between 8 and 16.** Below it nothing works; above it
everything does. No gradual degradation across the boundary -- one cell fails
completely and the next succeeds completely.

Worth one qualification to g1-03's strict bimodality claim: d_model=16 produced
a 0.969, the only intermediate value seen in 120 runs across both sweeps. Right
at the capacity threshold there is a narrow band of partial performance. Away
from it, the two-outcome pattern holds absolutely.


WHAT THIS MEANS FOR G1, AND IT IS A BETTER PLAN THAN THE ONE WE HAD
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Difficulty, for a competent model, does not live in the task parameters. It lives
in the model and in the learning problem. Two dials are now known to bite:

  - MODEL WIDTH, measured here: a sharp capacity threshold.
  - HOW MUCH THE ARCHITECTURE IS TOLD, measured in g1-03: giving or withholding
    the induction shape moved the failure rate from 0/20 to 8/20.

Neither is a property of the task. Both are properties of what the learner is
working with, which is exactly what G1 is about.

**So G1 gets a sharper design than "how close does a local rule get to 1.000".**

  Measure the WIDTH A LOCAL RULE NEEDS against the width backprop needs, on the
  same task, at the same success criterion.

Backprop crosses the threshold between 8 and 16. If a local rule needs 64, that
ratio IS the price of locality, in a unit that means something. If it never
crosses at any width, that is a different and much more serious result, and the
two are distinguishable -- which "it scored 0.4" would not be.

This also inherits g1-03's reporting rule: solved/stuck counts, not means.
