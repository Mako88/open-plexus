Does a gate work when the statistics stop being adversarial?
============================================================

Script: experiments/g8_02_zipf_gate.py

WHY THIS EXISTS
---------------
[Note 013](../../docs/notes/013-salience-and-the-missing-body.md) measured the
salience gate as **genuinely selective** -- query positions fire at 7.6x the
filler rate -- and losing anyway. The diagnosis was the base rate: filler is 92%
of an MQAR sequence, and because it is drawn UNIFORMLY it is also the most
surprising content in the sequence. A 7.6x enrichment does not survive a class
that is both the overwhelming majority and maximally unpredictable.

That diagnosis has never been tested. It is the difference between

  (a) surprise-driven storage does not work, and
  (b) surprise-driven storage does not work ON THIS BENCHMARK,

and every result in g8-01 rests on which of those is true.

**Uniform filler is the adversarial case for any surprise-driven mechanism, and
it was chosen for reasons that had nothing to do with surprise.** It was chosen
because structured filler starves a predictive objective (note 008 §4, measured
at 0.824 predictable versus 0.135 for task content). Nobody asked what it does to
a gate, because there was no gate yet.

Real language is Zipfian, and the two facts that matter are that the common word
is common everywhere and **the rare word is the informative one**. That is the
opposite of MQAR's arrangement, where the rare thing is noise by construction.


WHAT CHANGES, AND WHAT DELIBERATELY DOES NOT
----------------------------------------------
`MqarConfig` gains `filler="zipf"` and `zipf_s`. Filler tokens are drawn from a
power law by rank over the same alphabet they were always drawn from.

**Only the filler distribution changes.** Values stay uniform. This is the
central design decision and it is worth stating why: the trivial floor is
`1/n_pairs + (1 - 1/n_pairs)/n_values`, which assumes the answer alphabet is
uniform. Making VALUES Zipfian would break that -- guessing the commonest value
would beat the floor -- and every baseline, every answerability check and every
frozen-substrate control in this repository would need recomputing. Changing
filler alone attacks the diagnosis while leaving all of it valid.

`zipf_s = 0` is uniform, so **the mode contains its own control** and the sweep
does not need a separate arm to establish that the dial is doing something.


THE ARMS AND THE GRID
----------------------
The four arms of g8-01, unchanged: `none`, `oracle`, `on-use`, `salience`.
Crossed with the filler exponent.

  zipf_s      0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0      0.0 is the uniform control
  seq_len     768                          where g8-01's oracle gap is 0.293
  half-life   0.25                         mid-range from g7-04
  lr          0.02, 0.05, 0.1
  seeds       1, 2, 3

The reported quantity is **recovery**, `(arm - none) / (oracle - none)`, as in
g8-01, with the same refusal to divide when the oracle's advantage is not larger
than the spread across seeds.

Sequence length and half-life are PINNED rather than swept, and that is a real
narrowing: this asks whether the exponent moves recovery at one operating point,
not whether it moves it everywhere. Sweeping all four would be several hundred
jobs to answer a question that a single column answers if the effect exists at
all.


PREDICTIONS -- written before the script, before the controls, before any run
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. **Recovery for `salience` rises with `zipf_s`.** This is the whole hypothesis.
   Skewed filler is predictable filler, so filler stops clearing a surprise
   threshold, so the enrichment ratio improves. Guess: recovery above 0.2 at
   `zipf_s = 2.0` against approximately 0 at `zipf_s = 0`.

2. **Recovery for `on-use` does NOT rise with `zipf_s`.** Confirmation asks
   whether a retrieval was correct; it does not consult token frequency at all.
   **This asymmetry is what makes the sweep a test rather than a hope** -- if
   both arms rise together, something has changed the task's difficulty rather
   than the gate's selectivity, and the result means nothing about gating.

3. **The `none` arm improves with `zipf_s` too, and by a lot.** Predictable
   filler is easier to predict, and most positions are filler. **This is the
   trap in the whole design**: raw accuracy will rise for every arm, which is
   why the reported quantity is a ratio. If recovery is read off raw accuracy
   this sweep will produce a spurious success.

4. **The oracle is almost unmoved by `zipf_s`.** It never stores filler, so
   filler statistics should barely reach it. g8-01's control already shows the
   oracle at 0.998 across every half-life, unmoved by a dial that moves every
   other arm. If the oracle DOES move here, filler is reaching the memory by
   some path the gate was believed to close, and that is a finding about the
   implementation rather than about statistics.

Most likely wrong: (1). Everything measured so far says these gates do not work,
and one distributional change is a small lever against three independent
negative results.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- salience recovery rises, on-use flat  -> the base-rate diagnosis was RIGHT.
                                           MQAR was hiding a working mechanism,
                                           and the task, not the gate, was the
                                           problem
- both arms rise together               -> the task got easier; nothing has been
                                           learned about gating. Check arm 3
                                           before believing anything
- neither moves                         -> the base-rate diagnosis was WRONG.
                                           Surprise-driven storage fails for a
                                           reason not yet identified, and note
                                           013's explanation must be withdrawn
- the oracle moves                      -> filler is reaching the memory through
                                           a path the gate was believed to close


PRE-DISPATCH CONTROL -- the near end of the causal chain
--------------------------------------------------------
The hypothesis is a chain: skewed filler -> filler becomes predictable -> filler
stops clearing the surprise bar -> enrichment improves. The sweep measures the
far end and costs hours. This measures the near end and costs seconds, and if the
near end does not move the far end cannot.

**It measures the DATA, not a model.** A first draft replayed the model's inner
loop to recover a surprise trace, which is the reimplementation pattern that has
produced three vacuous tests here already -- and it was unnecessary, because the
claim is about the statistics of the sequences. Unigram self-information,
`-log p(token)` under the empirical distribution, is the honest floor on what any
model can find predictable.

Mean self-information by position kind, 40 sequences at seq_len 768:

    filler exponent      filler    query     pair   answer   answer-filler gap
    uniform               3.485    3.507    4.723    5.938               2.453
    zipf s=0.5            3.369    3.604    4.771    5.938               2.569
    zipf s=1.0            2.948    3.933    4.936    5.938               2.990
    zipf s=1.5            2.304    4.509    5.224    5.938               3.634
    zipf s=2.0            1.707    5.174    5.556    5.938               4.231

**The precondition holds.** Filler self-information falls monotonically and by
half, 3.485 to 1.707, while the answer alphabet is untouched at exactly 5.938 --
which is the design working as intended, since values were deliberately left
uniform to keep the trivial floor valid. The gap between task content and filler
widens by 72%.

An unplanned effect, in the same direction: query and pair self-information
RISE, 3.507 to 5.174 and 4.723 to 5.556. Keys become relatively rarer as filler
mass concentrates on a few tokens, so task content becomes more surprising as
well as filler becoming less. The separation improves from both ends at once,
which was not predicted.

> **The control also disqualifies one of its own columns.** It reported firing
> rates against a bar set from the overall mean and deviation, and those rates
> are NOT monotone -- no filler clears the bar at s=0.5 or s=1.0, then 2.1% does
> at s=2.0, and the answer rate wanders 1.00 / 0.71 / 0.19 / 0.85. The bar moves
> as the distribution moves, so the columns are not comparable across rows. The
> apparent "24x enrichment" at s=2.0 is that artefact and is not reported as a
> finding. Self-information is the measure that survives; the rates are not.

That the unigram measure understates how predictable task content is -- a query's
answer is predictable from its binding, not from its frequency -- biases AGAINST
the hypothesis, so the confirmation above is conservative.

WHAT THIS CANNOT SETTLE
------------------------
**Whether real language behaves like this.** A power law over a 28-token filler
alphabet shares one property with language and almost none of the others -- no
grammar, no long-range dependency, no compositional structure, and an alphabet
three orders of magnitude too small. A positive result here is a reason to build
the corpus benchmark, not a substitute for it.

**Whether the exponent is realistic.** Word frequencies sit near `s = 1`, but
that is measured over a vocabulary of tens of thousands. The same exponent over
28 tokens is a different distribution with the same name.

THE PREDICTIONS
----------------
1. **Recovery for `salience` rises with the exponent -- REFUTED.** It goes
   -0.05, -0.04, -0.02, 0.00, 0.00. The apparent upward drift is 0.05 across the
   whole grid against seed spreads of 0.160 to 0.321, so it is not a trend, it is
   noise. The guess was recovery above 0.2 at the top exponent. It is 0.00, and
   the 0.00 arrives only because everything below the oracle has collapsed.

2. **Recovery for `on-use` does not rise -- HELD, VACUOUSLY.** It does not rise.
   Neither does anything else. The prediction was designed as the control half of
   an asymmetry, and an asymmetry needs one side to move.

3. **The `none` arm improves with the exponent -- REFUTED, AND BACKWARDS.** It
   was predicted to rise "by a lot" because predictable filler is easier to
   predict and most positions are filler. It **falls**, 0.464 to 0.000.

   The pre-dispatch control is what made this look safe, and the control was
   measuring the right quantity and drawing the wrong conclusion from it. Filler
   self-information does fall, exactly as reported -- 3.485 to 1.707. What does
   not follow, and what was assumed, is that a more predictable stream is an
   easier task. See below.

4. **The oracle is almost unmoved -- REFUTED.** 0.999, 0.999, 0.980, 0.903,
   0.803. It falls by 0.196. The reasoning was that a gate which never stores
   filler cannot be reached by filler statistics. It is reached anyway, because
   filler still passes through the READOUT even when it is never written to the
   memory -- the delta rule learns from every position, gated or not. The gate
   controls storage, not learning, and that distinction had not been noticed.


WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED -- the model collapses onto one token
--------------------------------------------------------------
Accuracy of exactly 0.000, on every seed, is not a hard task. Chance alone lands
near the trivial floor. A follow-up probe at the same settings, counting what the
trained model emits over 30 test sequences:

    zipf_s   accuracy   distinct predictions   share of the commonest
       0.0      0.700                     40                   12.8%
       1.0      0.581                     40                   13.6%
       1.5      0.000                      1                  100.0%
       2.0      0.000                      1                  100.0%

**At exponent 1.5 and above the model emits token 0 at all 23,040 positions.**
Not a degraded answer: one answer. Accuracy is zero by construction, because the
answer alphabet is disjoint from the filler alphabet, so a model that has learned
to say "the commonest thing" can never be right at a query.

So the bottom two rows of the table measure degeneracy, not gating, and the
honest usable range is `zipf_s` 0.0 to 1.0. Across that range recovery is flat at
approximately zero, with every difference inside the seed spread.


WHAT THIS SETTLES
------------------
**The base-rate diagnosis is not supported.** Note 013 explained the salience
gate's failure by the base rate: filler is 92% of the sequence and, being
uniform, is also the most surprising content. Making filler predictable was
supposed to lift the gate. It does not, anywhere the measurement is meaningful.

That does not make note 013's *measurement* wrong -- queries do fire at 7.6x the
filler rate -- but its **explanation of why that is not enough** no longer has
support, and should be read as an unconfirmed hypothesis rather than as the
reason.

It also sharpens what g8-01 settled. The failure of both gates is now measured
under two very different filler distributions, which makes "MQAR is the problem"
a considerably weaker escape route than it was this morning.


THE UNPLANNED FINDING, WHICH MATTERS MORE THAN THE PLANNED ONE
----------------------------------------------------------------
**Our learning rule collapses onto the modal token under skewed input, and real
language is skewed.**

The plan of record was: Zipfian synthetic first, then a small real corpus at
character level, measured against a bigram baseline. That plan just acquired a
blocker, and acquired it cheaply -- in a 15-job sweep rather than after building a
corpus pipeline.

Zipf's law for words sits near exponent 1, which is the last column where
anything works here, and character-level English is more skewed still. So the
corpus benchmark cannot simply be attempted; the collapse has to be understood
and handled first. Frequency-normalised learning, a loss that does not reward the
majority class, and per-token learning-rate scaling are all candidates and none
is chosen.

**This is the most useful thing the sweep produced and it is not what the sweep
was for.**


CORRECTION -- THE USABLE RANGE IS NARROWER THAN CLAIMED, AND THE CONCLUSION
WEAKENS WITH IT
============================================================================
Found by tools/audit_floor.py while auditing g8-01 for the same fault.

**Seven of fifteen cells have the `none` arm at or below the trivial floor of
0.34375**, so their recovery ratios divide by the gap between a working ceiling
and a broken floor. Per seed, at the best learning rate for each exponent:

    zipf_s 0.0    0.560/0.401/0.430    mean 0.464    usable
    zipf_s 0.5    0.553/0.362/0.370    mean 0.428    usable
    zipf_s 1.0    0.413/0.277/0.196    mean 0.295    NO
    zipf_s 1.5    0.000/0.000/0.000    mean 0.000    NO
    zipf_s 2.0    0.000/0.000/0.000    mean 0.000    NO

The write-up above states that "the honest usable range is `zipf_s` 0.0 to 1.0".
**That was wrong.** The usable range is 0.0 and 0.5 — two points.

WHAT THIS DOES TO THE CONCLUSION. The sweep concluded that the base-rate
diagnosis is not supported: making filler predictable did not lift the gate. That
conclusion now rests on **two exponents, 0.0 and 0.5**, over which salience
recovery moves from -0.05 to -0.04.

That is not evidence that skew fails to help. It is evidence that a *small amount
of skew* fails to help, measured over a range that does not reach the interesting
part. **Word frequencies sit near exponent 1.0** — the first cell this sweep
cannot use.

So the honest status of the base-rate hypothesis is **largely untested**, not
refuted. The claim in the RESULT section that it is "NOT supported" is withdrawn
and replaced by: *no effect was detectable across the narrow band of skew where
the floor arm still works, and the band stops short of realistic skew.*

WHAT SURVIVES UNCHANGED.

- **The collapse finding.** At exponent 1.5 and above the model emits one token
  at every position. That is a property of the model under skewed input and does
  not depend on any recovery ratio.
- **The divergence.** Note 018 traces it to the fast store's geometric growth,
  measured without training.
- **Prediction 3 refuted backwards.** `none` falls rather than rises with skew.
  The floor arm's collapse IS that finding, not a confound of it.
- **Prediction 4 refuted.** The oracle moves because the delta rule learns from
  every position whether or not it was stored.

WHY THE BAND IS SO NARROW, WHICH IS THE USEFUL PART. The floor arm fails at
exponent 1.0 for the reason note 018 identifies: repetition drives the fast store
up, and the readout update is quadratic in it. **So this sweep could not test
realistic skew because the model cannot survive realistic skew** — which is a
better statement of the blocker than "the corpus benchmark needs care", and it
puts the fast-store cap ahead of any re-run.

Re-running this grid before that cap exists would produce the same broken cells.
