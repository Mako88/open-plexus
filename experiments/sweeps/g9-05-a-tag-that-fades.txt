=====================================================

Script: experiments/g9_05_the_tag.py
Status: RUN. 32 of 32 cells, no refusals.
Run:    https://github.com/Mako88/open-plexus/actions/runs/30239580807

ANSWER: the flat rows are flat at ZERO and the positive rows have the window's
        cliff. No cell is both -- and being both was the entire claim. Held back
        from being final by a pinned capacity axis; see THE GRID DID NOT CONTAIN

Every cell is healthy: floor arm 0.238-0.249 against a trivial floor of 0.125,
oracle gap 0.750-0.761, seed spread 0.062-0.113. Nothing was refused, so nothing
here is a ratio taken on noise.

**Read the spread column.** A tag that does not have to be told the delay is the
whole proposition, and it delivers that exactly where it has nothing to deliver:
fade 0.99 spans 0.03 and sits at -0.02. Where it is worth having -- fade 0.95 and
0.9 -- it spans 0.41 and 0.46, which is the window's 0.47 in a different costume.

**The tie is the interesting part.** At delays 1 and 4 the tag matches the window
(0.24/0.23 against 0.23/0.23) while keeping roughly a quarter as much. The
pre-dispatch control could not settle whether a gate capturing a third as often
while storing a quarter as much comes out ahead; it comes out level. So the two
mechanisms reach the same recovery from different signals, which is the case for
combining them rather than choosing between them.

IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
The model has to decide what to remember. It cannot know at the time, because
the signal saying "that mattered" arrives later. So it keeps everything briefly
and, when the signal arrives, throws away all but a few recent things.

"A few recent things" is the problem. Recent is measured in steps, and the thing
worth keeping might be four steps back or forty, and nothing tells it which. Look
too far back and it keeps forty pieces of junk with the one good item; look too
close and it misses the item entirely.

This tries measuring the keep-set in **items** instead of in **time**: mark a
small number of writes that look like real information, let the marks age, and
when the signal arrives keep whatever is still marked. If that works, the model
stops needing to be told how far back to look. If it does not, the whole idea
that a mark can substitute for a well-chosen window is wrong, and that is worth
knowing before more is built on it.


WHY THIS SHAPE
---------------
[g9-03](g9-03-is-the-cliff-reach-or-cost.txt) settled that the window's reach has
to be MATCHED: about 0.2 recovered on and above the diagonal, about -0.22 below
it, and every doubling past the smallest covering window costs about a fifth of
what is left. A window of 64 recovers 0.09 at every delay.

[g9-04](g9-04-is-there-a-local-signal.txt) found what a mark could hang on:
retrieval strength separates a binding-write from a filler-write at AUC 0.293 and
0.215 -- below 0.5, so **admit the weak retrievals**. Recency carries nothing
(0.479), so a window's only virtue was ever REACHING the binding.

The tag is those two together. Capacity over writes, admission on weak retrieval,
and the marks **fade** -- which note 010 took from Lehr et al. and which the first
build of this left out.


WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN, BEFORE ANY SWEEP
----------------------------------------
The claim a gate makes is about which writes survive, so the pre-dispatch control
counts them directly against `position_kinds()` rather than reading the score.
Eight sequences, 32 captures, and exactly one rewarded binding is available per
capture. `kept` is total writes surviving, which is the interference cost.

              arm       delay 1        delay 8       delay 20      kept
    -------------------------------------------------------------------
      window 8            100%           100%             0%      ~224
      tag 8 fade 1.0         9%             9%            16%      ~250
      tag 8 fade 0.99       44%            44%            34%      ~250
      tag 8 fade 0.95      100%            84%            25%      ~230
      tag 8 fade 0.9       100%            84%             6%      ~220
      tag 4 fade 0.99       25%            19%            12%      ~125
      tag 8 STRONGEST        9%             6%             0%      ~245

Four things are already visible and they shape every prediction below.

**The fade is the mechanism, not a refinement.** An un-faded tag captures the
rewarded binding in 2 to 5 of 32 captures at every capacity and delay measured,
and in exactly 2 of 32 at slots 1, 2 and 4 -- unmoved by either dial. Without
ageing it ranks the whole interval at once, and the weakest retrievals it will
ever see are the writes made just after a capture, when the store is nearly
empty and everything retrieves weakly. It fills with those.

**The fade is also a reach dial.** Read the rows: 0.95 and 0.9 are best at short
delays and collapse at 20; 0.99 is the only setting that holds anything at 20.
That is the same matching problem as the window, in a softer form, and it is the
strongest reason to expect this mechanism to fall short of its billing.

**Direction is doing real work.** `tag-strongest` captures the rewarded binding
essentially never. Whatever else is true, g9-04's inversion is not decoration.

**Where a matched window reaches, it wins.** 100% against 44% at delays 1 and 8
for the same storage. Recency carries no information about being a BINDING, which
is what g9-04 measured -- but it carries a great deal about being the REWARDED
binding, because the reward token is placed a fixed distance after it. The window
and the tag are not better and worse selectors of one thing. They select
different things, and only one of them is the thing the task asks about.


THE GRID
---------
  slots     4, 8                 how many writes survive a capture
  fade      1.0, 0.99, 0.95, 0.9 how fast a mark ages out; 1.0 is the control
  delay     1, 4, 8, 20          how far the binding is from its reward
  arms      none, oracle, reward, tag, tag-strongest
  seeds     1, 2, 3
  lr        0.05, FIXED on every arm

`reward` runs at the fixed reach of 8 that g9-02 used, so these cells are
comparable with that sweep. The full window x delay table is g9-03's and is not
re-run here.

`tag-strongest` runs at the same slots and fade as `tag` in the same cell. It is
the only arm that can separate "the signal works" from "any capacity works".


PREDICTIONS
------------
1. **The tag loses to the window at delays 1, 4 and 8, and beats it at 20.**
   Guess: tag 0.05-0.20 at every delay; reward about 0.20-0.25 at delays 1-8 and
   about -0.2 at delay 20, reproducing g9-02 and g9-03. The reason is in the
   control: where the window reaches, it captures the rewarded binding every
   time and the tag captures it in under half of captures.

2. **The tag's row across delay is flatter than the window's.** The window spans
   about 0.45 from best to worst; predict the tag at fade 0.99 spans under 0.15.
   This is the mechanism's actual claim -- not that it wins, but that it does not
   have to be told the delay.

3. **The flatness is a property of the LONG fade only.** At 0.95 and 0.9 predict
   a cliff of the window's own shape, positive at delays 1-8 and at or below zero
   at 20. If the fade behaves like a span at every setting, the tag is a soft
   window and should be named one in the write-up rather than defended.

4. **The un-faded tag (fade 1.0) sits at or below `none` at every delay.** It is
   the internal control that the fade is load-bearing. If it scores as well as
   the faded arms, the capture-rate measurement above is not reaching the score
   and something between them is wrong.

5. **`tag` beats `tag-strongest` at every unrefused cell**, and by more at delays
   8 and 20 than at delay 1. If the gap is within noise, g9-04's inversion does
   not survive contact with a mechanism and prediction 1's reasoning is void.

6. **No tag cell beats g9-03's best window cell of 0.25.** This is the one I
   would most like to be wrong about, and it is the honest reading of the
   control.

Most likely wrong: **prediction 2.** The fade is a time constant, and a time
constant is a span with a soft edge. The claim that its reach is set by the
density of bindings rather than by the clock has not been measured, only argued.


PREDICTIONS, SCORED
--------------------
1. **PARTLY REFUTED.** Predicted the tag loses at delays 1, 4 and 8 and beats the
   window at 20, within 0.05-0.20. It TIES at 1 and 4, loses at 8, and at 20 is
   -0.03 against the window's -0.24 -- much less harmful, still harmful. The band
   was wrong in both directions: the actual range is -0.22 to +0.24.

2. **CONFIRMED, and it was named most likely wrong.** fade 0.99 spans 0.03 at
   slots 8 and 0.05 at slots 4, against the window's 0.47. The tag genuinely does
   not need to be told the delay. It has nothing to offer once it does not.

3. **CONFIRMED exactly.** fade 0.95 and 0.9 cliff in the window's own shape:
   positive at delays 1-8, negative at 20. The fade is a reach dial with a soft
   edge, which is what makes prediction 2 hollow rather than a vindication.

4. **CONFIRMED strongly.** The un-faded tag is -0.18 to -0.22 everywhere, at both
   capacities -- worse than not gating, at every delay. The fade is not a
   refinement; without it the mechanism is harmful.

5. **MOSTLY CONFIRMED.** tag beats tag-strongest in 28 of 32 cells, mean +0.10.
   All four exceptions are at fade 1.0, where prediction 4 says there is no
   working mechanism -- so the direction holds wherever there is one. The second
   half is half right: the mean gap by delay is +0.10, +0.12, +0.14, +0.06, so
   bigger at 8 than at 1 but smaller at 20.

6. **CONFIRMED at 0.24 against 0.25 -- and it is a bound, not a value.** See the
   pinned capacity axis above. This is the prediction most likely to be overturned
   by g9-06 and the one it would be best to be wrong about.

Four of six held, one partly, one is provisional on a grid that pinned. The
prediction named most likely wrong is the one that held, and it held in the way
that made it worthless: flat, at zero.

DISCLOSURE
-----------
The control table above was run locally, on 8 sequences at one seed, BEFORE these
predictions were written. It is reported here rather than described afterwards as
a hunch, because a prediction written after a control has run is a summary rather
than a commitment (CLAUDE.md, "a prediction written after the controls have run").

Specifically: predictions 1, 3, 4 and 5 are readings of that table and should be
counted as weak evidence of foresight at best. **Predictions 2 and 6 are not in
it** -- the control measures what is captured, and neither flatness of RECOVERY
nor the comparison against g9-03's ceiling can be read off a capture count.

The control also cost the mechanism a defect. `tag_decay` was first implemented
as a plain multiplication on every rank, which fades one end of the ranking and
entrenches the other, and it produced numbers identical to no fade at all at
every setting from 0.99 to 0.7. It was found by looking at a dial that changed
nothing, which is the failure mode CLAUDE.md is written against, and it is now
pinned by `the-fade-entrenches-instead-of-releasing` in tools/mutate.py.


THE ORACLE, SAID TWICE
-----------------------
The `oracle` arm reads `position_kinds()` and is a CEILING, not a result. The
control's labels come from the same place and score writes the model chose
without them. Nothing measured is fed back into anything.
