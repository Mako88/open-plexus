Can grouping answer a question about something never stated?
============================================================

Script: experiments/g19_01_can_grouping_answer_what_was_never_stated.py
Task:   openplexus/tasks/families.py, calibrated by g19-00
Status: THREE SEEDS, all three predictions confirmed. Decision 143.

ANSWER: **Yes, and by 0.91 accuracy.** This is the first measurement in which
        `concepts.py`'s surface-to-concept indirection pays for itself.

        **AND THE RESULT IS NARROWER THAN THE NUMBER LOOKS. Read the tautology
        section before quoting it.**


MEASURED ON
------------
`families.py`: 8 families of 4 entities, 3 attributes each, 8 values, 2 facts
stated per family, vocabulary 66. Background streams fit `ContentIndex`
(window 1, no weighting, 200 streams); task sequences carry facts and questions
only and run ~60 tokens. 400 training sequences, 6 epochs, 200 held out, width
64, single keys, lr 0.05, decay 0.99, three seeds.

`k = 16` from g19-00, which must cover the token CLASSES rather than just the
families.


PREDICTIONS (registered in note 048, before the task was built)
----------------------------------------------------------------
  P1  THE GATE. `concept` beats `ungrouped` on TRANSFER by more than 0.20.
  P2  THE CONTROL. `permuted` does not beat `ungrouped` on TRANSFER by 0.05.
  P3  THE RAIL. `nostore` is at chance on both kinds.

SCORED: **P1 CONFIRMED** by +0.9116, against a gate of 0.20.
        **P2 CONFIRMED** -- permuted is 0.0209 BELOW ungrouped.
        **P3 CONFIRMED**, and at zero rather than at chance, for the reason
        decision 142 found on MQAR: a model with nothing to retrieve does not
        guess, it emits a constant.


THE TABLE
----------
Mean over three seeds. Chance is 0.125.

    arm           direct  transfer   family recovery
    ungrouped     0.6583    0.0867         --
    concept       0.9967    0.9983      1.000
    permuted      0.2725    0.0658      1.000
    nostore       0.0000    0.0000         --

**`ungrouped` at 0.087 on TRANSFER is the point.** Nothing was ever said about
that entity, so today's model can only guess, and it does — structurally, not
for want of tuning.

**`concept` at 0.998 answers almost every one.** The family shares the store's
address, so a sibling's write is what a read at this entity returns.

**`permuted` fails, and that is the control that matters.** It has the same
number of groups and the same group sizes; only the membership is wrong. On text
(decision 141) the entire measured gain came from having fewer addresses however
chosen, and only a size-matched control could tell that apart. Here it cannot
carry the result: **the similarity has to be the real one.**

**And wrong grouping is worse than none** -- permuted DIRECT 0.273 against
ungrouped 0.658. Accidental siblings share an address and overwrite each other,
so a bad concept map actively corrupts recall that plain addressing gets right.


THE TAUTOLOGY, STATED BEFORE THE RESULT IS QUOTED ANYWHERE
------------------------------------------------------------
**The task is built so that every member of a family shares one value.** So
"group by family" and "know the answer is shared" are close to the same
statement, and a sceptic is right to say the experiment is partly circular.

What is NOT circular, and is what the 0.91 actually demonstrates:

  - the families are **never labelled**. They are discovered from co-occurrence
    in a separate stream, by a mechanism that sees nothing else
  - the value is **redrawn every sequence**, so nothing is memorised across them
    and no prior can help -- note 047's condition
  - the model must **store a sibling's fact and retrieve it at the query**, which
    is the store doing its job through an indirection it was never tested with
  - `permuted` **fails at matched address count**, so the gain is not the
    address collapse that explained everything on text

So the honest claim is about the **composition**: discover a grouping, address a
store by it, and recall through it. All three parts work together, measured, for
the first time.

**What it does NOT show** is that concept representation solves anything harder
than *same kind, same answer*. The relation here is identity of value within a
family, which is the simplest generalisation there is. A task where the answer
depends on the family AND on something entity-specific would be the next real
test, and this result says nothing about it.
