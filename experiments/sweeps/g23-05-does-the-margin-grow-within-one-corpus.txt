Does the margin grow with size WITHIN one corpus?
==================================================

Script: tools/relation_contrastive.py --graph <subsample of FB15k-237>
Status: NOT RUN. Predictions registered and committed before the run.

ANSWER: pending.


IN PLAIN TERMS
---------------
Across eighteen graphs, the learned representation beat plain counting by more on
the big ones than the small ones. That is encouraging -- it says the result is not
an artefact of small test sets -- but it was noticed AFTER the fact, and the big
graphs also came from different sources than the small ones.

So this holds the source fixed. Take one graph, throw away most of it, and see
whether the advantage shrinks.

If it does, the size story survives. If the advantage is flat, the earlier pattern
was about which corpus a graph came from rather than how big it was.


WHY THIS EXISTS
----------------
`docs/options/structured-relations.md` carries a POST-HOC observation: Pearson
`r = +0.507` between margin and `log10(rules)` over 17 graphs, mean margin
`+0.0451` below 300 rules against `+0.1167` above, both losses at the small end.

**It is labelled post-hoc there and it settles nothing.** Two confounds were named
with it: size is entangled with SOURCE -- the largest graph is the only Freebase
one -- and `r = +0.507` over 17 points is weak.

This removes the first confound by construction: every point comes from
FB15k-237.

**It is still not `GOALS.md`'s G5.** G5 asks whether the margin survives as the
NETWORK grows -- more machines, smaller slices. This is corpus size at one
machine. A result here says nothing about that axis and must not be quoted at it.


THE MEASUREMENT
---------------
Subsample FB15k-237's 272,115 edges at several fractions, then run the same two
arms on each. Everything else held: width 32, epochs 8, lr 0.05, temperature 0.1,
5 seeds, 75/25 rule split.

**Edges are sampled, not rules**, because a corpus arrives as edges and the rule
set is what falls out. Rule count is reported per row rather than controlled, and
P4 is about the relationship between the two.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
  P1  THE TEST. The margin grows with subsample size, and the largest subsample
      beats the smallest by more than 0.05.

      Refuted by a flat margin, which would mean the post-hoc pattern was about
      corpus IDENTITY and not size -- and would take the small-graph losses back
      to being unexplained.

  P2  At the smallest subsample the margin is at or below +0.03, approaching the
      losses seen at 48 and 594 rules.

      This is P1's other end. If small subsamples of a big corpus keep a healthy
      margin while genuinely small corpora do not, size is not the variable and
      something about those corpora is.

  P3  `counted` is FLAT across sizes -- its best and worst differ by less than
      0.05.

      `g23-04` already refuted the prediction that counting improves with data:
      four times the rules bought it nothing. If it is flat here too, the margin's
      shape is entirely the learned arm's.

  P4  THE ONE I EXPECT TO BE WRONG. Rule count falls roughly LINEARLY with edge
      count.

      I expect it does not. A rule needs a closing triangle -- three specific
      edges all present -- so removing half the edges should remove far more than
      half the triangles. If rules fall super-linearly, then "corpus size" in
      EDGES and in RULES are different axes, the post-hoc correlation was computed
      on rules, and this sweep's x-axis has to be stated carefully wherever it is
      quoted.


WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS NOT COUNT
--------------------------------
**Subsampling that is not uniform.** Taking the first N lines would follow
whatever order the file is in, which for a knowledge graph is not random. Edges
are sampled with a seeded shuffle and the seed is part of the condition.

**Too few rules to split.** Below a few dozen rules a 75/25 split leaves single
digits held out and the score is noise. Rule count is reported per row and any row
under 40 held-out rules is called out rather than averaged in.

**Knobs carried, not tuned per size.** Tuning the learned arm at each size while
counting has no knobs is the one-sided sweep the price-of-locality calibration is
about.


COST
----
Five subsample sizes, two arms, 5 seeds, on a corpus that takes about four minutes
at full size. Under half an hour local, no dispatch.

-----------
`data/fb15k237/train.txt`, sha256 61099230e4439f90..., subsampled by edge count.
