Is the crossing a capacity limit or just a convergence limit?
=============================================================

Script: experiments/g1_07_convergence.py
Status: RUN on Actions, 18/18 cells. PREDICTION 1 REFUTED. It is a CAPACITY
        limit -- the 4-6x price stands -- and that leaves g3-01's ablated 0.924
        at width 32 UNEXPLAINED, which is the outcome flagged in advance as the
        more interesting one.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
This decides whether the headline number of this whole project is right.

We reported that a locality-respecting rule needs four to six times more room
than the unrestricted version. That was the price of running on ordinary
machines, and it is the number everything else has been argued against.

Then the churn experiment produced something that does not fit. A model shrunk
DOWN to a small size scored 0.92. A model of that same small size, trained from
scratch, scored 0.22. Same design, same amount of training, same data. The only
difference is where it started.

Which raises an uncomfortable possibility. Maybe the small model is not too small
at all. Maybe it is just SLOW -- and we stopped training before it got there.

If that is what is going on, then "needs four to six times more room" is not
about room. It is about patience, and the number is measuring the wrong thing.

This is the cheapest possible way to find out: take the small models and train
them for much longer.


QUESTION
--------
g1-05 and g1-06 both trained for 8 epochs and located a crossing between widths
48 and 64. g3-01 then found ablated models reaching 0.924 at surviving width 32
where from-scratch reaches 0.225.

Only the readout learns; wk and wv are frozen random. A random 32 of 64 random
columns is statistically just 32 random columns, so both models face the same
problem with the same tools. The difference must be the readout's starting state.

So: train from scratch at widths 24, 32 and 48 for 8, 16, 32 and 64 epochs.
6 seeds. Does accuracy climb with budget, or plateau?


PREDICTIONS -- written before the run
--------------------------------------
1. **d=32 climbs substantially with more epochs.** This is the central
   prediction and I expect it to hold, because the ablated model demonstrates
   that 0.92 is reachable at that width -- it is an existence proof that the
   capacity is there. What has not been shown is that a cold start can find it.

2. It gets most of the way there, but perhaps not all. Guess: d=32 reaches
   0.75-0.92 by 64 epochs, against 0.225 at 8. If it lands short of 0.92 the
   honest reading is that BOTH effects are real -- some capacity limit and some
   convergence limit -- and the price of locality is smaller than 4-6x but not
   zero.

3. d=48 also climbs, from 0.910 toward 0.99. Less dramatically, since it starts
   much closer.

4. d=24 climbs least. At some width there genuinely is not enough room, and 24
   is the best candidate for it among those tested -- g1-05 put it at 0.027 and
   g1-06 at 0.027.

5. The gains are still arriving at 64 epochs rather than flat, at least for
   d=32. If accuracy is still rising when the budget runs out, no plateau has
   been demonstrated and the honest answer is "we do not know where it stops".

Most likely to be wrong: (2)'s magnitude, in either direction. And there is a
real chance of (1) failing outright, which would be the more interesting result:
if d=32 from scratch plateaus at 0.3 no matter the budget, then the difference
with the ablated model is NOT about convergence, and something else is being
carried across the ablation that this project does not currently understand. That
would need chasing, because "only the readout persists" is a claim the design
rests on.


WHAT WOULD REFUTE WHAT
-----------------------
- d=32 reaches ~0.92        -> CONVERGENCE limit. The 4-6x price is about
                               training budget, not room, and g1-05's headline
                               must be restated
- d=32 plateaus well below  -> CAPACITY limit stands, AND the ablated model's
                               0.924 becomes unexplained -- something survives
                               ablation that we have not accounted for
- d=32 lands in between     -> both effects real; price smaller than 4-6x and
                               larger than zero, and the honest number is
                               whatever the plateau says
- still climbing at 64      -> no plateau demonstrated; the answer is "unknown,
                               and here is the lower bound"


WHY THIS MATTERS BEYOND THE NUMBER
------------------------------------
If the crossing is about convergence, then the comparison in g1-05 was unfair in
a specific and fixable way: attention and the local rule were given the same
number of epochs, but they are not the same kind of learner. Attention updates
every parameter through a global gradient; the local rule updates a readout by a
delta rule over frozen features. There is no reason those should converge at the
same rate, and matching epochs rather than matching convergence would compare
two things at different points on their own curves.

That would be a methodological error rather than an arithmetic one, and worth
recording as such -- the sort of thing that produces a confidently wrong number
that no downstream experiment ever contradicts.

PREDICTION 1 REFUTED -- IT IS CAPACITY, NOT CONVERGENCE
--------------------------------------------------------
d=32 does not climb. It is FLAT: 0.185 at one epoch, 0.180 at sixty-four. Eight
times the training budget buys nothing whatever, and the same is true at 24 and
48. Every width converges within about one epoch and then sits there.

So:

  **THE 4-6x PRICE OF LOCALITY STANDS.** The crossing measured in g1-05 and
  g1-06 is a real capacity threshold, and the doubt g3-01 raised against it is
  resolved in the number's favour. g1-05's IN DOUBT annotation can be lifted.

  *** THIS PARAGRAPH IS WRONG. *** g3-02 ran the deciding experiment set out
  below and the answer was (a): initialisation scale. A native width-32 model
  scores 0.263; the same model with its projections scaled by 0.71 scores
  0.960. It is not capacity. The 4-6x figure is back in doubt and must not be
  quoted. This paragraph is left standing rather than deleted, because it was
  written from the evidence available at the time, and the sequence of being
  right, wrong and wrong again about the same number is the useful record.

Prediction 5 -- that gains would still be arriving at 64 epochs -- is refuted in
the strongest possible way. There is no trajectory here at all.

That also disposes of the methodological worry this note raised: if every width
converges in one epoch, then matching epochs between attention and the local rule
was not comparing two learners at different points on their own curves. Both were
converged. The comparison was fair.


AND NOW THE PUZZLE
------------------
Which leaves the thing this run was supposed to explain, unexplained:

  from scratch at width 32, any budget    0.18
  ablated DOWN to width 32 (g3-01)        0.924

Same architecture. Same number of live dimensions. Same data. Only the readout
learns, and the projections are frozen random. **A random 32 of 64 random columns
is statistically just 32 random columns**, so the two should be solving the same
problem with the same tools -- and one of them scores five times the other.

Convergence is now ruled out as the explanation, because there is nothing to
converge to: the from-scratch model reaches its ceiling in one epoch and stays.

This note said in advance what such a result would mean, and it is worth quoting
because it is uncomfortable: "something survives ablation that this project does
not understand, and 'only the readout persists' is a claim the whole design rests
on."

Two candidates, and they are cleanly separable by one experiment:

  (a) INITIALISATION SCALE. The projections are drawn with standard deviation
      1/sqrt(d_model). A d=64 model ablated to 32 live dimensions therefore has
      keys of norm ~0.71, where a native d=32 model has norm ~1.0. Signal-to-noise
      arithmetic says a uniform rescaling should cancel out of a *learned*
      readout, but arithmetic said a lot of things in g1-06 that measurement
      disagreed with.

  (b) THE TRAINED READOUT. The ablated model's readout spent four epochs learning
      at width 64 before losing half its input. If what it retained on the
      surviving columns is better than anything a cold start finds, then a wider
      model is distilling something into its own subspace -- and "only the readout
      persists" would be *true* while badly understating what persisting costs.

THE DECIDING EXPERIMENT, which is cheap: build a d=64 model, ablate 32 dimensions
**at initialisation, before any training**, and train it. It then has the ablated
model's initialisation scale and the from-scratch model's cold readout.

  reaches ~0.92  -> (a). It is the initialisation, and the width curve is
                    partly an artifact of how the projections are scaled --
                    which would put the 4-6x number back in doubt from a
                    completely different direction
  reaches ~0.18  -> (b). The trained readout carries real information into a
                    smaller space, which is a genuine and unplanned finding
                    about the design

Recorded rather than guessed. The last two times this project explained a result
before testing the explanation, the explanation was wrong.
