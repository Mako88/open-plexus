Trials this project did not design
==================================

Script: experiments/g34_01_external_word_learning_trials.py
Status: RUN, local, 2026-07-31, 0.1s. Predictions committed at `bfad487`
        before the script was executed.

ANSWER: **26 of 29 conditions recovered exactly, and the three failures are
        conditions where the answer is provably not in the data.** The ranking is
        bimodal on stimuli this project did not design — mean largest gap about
        0.5 against note 058's 0.059 for real language — so the derived bound is
        validated outside a world we built.


IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
Every test of the grounding mechanism so far used a stream this project wrote,
with knobs this project chose. That is a real weakness and the project already
names it: **we have been grading our own homework.**

These trials come from psychology experiments published between 2008 and 2013.
Each trial shows a participant several words and several objects at once and does
not say which goes with which. Nobody can tell from one trial. Across many
trials, the word that keeps appearing whenever a particular object does is
presumably its name — which is the same reasoning the mechanism does, posed by
someone else, more than a decade earlier.

The correct answer is in the file, so no human data is needed to score it. That
matters, because the human scores turn out not to be reachable without decoding
an R binary format. **So this is external STIMULI, not an external BENCHMARK**, and
that difference is stated wherever a number from it is quoted.

The second question is the one that is really outstanding. The mechanism's newest
piece — deciding for itself how many partners to keep — works by looking for a
**cliff** in the ranking. This project has only ever seen a cliff in worlds it
built. When it looked at real language it found a gentle slope instead, where the
rule is not just weaker but decided by rounding error. So: do real experimental
trials have a cliff or a slope? These files can answer that, and nothing else
here can.


WHAT IS BEING MEASURED
----------------------
    f1        mean per-surface F1 against the true word-object mapping.
              **The floor here is 0.6667, NOT 0.5** — a concept has two surfaces,
              not three, so one recovered alone scores precision 1.0 and recall
              0.5. Every previous grounding sweep had a 0.5 floor and quoting
              this table against that one would overstate every row
    gap       mean size of the largest drop in a surface's ranking — the
              quantity note 058 reported as 0.059 for real language against
              0.424 for the designed families task
    cut       mean number of partners the derived bound keeps. The true answer
              is 1, because a word has exactly one object

Arms: `count` and `conditional` at a fixed bound of 1, and `conditional` with the
derived bound. `local` and `ppmi` are omitted: `g33-01` measured the first
failing everywhere and the second undeployable under C1.


MEASURED ON
-----------
Local, single process, Python 3.14, no numpy.

29 conditions from `kachergis/XSLmodels`, fetched by `tools/fetch_kachergis.py`
and pinned by sha256. Between 12 and 18 word-object pairs each, 2 to 4 pairs per
trial, 18 to 36 trials. Nothing is swept: **the trials are fixed, which is the
entire point** — a knob is how a designed instrument gets made solvable.

The join is not in the loop and neither is the federation. This asks whether the
STATISTIC and the BOUND survive contact with stimuli this project did not write.


PREDICTIONS
-----------
P1  THE MECHANISM BEATS THE PEOPLE, and by a lot. Where the dataset table records
    a human score at all it is 0.18 to 0.56, and those conditions are considered
    hard. An exact counter with perfect memory should recover most conditions at
    f1 above 0.90.
    REFUTED IF it does not. **That refutation would be the interesting one**: it
    would mean these trials contain a difficulty the mechanism shares with people
    rather than one it sidesteps, and the mechanism would have found a real limit
    on somebody else's data at the first attempt. Note the comparison is
    indicative only — the human numbers are for conditions whose orderings are
    not in this set, so this is a claim about the family, not a paired contrast.

P2  THE RANKING IS BIMODAL HERE, with a mean largest gap above 0.20 — far above
    note 058's 0.059 for real language, and below the designed task's 0.424.
    REFUTED IF the mean gap is under 0.10, which would put these trials on the
    slope side with natural language and would mean `g33-04`'s derived bound is
    validated only on worlds this project designed. That is the outcome that
    would matter most, because it would make the per-surface bound —
    `g33-02`'s only working answer to a hub — unsafe on anything real.

P3  THE DERIVED BOUND CUTS AT 1, on average, within 0.3. The true class has two
    surfaces so exactly one partner is right, and the rule is not told that.
    REFUTED IF the mean cut is far from 1, which would say the cliff is in the
    wrong place even where one exists.

P4  THE `freq369` CONDITIONS SCORE WORSE THAN `orig_*`. Those deliberately show
    some pairs 3 times, some 6 and some 9, and `g32-02` measured frequency skew
    as the failure mode of raw counting — so `count` should suffer there and
    `conditional` should not.
    REFUTED IF `count` holds up under varied frequency, which would narrow
    `g32-02`'s finding to streams where the skew is much larger than an
    experimenter would use.

SCORECARD
---------
  P1  HELD. The derived bound recovers **26 of 29** conditions exactly, mean
            0.9569. Where the dataset table records a human score at all it is
            0.18 to 0.56. **Indicative only** — those human numbers belong to
            conditions whose orderings are not in this set — but the gap is not
            subtle, and it is what an exact counter with perfect memory should do
            to participants who have neither.

  P2  HELD, AND IT IS THE ANSWER `g33-04` WAS WAITING ON. The mean largest gap is
            about **0.5** across the 26 solvable conditions — the same order as
            the designed families task's 0.424 and **an order of magnitude above
            note 058's 0.059 for real language.** So the ranking IS bimodal on
            stimuli this project did not write, and the derived bound is not
            validated only on worlds we built.

            **The caveat that survives**: these are laboratory experiments
            designed to be learnable from co-occurrence. Between them and natural
            language there is a gap this run does not cross, and note 058 is
            still the only measurement on the far side of it.

  P3  REFUTED, AND THE REFUTATION IS A GOOD OUTCOME. The mean cut was predicted
            within 0.3 of 1, since a word has exactly one object. It is about
            **1.34**, because every `filt3E`–`filt9E` condition cuts at **2.0**.

            Cutting at 2 is CORRECT there: those conditions score **1.0000** with
            the derived bound against **0.8333** at a fixed bound of 1. The
            second candidate enters the ranking and is then removed by mutuality,
            while a true partner whose score is asymmetric — the same asymmetry
            `g33-04` found on chains — gets in where a cut of 1 excluded it. **So
            the rule beat the count it was predicted to match, and the prediction
            was measuring the wrong thing.**

  P4  REFUTED, AND IT NARROWS `g32-02` USEFULLY. Raw counting was predicted to
            suffer where pairs appear 3, 6 and 9 times. It does not: every
            `freq369` condition scores 1.0000 under `count` except `39mx` at
            0.9444, which is what `conditional` scores too.

            `g32-02`'s skew finding stands at the skew it was measured under —
            `zipf` 2.0, where the commonest concept took 4,992 occasions and the
            rarest zero. **A 3:9 ratio is not that**, and P4's own refutation
            clause said exactly this: the finding is about skew far larger than an
            experimenter would use.


THE THREE FAILURES ARE THE MOST VALUABLE ROWS
-----------------------------------------------
`filt0E_3L`, `filt0E_6L` and `filt0E_9L` score **0.5833 on every arm**, with a
largest gap of **exactly 0.000**, at 18, 36 and 54 trials.

The flat ranking is not noise. In that condition **pair 1 is only ever presented
alongside pair 7** — every trial containing one contains the other and nothing
else. So word-1 co-occurs with object-1 and with object-7 on precisely the same
trials, and `conditional` gives both exactly **1.0**. They do not nearly tie;
they tie identically, forever.

**More data cannot help, and the run shows that rather than arguing it**: 3L, 6L
and 9L triple the trials and return the same 0.5833. The information is ABSENT,
not scarce.

**So this is a structural limit found on somebody else's data at the first
attempt**, and it is not a defect in the statistic — no function of co-occurrence
counts can separate two things that co-occur identically.

> **CORRECTED, an hour after this section was first written.** It said the
> missing ingredient was *mutual exclusivity* — one word names one object, so
> committing word-7 to object-7 should remove object-7 from word-1's candidates.
> **That is wrong, and checking it took one probe.**
>
> The four surfaces `{word1, obj1, word7, obj7}` are a **closed, fully symmetric
> clique**: every pairwise `conditional` inside it is exactly 1.000, and nothing
> outside it ever appears with any of them. There are two assignments consistent
> with every observation ever made — `w1→o1, w7→o7` and `w1→o7, w7→o1` — and a
> one-word-one-object constraint **keeps both**. It halves the hypothesis space
> from two to two.
>
> **Nothing recovers this.** Not this mechanism, not mutual exclusivity, not a
> perfect Bayesian learner: the information distinguishing the two assignments
> was never presented. The condition is a designed control that proves
> co-occurrence has a ceiling, and the humans who score near chance on this
> family are up against the same wall rather than a different one.
>
> **So it does not motivate building anything**, which is the part that matters:
> the first version of this paragraph made it read as a to-do list.

**And the ladder is the experimenters', not ours.** `filt0E` withholds
disambiguating exposure and the mechanism scores 0.5833; `filt3E` through
`filt9E` supply it and the mechanism scores 1.0000. It fails exactly where the
information was withheld and succeeds exactly where it was given, which is better
evidence that it is doing the intended thing than any of the 26 perfect rows.


WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE
--------------------------
**No human comparison.** Not one condition here has a published accuracy — see
`tools/fetch_kachergis.py`. Calling this an external benchmark would be wrong; it
is external stimuli with self-contained ground truth.

**No join, no federation, no containers.** This is the statistic and the bound.

**Laboratory stimuli are not natural data.** P2's answer holds for designed
experiments and note 058's slope still stands for real language; nothing here
bridges those two.
